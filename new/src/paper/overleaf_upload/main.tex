\title{Conformal Perceptual Shielding for Adaptive Bitrate Streaming}

\author[a]{Saeed Zarbi}
\author[a]{Leili Farzinvash\corref{c1}}
\ead{l.farzinvash@tabrizu.ac.ir}
\author[a]{Pedram Salehpour}
\cortext[c1]{Corresponding author.}

\address[a]{Department of Computer Engineering, Faculty of Electrical and Computer Engineering, University of Tabriz, Tabriz, Iran}

\begin{abstract}
Client-side adaptive bitrate (ABR) controllers decide chunk by chunk. A
single request can stall playback when throughput drops, or waste bandwidth
when the top rungs of the bitrate ladder are perceptually saturated. Standard
runtime repairs address neither problem: they ignore perceptual saturation and
offer no coverage guarantee. We propose
the Certified Perceptual Shield (CPS), a model-agnostic projection that
wraps heuristic, model-based, or learned ABR policies. First, knee-based
bandwidth banking lowers even a feasible proposal to the smallest rung whose
Video Multimethod Assessment Fusion (VMAF) score lies within a perceptual
budget of the proposal. Second, an online split-conformal lower bound on
throughput provides distribution-free coverage under residual exchangeability.
Third, a feasibility check prefers rungs whose download time under that
bound fits the buffer margin. If none does, or if the buffer is below a
critical level, CPS still emits the lowest rung. The stall statement is
then a per-chunk probabilistic certificate only when the executed rung
meets the test and the buffer is at least \(0.1\,\mathrm{s}\), not a
zero-stall promise. We evaluate CPS
on \(\CPSepisodes\) paired synthetic \mbox{5G} episodes over twelve
titles with diverse rate--quality ladders. Compared with a safety-only
shield, banking reduces delivered bitrate by 3.8--4.5\% and rebuffering by
8.9--9.3\% for four deterministic controllers. Mean VMAF stays within one
point (equivalence test). Empirical coverage is \(\CPSGreedyCov\) against a \(\CPScovTarget\) target, and the first \(20\) of \(48\) chunks in each episode use a fixed fallback scale.
As a supporting mechanism study, risk-aware predictive banking (CPS-P) reaches 14.6\% bitrate and 12.6\% rebuffering savings versus safety at the default budget; a larger static \(\varepsilon{=}2\) cuts rebuffering further. On real fixed and mobile broadband traces,
banking gains fall to about 0.9\%. A feasibility projection removes about
98.7\% of stall time versus unshielded greedy, which already stalls for most of those sessions. Banking therefore
pays off where ladders saturate. The broadband stall cut is a feasibility effect, not evidence that the conformal bound transfers.
\end{abstract}

% =========================================
\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Client-side adaptive bitrate (ABR) policies target average quality of experience (QoE). Several controller families pursue this target.
Buffer-based adaptation (BBA)~\cite{huang2014buffer} and BOLA~\cite{spiteri2018bola} use buffer or Lyapunov rules.
RobustMPC~\cite{yin2015control} plans over a short throughput horizon.
Bitrate-greedy selection always requests the top rung, and proximal policy optimization (PPO)~\cite{mao2017neural,schulman2017proximal} learns a policy from traces.
Session averages, however, can obscure transient risks associated with individual chunks.
A single segment can still stall under volatile sub-6\,GHz and mmWave \mbox{5G} throughput~\cite{narayanan2021variegated,cisco2023,seufert2015survey}. A comfortable buffer creates the opposite problem: wasted bandwidth.
Commercial rate ladders often saturate in Video Multimethod Assessment Fusion (VMAF)~\cite{li2016vmaf}: further bitrate increases at the top often provide only marginal perceptual gains.
Requesting those rungs when the buffer is healthy consumes bandwidth that could cushion the next dip.

A runtime shield sits between the base policy and the player~\cite{alshiekh2018shielding,konighofer2025shields,gu2024safereview}. It checks each proposed representation before download and replaces an unsafe proposal.
The standard repair decrements the representation index until a buffer-feasibility test passes.
That repair is content-blind, because it ignores VMAF saturation. Prior shields often apply a fixed throughput scale without a stated coverage guarantee. CPS uses the same kind of fallback scale only during a short warm-up (\(W_{\mathrm{calib}}{=}20\) chunks), then switches to an online split-conformal lower bound with finite-sample coverage.
Deployed ladders carry exploitable structure---rate--quality saturation near the top.
That structure supports perceptually negligible bitrate reductions while freeing additional buffer capacity.

We introduce a \emph{Certified Perceptual Shield} (CPS) that exploits this structure while stating a formal coverage guarantee:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{VMAF-knee bandwidth banking.} Even a feasible action is lowered to the smallest rung whose VMAF lies within a budget \(\varepsilon\), in VMAF points, of the proposal. The downshift is perceptually bounded, and reclaimed bytes bank as buffer for later chunks.
  \item \textbf{Conformal throughput lower bound.} Download-time tests use an online split-conformal lower bound on throughput. Under residual exchangeability, the bound covers the next chunk with probability at least \(1{-}\alpha\), where \(\alpha\) is the tolerated miss rate. Calibration uses recent residual ratios~\cite{vovk2005algorithmic,angelopoulos2023gentle}.
  \item \textbf{Certified feasibility.} After banking, CPS prefers a rung whose download time under the lower bound fits the buffer margin. If no such rung exists, or if \(B_k\le B_{\mathrm{crit}}\), it still emits the lowest rung. When the executed rung meets the test and \(B_k\ge 0.1\,\mathrm{s}\), the result is a per-chunk probabilistic stall certificate at level \(1{-}\alpha\), rather than a deterministic no-stall guarantee.
\end{enumerate}
On a monotone ladder, VMAF never decreases with the rung index. A shield that only ranks rungs by VMAF then picks the highest feasible rung, so a perceptual-loss budget cannot change its choice.
The same projection attaches to bitrate-greedy, BBA, and PPO controllers alike.

The main contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Certified Perceptual Shield.} A model-agnostic runtime projection that combines VMAF-knee bandwidth banking, a conformal throughput lower bound with finite-sample coverage under residual exchangeability, and certified buffer feasibility.

  \item \textbf{Isolated banking evaluation.} Paired \textsc{Raw}, \textsc{Safety}, and \textsc{Certified} rollouts on greedy, BBA, BOLA, RobustMPC, and PPO hosts. We report Wilcoxon tests on bitrate and rebuffering, and two one-sided tests (TOST) of VMAF within \(\pm\varepsilon\)~\cite{lakens2017equivalence}.

  \item \textbf{Optional predictive banking (supporting).} A buffer- and look-ahead-conditioned perceptual budget (CPS-P) that widens to \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{risk}}\) under risk. Section~\ref{sec:eval:improved} compares it with static \(\varepsilon\) sweeps; a larger static budget can cut rebuffering further.

  \item \textbf{Regime characterization.} Banking gains concentrate where ladders saturate at the top and hosts request those rungs (primary \mbox{5G} suite). Real-trace broadband stress is dominated by safety projection.

  \item \textbf{Policy--shield co-design (supporting).} An optional single-seed PPO study with CPS in the training loop. We report the QoE weight \(w^{\star}\) at which this co-trained policy crosses shield-at-eval-only operation, but we do not treat the arm as a headline claim.
\end{itemize}

To separate banking from safety, we run three paired arms on identical episode seeds: \textsc{Raw} (unshielded), \textsc{Safety} (conformal feasibility only), and \textsc{Certified} (feasibility plus banking).
The banking effect is the \textsc{Certified} minus \textsc{Safety} contrast. The safety effect compares \textsc{Safety} or \textsc{Certified} against \textsc{Raw}.
We run \(\CPSepisodes\) paired \mbox{5G} episodes over twelve titles, with a perceptual budget \(\varepsilon{=}\CPSepsilon\) and a tolerated miss rate \(\alpha{=}\CPSalpha\). Certified banking reduces mean bitrate and rebuffering on greedy, BBA, BOLA, and RobustMPC (\tabref{tab:cps:full}).
For greedy, banking reduces bitrate by 4.5\% and rebuffering by 9.3\%.
Mean VMAF stays within \(\pm\varepsilon\) by TOST, and empirical coverage is \(\CPSGreedyCov\) against a \(\CPScovTarget\) target.
Optional CPS-P and static \(\varepsilon\) sweeps appear in Section~\ref{sec:eval:improved}.
On real fixed and mobile broadband traces from the FCC Measuring Broadband America program and the Norway/Nornet logs~\cite{fcc2015mba,kvalbein2014}, banking gains narrow to about 0.9\% bitrate reduction (certified vs.\ safety).
Conformal feasibility still reduces stalls by about 98.7\% vs.\ raw greedy on those traces. That host already stalls for most of the session, so the cut is a feasibility effect.
These results indicate where perceptual banking is effective.
Optional PPO co-training with CPS remains a supporting, single-seed study.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

Three areas bear on client-side representation selection: model-based ABR, learned ABR, and constrained control with runtime shielding.
Our focus is the control policy and its runtime action projection; throughput prediction lies outside this scope.
Predictors such as CS2P~\cite{sun2016cs2p} play a complementary role: they feed inputs to model-based or learned controllers~\cite{yin2015control,mao2017neural}.
ABR surveys~\cite{bentaleb2018survey,seufert2015survey,peroni2025survey} and \mbox{5G/mmWave} measurements~\cite{narayanan2021variegated,fezeu2024midband} agree on one empirical lesson: control must remain robust when throughput fluctuates sharply.

\subsection{Heuristic and model-based ABR}

MPEG Dynamic Adaptive Streaming over HTTP (DASH) separates packaging from transport~\cite{sodagar2011mpeg}.
Clients execute local adaptation policies based on ladder metadata and observed download conditions.
Buffer-threshold heuristics such as BBA~\cite{huang2014buffer} remain simple and interpretable.
BOLA~\cite{spiteri2018bola}, by contrast, derived its decisions from a Lyapunov-based utility formulation.
RobustMPC~\cite{yin2015control} planned ahead.
It selected representations by solving a short-horizon model predictive control (MPC) problem under estimated future throughput.
The payoff is interpretable thresholds and horizons.
The cost is fragility. These methods depend on manual calibration and on throughput predictions that vary across networks and devices~\cite{narayanan2021variegated,cisco2023}.
Moreover, VMAF scores saturate on the upper rungs of a ladder, so those rungs add little perceptual quality~\cite{li2016vmaf}. Conventional feasibility rules rarely exploit this saturation to reserve throughput headroom in advance.
Other related efforts target different stages of the delivery pipeline.
SODA~\cite{chen2024soda} pursued smoothness-aware online control. Edge assistance~\cite{mehrabi2019edge} and Oboe-style automatic tuning~\cite{akhtar2018oboe} addressed other parts of the pipeline.
Recent client-side designs also treat throughput uncertainty explicitly rather than relying on a single point forecast.
Conformal-ABR~\cite{zhong2026conformalabr} wraps a throughput predictor with split-conformal intervals. It selects a representation under an explicit stall-risk budget. This yields distribution-free control of prediction error when the traces are exchangeable.
SafeSABR~\cite{liu2025safesabr} targets volatile satellite broadband with risk-calibrated reinforcement learning. A runtime safety auditor then checks each policy proposal against conservative capacity bounds before download.
Kairos~\cite{zhong2025kairos} instead strengthens MPC-based ABR with streaming-aware prediction and buffer-conditioned uncertainty, improving robustness without a conformal certificate.
These lines of work address complementary failure modes: risk-aware control, runtime auditing, and robust planning.
None of them combines all three of our ingredients. These are a model-agnostic post-shield that overwrites an unsafe proposal, a stated conformal throughput lower bound, and VMAF-knee bandwidth banking on saturated ladders.
CPS fills that gap by separating an optional performance policy from a deterministic projection that enforces both certified feasibility and perceptual banking at every chunk.

\subsection{Deep reinforcement learning for ABR}

Pensieve~\cite{mao2017neural} established the template: a trace-driven DRL formulation for ABR.
D-DASH~\cite{gadaleta2017ddash} and more recent PPO-based controllers~\cite{naresh2023ppoabr} followed this line.
Their neural policies combine throughput histories, buffer state, and manifest features~\cite{sutton2018reinforcement}.
Comyco~\cite{huang2019comyco} trained by imitation against a VMAF-based QoE objective and showed that perceptual signals helped inside the learned policy.
Loki~\cite{zhang2021loki} combined learned features with a rule-based model and reduced long-tail failures in production.
Its rule module limits extreme decisions, but it provides no formal feasibility certificate.
Fugu~\cite{yan2019learning} deployed learned control in live networks. It exposed the distribution shift between simulation and deployment. That shift motivates runtime safeguards beyond expected-return training.
Taken together, these systems motivate constrained learning and decision-time enforcement.
An expected-return objective alone encodes no explicit per-episode stall or smoothness budget~\cite{hu2023corel,gu2024safereview}.
Explicit constraints do not fully close this gap.
A controller trained under a constrained Markov decision process (CMDP) retains guarantees only over the sampled trajectory distribution.
An unseen blockage pattern can still induce an unsafe proposal at a single chunk boundary.
This gap argues for splitting the stack into a learned performance layer and a runtime enforcement layer, because additional training cannot substitute for runtime validation.

\subsection{Safe/constrained RL, shields, and the content-blindness gap}

CMDPs and Lagrangian primal--dual methods optimize expected return under explicit constraints~\cite{altman1999constrained,paternain2019dual,achiam2017constrained}.
Safe-RL surveys catalogued related families: shielding, Lyapunov, and risk-sensitive schemes~\cite{gu2024safereview,chow2018lyapunov}.
COREL~\cite{hu2023corel} is closest to our training formulation.
It cast ABR over commercial \mbox{5G/mmWave} traces as a CMDP with a stall-time constraint.
The CMDP is solved by a primal--dual actor--critic.
That constraint, however, operated within training. It is not a standalone runtime projection that logs each intervention at decision time.
Runtime shielding takes the opposite approach: it repairs a proposed action before execution and can log each intervention~\cite{alshiekh2018shielding}.
Surveys split the family into \emph{pre-shielding}, which prunes the action set, and \emph{post-shielding}, which overwrites an unsafe proposal~\cite{konighofer2025shields}.
By that taxonomy, CPS is a post-shield.
Its safety set is drawn by a conformal feasibility test rather than a temporal-logic model.

This distinction shapes our design: we separate an optional learned performance layer from a deterministic runtime CPS.
Learned policies such as Lagrangian PPO can pursue expected stall and smoothness budgets across training rollouts~\cite{paternain2019dual,hu2023corel}.
They cannot certify per-chunk feasibility under conformal throughput bounds.
CPS targets exactly this gap.
Before every download it (i)~banks bandwidth on VMAF-saturated ladder rungs within a perceptual budget \(\varepsilon\), measured in VMAF points, and (ii)~projects onto actions feasible under a distribution-free throughput lower bound with coverage \(1{-}\alpha\), where \(\alpha\) is the tolerated miss rate~\cite{vovk2005algorithmic,angelopoulos2023gentle,alshiekh2018shielding}.
Its use of perceptual data also differs from Comyco, where VMAF enters only the learned objective~\cite{huang2019comyco}.
CPS reads the ladder at decision time to bank bandwidth proactively, rather than only repairing the representation index in an emergency.

\subsection{Streaming architecture and control loop}

We work in the standard Hypertext Transfer Protocol (HTTP) adaptive-streaming abstraction~\cite{yin2015control,mao2017neural,sodagar2011mpeg}.
A video origin or content delivery network (CDN) publishes \(L\) encoded representations.
We index them by \(\mathcal{B}=\{0,\ldots,L{-}1\}\); the Dynamic Adaptive Streaming over HTTP (DASH) or HTTP Live Streaming (HLS) manifest carries the matching per-chunk sizes and perceptual metadata.
At decision step \(k\), the client fetches chunk \(k\) while holding a playout buffer \(B_k\).
The effective trace throughput \(C^{\mathrm{tr}}_k\) is exogenous to the controller: the ABR client reacts to measured delivery rates, not to radio-scheduler internals.
This matches mobile access traces where blockage and mobility shift capacity abruptly~\cite{narayanan2021variegated,cisco2023}.

A base policy \(\pi\) (heuristic or learned) maps an augmented state \(s_k\)---buffer occupancy, throughput history, manifest lookahead, and the VMAF ladder (defined below)---to a proposed action \(a^{\mathrm{raw}}_k\in\mathcal{B}\).
Before chunk \(k\) is downloaded, the Certified Perceptual Shield \(\mathcal{S}\) applies the deterministic projection
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:shield-map}
    \big(a^{\mathrm{exec}}_k,\iota_k\big)=\mathcal{S}\!\left(s_k,a^{\mathrm{raw}}_k\right),\qquad a^{\mathrm{exec}}_k\in\mathcal{B},\ \ \iota_k\in\{0,1\},
\end{equation}
The projection returns the executed representation index \(a^{\mathrm{exec}}_k\) and an intervention bit \(\iota_k\), which indicates whether the projection altered the proposal.
Only \(a^{\mathrm{exec}}_k\) feeds the chunk download and buffer dynamics~\eqref{eq:buffer}; \figref{fig:arch} traces the \(a^{\mathrm{raw}}_k\!\rightarrow\!\mathcal{S}\!\rightarrow\!a^{\mathrm{exec}}_k\) path.

Decisions advance on the chunk clock, one step per playback duration \(T_{\mathrm{c}}\) (\(4\,\mathrm{s}\) in our emulator)~\cite{mao2017neural,yin2015control}.

The two layers serve distinct roles. Optional learned-host training (Section~\ref{sec:system:cmdp}) handles stall and smoothness budgets in expectation over rollouts~\cite{paternain2019dual,achiam2017constrained}. However, it does not guarantee per-step stall avoidance from throughput uncertainty alone. CPS supplies a separate layer: given a conformal lower bound \(\underline{C}_k\) (Section~\ref{sec:method}), it deterministically projects \(a^{\mathrm{raw}}_k\) to a feasible rung; marginal coverage of \(C^{\mathrm{act}}_k\ge\underline{C}_k\) is probabilistic at rate \(1{-}\alpha\)~\cite{alshiekh2018shielding,vovk2005algorithmic}.

\subsection{State, action, reward, and nonlinear perceptual mapping}

\emph{State \(s_k\).}
The observation holds buffer occupancy, past throughput samples, and the previous decision.
It also includes manifest lookahead: upcoming chunk sizes across the representation ladder, plus spatial complexity (SI) and temporal complexity (TI) indices~\cite{yin2015control,sodagar2011mpeg,mao2017neural} (higher SI/TI indicate richer spatial or temporal structure in the chunk).
The VMAF ladder \(\{V_k(j)\}_{j\in\mathcal{B}}\) completes the observation, where \(V_k(j)\) is the perceptual score for chunk \(k\) at representation \(j\)~\cite{li2016vmaf}.
This last component is essential: without \(\{V_k(j)\}\), neither a learned host nor CPS can detect the content-dependent ladder saturation that bandwidth banking exploits.

\emph{Nonlinear, content-dependent fidelity.}
Saturation arises because quality does not scale linearly with rate: for each chunk \(k\), the map \(j\mapsto V_k(j)\) is not affine in the discrete index \(j\).
This map also varies across chunks. Different group-of-pictures (GOP) and texture structures yield different marginal VMAF gains between adjacent rungs. Therefore, the bitrate-to-VMAF relationship is both nonlinear and content-dependent.
Consequently, adjacent rungs may differ by a few VMAF points or by more than ten. Quality often saturates at the upper rungs of the ladder---precisely the structure that CPS banking exploits~\cite{li2016vmaf}. Perceptually aware, JND-based per-title encoding aims to remove this saturation at authoring time~\cite{menon2022perceptual}. In contrast, CPS exploits any saturation that remains in a deployed ladder at decision time.

\emph{Action.}
\(a^{\mathrm{raw}}_k\in\mathcal{B}\) is the policy's proposed representation index for chunk \(k\); the environment executes only \(a^{\mathrm{exec}}_k\).

\emph{QoE surrogate.}
Keeping to RobustMPC/Pensieve reporting conventions~\cite{yin2015control,mao2017neural}, we log a scalar per-chunk surrogate with fidelity \(q_k \equiv V_k\!\left(a^{\mathrm{exec}}_k\right)\):
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:qoe-rep}
    \mathrm{QoE}^{\mathrm{(rep)}}_{k} = q_k - \mu\, \text{smooth}_k - \beta\, \tau^{\mathrm{stall}}_k,
\end{equation}
where \(\text{smooth}_k=\lvert q_k-q_{k-1}\rvert\) is the absolute VMAF change between adjacent chunks and \(\tau^{\mathrm{stall}}_k\) is the stall duration at step \(k\).
The first chunk takes a zero smoothness term.
Our shared emulator fixes \(\beta{=}4.3\) per stall second and \(\mu{=}1.0\) per absolute VMAF-point change. The symbol \(\alpha\) remains reserved for the conformal miscoverage throughout.
The rebuffering weight is a Pensieve-style penalty, rescaled onto the VMAF fidelity axis.
Session QoE sums over chunks: \(\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}_e}\mathrm{QoE}^{\mathrm{(rep)}}_{k}\) (Section~\ref{sec:eval}).
For a learned host, optional RL training substitutes a Lagrangian CMDP objective over rollouts (\ref{app:cmdp})~\cite{altman1999constrained,paternain2019dual}.

\subsection{Chunk-timescale dynamics}

\subsubsection{Throughput and latency}
Let \(C^{\mathrm{tr}}_k>0\) be the trace-derived effective throughput, in kb/s, for chunk \(k\).
Section~\ref{sec:method} defines a separate predictor \(\hat{C}_k\) (harmonic mean of recent realizations) that enters \(\underline{C}_k\). \(C^{\mathrm{act}}_k\) is the realized throughput of the executed download.
Every method sees the same throughput trace in paired evaluations.
The choice of \(a^{\mathrm{exec}}_k\) sets the manifest payload size \(\sigma_{k}(a^{\mathrm{exec}}_k)\) in bits.
Write \(\tilde{C}_k=\max\{C^{\mathrm{tr}}_k,\varepsilon_0\}\) for a small constant \(\varepsilon_0{>}0\) (the same numerical guard as in~\eqref{eq:dl}), and \(d_k(j)=\sigma_{k}(j)/(10^3\tilde{C}_k)\) for the nominal download duration of rung \(j\). The executed chunk uses \(d_k\equiv d_k(a^{\mathrm{exec}}_k)\)~\cite{yin2015control}. Feasibility timing under \(\underline{C}_k\) is defined in Section~\ref{sec:method}.

\subsubsection{Buffer and stall}
Let \(B_k\ge 0\) be the buffered playback time when the download of chunk \(k\) begins~\cite{yin2015control,mao2017neural}.
Rebuffering (stall) and the buffer update follow
\begin{equation}
    \tau^{\mathrm{stall}}_k=\max\{0,\, d_k-B_k\},
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:buffer}
    B_{k+1}=\max\{0,B_k-d_k\}+T_{\mathrm{c}}.
\end{equation}
Randomness across episodes has a single source: trace and initial-condition sampling~\cite{fcc2015mba,kvalbein2014,bentaleb2018survey}.
Given \((s_k,a^{\mathrm{exec}}_k,C^{\mathrm{tr}}_k)\), each transition is deterministic.

\subsection{Optional Lagrangian training for RL hosts}
\label{sec:system:cmdp}

When the base policy is learned, we train PPO with a Lagrangian-relaxed constrained MDP (CMDP) objective that penalizes rebuffering and smoothness alongside fidelity~\cite{altman1999constrained,paternain2019dual,achiam2017constrained}.
The dual variables adapt as training proceeds; \ref{app:cmdp} details the optional buffer-deviation and Lyapunov stabilizers~\cite{neely2010stochastic}.
None of this certifies per-chunk feasibility, which finite-sample PPO with clipped objectives cannot provide~\cite{schulman2017proximal}; CPS supplies this layer at deployment time.
Co-design wraps the training environment in CPS, so the actor--critic observes the projected action distribution, whereas shield-at-eval-only policies train without it.

\section{Method: Certified Perceptual Shield}
\label{sec:method}

CPS is a model-agnostic wrapper around any base policy that proposes \(a^{\mathrm{raw}}_k\in\mathcal{B}\). The projection is deterministic: for fixed inputs it always returns the same executed rung. What it certifies is probabilistic, because feasibility is tested against a conformal throughput lower bound that holds with probability at least \(1-\alpha\), where \(\alpha\) is the tolerated miss rate.
The rest of this section covers the conformal estimator, the banking and feasibility projection (Algorithm~\ref{alg:cps}), a predictive extension, optional PPO co-training, and the evaluation arms.

\subsection{Conformal throughput lower bound}
\label{sec:method:conformal}

The point predictor \(\hat{C}_k\) is the harmonic mean of the last \(\kappa\) observed throughputs, RobustMPC-style~\cite{yin2015control}.
After each step, the multiplicative residual \(r=C^{\mathrm{act}}/\hat{C}\) is appended to a rolling window of size \(W\).
Given \(n\) residuals, the split-conformal \(\alpha\)-quantile uses the finite-sample rank \(\lfloor\alpha(n{+}1)\rfloor\) (1-based); the \((n{+}1)\) term is the standard finite-sample correction. We avoid a linearly interpolated empirical quantile, because it under-covers on short episodes~\cite{vovk2005algorithmic,angelopoulos2023gentle}.
If that rank is undefined---or while \(n<W_{\mathrm{calib}}\) with \(W_{\mathrm{calib}}{=}20\)---we fall back to a fixed scale \(\rho_{\mathrm{fb}}{=}0.80\). That warm-up is not the finite-sample certificate.
The lower bound reads \(\underline{C}_k=\hat{C}_k\cdot q_{\alpha}\), clipped to stay at or below \(\hat{C}_k\).
Under residual exchangeability, the construction guarantees \(\mathbb{P}(C^{\mathrm{act}}\ge\underline{C}_k)\ge 1-\alpha\) once the residual window has length at least \(W_{\mathrm{calib}}\).
Given \(\underline{C}_k\), the certified download duration of rung \(j\) is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:dl}
    d_k(j)=\min\!\left\{\frac{\sigma_k(j)}{\underline{C}_k\cdot 10^{3}+\varepsilon_0},\ 60\right\}.
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Per-chunk stall certificate]
\label{prop:feas}
Fix chunk \(k\), executed index \(j\), buffer \(B_k\ge 0.1\,\mathrm{s}\), and conformal lower bound \(\underline{C}_k\) computed with miscoverage \(\alpha\).
If \(d_k(j)\le \max\{B_k-m,0.1\}\) via~\eqref{eq:dl} and the realized throughput satisfies \(C^{\mathrm{act}}_k\ge \underline{C}_k\), then \(\tau^{\mathrm{stall}}_k=0\).
The buffer floor is required: \(\max\{B_k-m,0.1\}\le B_k\) only when \(B_k\ge 0.1\).
Under exchangeability of conformal residuals, \(\mathbb{P}(C^{\mathrm{act}}_k\ge \underline{C}_k)\ge 1-\alpha\) at each step where the finite-sample bound is defined (\(n\ge W_{\mathrm{calib}}\)).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
When \(C^{\mathrm{act}}_k\ge \underline{C}_k\) and \(B_k\ge 0.1\,\mathrm{s}\), the realized download time is at most \(d_k(j)\le B_k\); hence~\eqref{eq:buffer} gives \(\tau^{\mathrm{stall}}_k=0\).
Coverage follows from standard split-conformal prediction with the \((n{+}1)\) rank correction~\cite{vovk2005algorithmic,angelopoulos2023gentle}.
\end{proof}

\emph{Scope of the guarantee.} Two parts of the certificate behave differently. The feasibility test is deterministic only when the executed rung satisfies \(d_k(j)\le\max\{B_k-m,0.1\}\) and \(B_k\ge 0.1\,\mathrm{s}\). If the realized throughput then clears \(\underline{C}_k\), that chunk cannot stall. Algorithm~\ref{alg:cps} can still return rung \(0\) when \(B_k\le B_{\mathrm{crit}}\), or when the decrement loop reaches \(0\) while \(d_k(0)\) fails the test. Those chunks are outside the certificate. Only the throughput bound is probabilistic. It provides marginal coverage over the exchangeable residual window, \(\mathbb{P}(C^{\mathrm{act}}_k\ge\underline{C}_k)\ge 1-\alpha\), averaged over residual draws rather than per chunk, and only after warm-up. It does not provide coverage conditional on the current buffer or throughput regime. Distribution-free split conformal cannot do so without stronger assumptions~\cite{angelopoulos2023gentle}. CPS therefore certifies feasibility on every chunk whose bound holds and whose rung passed the test; it does not promise a stall-free session.

Abrupt \mbox{5G/mmWave} LOS/NLOS and scheduling shifts can break residual exchangeability~\cite{narayanan2021variegated}. Our online defense is a sliding residual window of length \(W\). The recent ratios play two roles: they calibrate \(q_{\alpha}\), and they are the ratios immediately preceding the chunk we predict. The bound therefore tracks local nonstationarity, even though finite-sample exactness holds only on an exchangeable window. If drift is large, coverage decays. The decay is bounded by a total-variation term between calibration and test residuals~\cite{barber2023beyond}. A weighted-conformal variant that down-weights stale residuals is a natural extension, which we leave to future work.
Empirically, we treat reported coverage as the operational check of Proposition~\ref{prop:feas}. That rate averages over every chunk with a defined bound, including the \(W_{\mathrm{calib}}\) warm-up steps that use \(\rho_{\mathrm{fb}}\). On the primary \mbox{5G} pool, the certified arm meets \(1-\alpha\) (\(\CPSGreedyCov\) vs.\ \(\CPScovTarget\)). Real broadband traces and tighter \(\alpha\) can sit marginally below the target (Section~\ref{sec:eval:improved}). This is what the theory predicts: short calibration windows and nonstationary traces both erode finite-sample coverage.

\subsection{Banking and certified feasibility}
\label{sec:method:shield}

\emph{Safety set.} Indices that never exceed the proposal and fit the buffer margin \(m\) form
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:asafe}
    \mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{safe},k}(a)=\bigl\{j\le a:\ d_k(j)\le \max\{B_k-m,0.1\}\bigr\}.
\end{equation}
\emph{VMAF-knee banking.} The perceptual budget \(\varepsilon\) is a tolerance in VMAF points. Even when \(a\) is feasible, CPS steps down to the smallest rung whose VMAF lies within \(\varepsilon\) of the proposal, namely
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:knee}
    j^{\mathrm{knee}}_k(a)=\min\bigl\{j\le a:\ V_k(a)-V_k(j)\le\varepsilon\bigr\},
\end{equation}
The shield then projects \(j^{\mathrm{knee}}_k(a)\) onto \(\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{safe},k}(a)\); safety always takes precedence over \(\varepsilon\).
\emph{Remark.} Banking may run a lower rung than the proposal, and a lower rung can only shorten \(d_k(\cdot)\). Once~\eqref{eq:asafe} holds, safety projection therefore keeps the certificate intact.

Banking distinguishes CPS from prior VMAF-ranking shields, and the distinction is substantive. On any nondecreasing prefix of a ladder, a ranking shield provably banks nothing.

\begin{proposition}[Ranking shields collapse on monotone ladders]
\label{prop:collapse}
Fix chunk \(k\) and suppose the VMAF ladder is nondecreasing in the rung index, \(V_k(0)\le\cdots\le V_k(L-1)\), and that download time \(d_k(\cdot)\) is nondecreasing in the rung index. Let \(\mathcal{F}=\{j: d_k(j)\le B_k-m\}\) be the feasible set. Then any shield that returns \(\arg\max_{j\in\mathcal{F}} V_k(j)\) returns the highest feasible index \(\max\mathcal{F}\), independently of any perceptual-loss budget \(\varepsilon\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Because \(d_k\) is nondecreasing, \(\mathcal{F}\) is a prefix \(\{0,\dots,j_{\max}\}\) with \(j_{\max}=\max\mathcal{F}\). Since \(V_k\) is nondecreasing, \(\arg\max_{j\in\mathcal{F}} V_k(j)=j_{\max}\); the budget \(\varepsilon\) never enters the maximization.
\end{proof}
Ranking by retained VMAF is therefore equivalent to projection onto the highest feasible index, and it frees no bandwidth. CPS instead uses the proposal's quality and steps down to the smallest rung within \(\varepsilon\) of it~\eqref{eq:knee}. This mechanism lets CPS exploit rate--quality saturation: when top rungs carry near-identical VMAF, the knee lies well below \(j_{\max}\), and the freed bytes bank as buffer.

Algorithm~\ref{alg:cps} realizes~\eqref{eq:knee}--\eqref{eq:asafe}.
If \(B_k\le B_{\mathrm{crit}}\), CPS returns the lowest rung with no feasibility test. Otherwise it computes the knee index from~\eqref{eq:knee} under the effective budget \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{eff}}\). This budget equals \(\varepsilon\) unless predictive mode is on (Section~\ref{sec:method:predictive}). CPS then decrements the index until the certified duration~\eqref{eq:dl} respects the margin \(m\), or until the index is \(0\). If rung \(0\) still fails the test, the algorithm returns it anyway. The certificate in Proposition~\ref{prop:feas} does not cover that return, nor the \(B_{\mathrm{crit}}\) return.
The shield never upgrades a proposal.
Diagnostics log banked bits, VMAF surrendered, and whether realized throughput met \(\underline{C}_k\) (empirical coverage).

\begin{algorithm}[!t]
\footnotesize
\caption{Certified Perceptual Shield (compact)}
\label{alg:cps}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\STATE \textbf{Input:} \(a^{\mathrm{raw}}\), \(B_k\), \(\{\sigma_k(j),V_k(j)\}\), conformal estimator; parameters \((\varepsilon,m,B_{\mathrm{crit}})\)
\STATE \textbf{Output:} \((a^{\mathrm{exec}},\iota)\) with \(\iota=\mathbf{1}[a^{\mathrm{exec}}\neq a^{\mathrm{raw}}]\)
\STATE \(a\leftarrow\mathrm{clip}(a^{\mathrm{raw}})\); \(\underline{C}_k\leftarrow\) conformal lower bound; define \(d_k(\cdot)\) via~\eqref{eq:dl}
\IF{\(B_k\le B_{\mathrm{crit}}\)} \STATE \textbf{return} \((0,\mathbf{1}[a\neq0])\) \ENDIF
\STATE compute \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{eff}}\) (Section~\ref{sec:method:predictive}); \(j\leftarrow j^{\mathrm{knee}}_k(a)\) via~\eqref{eq:knee} with \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{eff}}\)
\WHILE{\(j>0\) and \(d_k(j)>\max\{B_k-m,0.1\}\)} \STATE \(j\leftarrow j-1\) \ENDWHILE
\STATE \(a^{\mathrm{exec}}\leftarrow\min\{a,j\}\); \textbf{return} \((a^{\mathrm{exec}},\mathbf{1}[a^{\mathrm{exec}}\neq a])\)
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\paragraph{Evaluation arms.}
\textsc{Raw}: we run \(a^{\mathrm{raw}}\) unshielded.
\textsc{Safety}: Algorithm~\ref{alg:cps} with banking switched off (\(j^{\mathrm{knee}}{=}a\)), leaving conformal feasibility alone.
\textsc{Certified}: the full CPS, banking plus feasibility.
Paired seeds keep the Wilcoxon and TOST tests valid across arms.

\subsection{Predictive and risk-aware perceptual budget}
\label{sec:method:predictive}

The predictive budget widens when slack is scarce: it banks lightly when buffer headroom is ample and more aggressively when risk arises. While the buffer stays comfortable, \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{eff}}{=}\varepsilon\) holds quality steady.
Two triggers widen the budget. The first is a drop of \(B_k\) below a risk threshold \(B_{\mathrm{risk}}\). The second is a short \(H\)-step look-ahead under a planning throughput floor (a low quantile of recent observations) that forecasts a dip below \(B_{\mathrm{risk}}\),
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:eps-eff}
    \varepsilon_{\mathrm{eff}}=\varepsilon+(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{risk}}-\varepsilon)\cdot f(B_k,\mathrm{lookahead}),
\end{equation}
where \(f\in[0,1]\) ramps between the two budgets and \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{risk}}>\varepsilon\) is the wider risk budget. The shield can therefore pre-bank more deeply, before a stall forces an emergency cut.
The predictive-banking experiments (CPS-P) use \(\varepsilon{=}\CPSepsilon\), \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{risk}}{=}4\), \(B_{\mathrm{risk}}{=}8\,\mathrm{s}\), and \(H{\ge}6\) at forecast quantile \(0.2\).

\subsection{Optional learned hosts and co-design}
\label{sec:method:codesign}

CPS does not require a learned policy.
When the host is an RL policy, we train PPO (optionally Lagrangian-shaped) with Stable-Baselines~3~\cite{raffin2021sb3,schulman2017proximal} on content-aware observations: throughput history, buffer, spatial and temporal complexity (SI/TI), and the VMAF ladder.
\emph{Co-design:} wrapping the training environment in CPS changes the action distribution observed by the actor--critic. The policy can then learn to propose bolder rungs that banking trims for safety.
To gauge the effect, we compare two policies under identical \mbox{5G} train/test provenance: one trained without the shield (shield-at-eval-only), one co-trained with CPS. Both face CPS-P at test time.

\subsection{Implementation notes}
\label{sec:method:impl}

At the primary \mbox{5G} operating point the defaults are \(\alpha{=}\CPSalpha\) (coverage target \(\CPScovTarget\)), residual window \(W{=}200\), warm-up \(W_{\mathrm{calib}}{=}20\), fallback \(\rho_{\mathrm{fb}}{=}0.80\), predictor \(\kappa{=}5\), margin \(m{=}0.5\,\mathrm{s}\), \(B_{\mathrm{crit}}{=}0.3\,\mathrm{s}\), and buffer cap \(12\,\mathrm{s}\).
Each episode has \(48\) chunks and the residual window resets at the episode boundary, so \(W{=}200\) never fills. The first \(W_{\mathrm{calib}}\) chunks of every episode use \(\rho_{\mathrm{fb}}\).
\tabref{tab:cps:hypers} collects the shield and predictive-banking defaults from the primary experiments. Empirical coverage is the fraction of steps with \(C^{\mathrm{act}}\ge\underline{C}_k\), counted over all chunks including warm-up.
Per decision, the projection runs in \(O(L{+}H)\) over the \(L\) ladder rungs and an \(H\)-step look-ahead. Conformal-quantile upkeep on the length-\(W\) residual window is \(O(1)\) amortized.
Across \(20{,}000\) rollout decisions on a commodity CPU (\(L{=}6\), full predictive shield), per-decision wall time is \(131\,\mu\mathrm{s}\) at the median and \(310\,\mu\mathrm{s}\) at the \(99\)th percentile. This is roughly \(10^{-5}\) of the \(4\,\mathrm{s}\) chunk budget. CPS therefore runs inline, and the control loop is unaffected.

\section{Experimental evaluation}
\label{sec:eval}

We organized the evaluation around four research questions:

\begin{itemize}
  \item[\textbf{RQ1}] \emph{Banking vs.\ safety.} Does VMAF-knee banking reduce bitrate and rebuffering relative to conformal safety-only projection, with VMAF inside \(\pm\varepsilon\)?
  \item[\textbf{RQ2}] \emph{Coverage.} Does empirical conformal coverage meet the finite-sample target \(1{-}\alpha\)?
  \item[\textbf{RQ3}] \emph{Budget extensions (supporting).} Relative to the default budget \(\varepsilon\), do CPS-P or a larger static \(\varepsilon\) change bitrate and rebuffering savings?
  \item[\textbf{RQ4}] \emph{Robustness.} Do the gains of RQ1--RQ3 persist on the non-monotone per-chunk ladder? Do they remain stable as \(\varepsilon\) and \(\alpha\) vary?
\end{itemize}
A supporting single-seed co-design study appears in Section~\ref{sec:eval:codesign}; no certified claim rests on it.

\subsection{Experimental setup and datasets}
\label{sec:eval:setup}

The testbed is a multi-video, chunk-timescale emulator (\(K_e{=}48\) chunks/episode, \(T_{\mathrm{c}}{=}4\,\mathrm{s}\), buffer cap \(12\,\mathrm{s}\) unless noted). It drives twelve publicly available reference titles with session-mean VMAF ladders~\cite{li2016vmaf}: five Blender open animations (Big Buck Bunny, Tears of Steel, Sintel, Elephants Dream, Cosmos Laundromat) and seven natural-motion Xiph DERF clips (Park Joy, Old Town Cross, Rush Hour, Into Tree, Sunflower, Kristen \& Sara, Ducks Take Off).
We selected them for ladder structure, not catalogue breadth: saturation-rich animation (Big Buck Bunny, Tears of Steel), steep natural motion (Park Joy, Ducks Take Off), wide-span DERF clips (Into Tree, Old Town Cross), and mixed held-out titles (Sintel, Cosmos Laundromat, Kristen \& Sara) for generalization checks (\tabref{tab:ladder:spacing}).
Nine titles appeared in PPO co-design training; three were held out entirely (Sintel, Cosmos Laundromat, Kristen \& Sara).
The negative broadband result (Section~\ref{sec:eval:improved}) showed a regime in which banking yields little benefit. CPS is a deterministic per-chunk projection fixed by the observed VMAF ladder and buffer state, so ladder shape---not semantic content---drove its effect. The twelve-title suite therefore spanned both saturated and steep/inverted session-mean ladders (\(\LadPctNonMonotone\%\) non-monotone).

\textbf{Primary \mbox{5G} suite.} The primary pool used synthetic \mbox{5G} traces with intermittent dips and outages, held out from the co-design training seeds (\(\CPSepisodes\) paired episodes). Public per-chunk \mbox{5G/mmWave} logs suitable for chunk-level ABR replay remain scarce, so we generate this pool and tune its dip-and-outage statistics to reported \mbox{5G/mmWave} measurements~\cite{narayanan2021variegated,fezeu2024midband}. The broadband suite below provides a real-trace counterpart.
Train and test trace pools stayed source-disjoint. Co-design policies trained on the training pool alone and faced held-out test seeds.

\textbf{Broadband suite.} The broadband suite used real-measured traces: the FCC Measuring-Broadband-America fixed-line logs~\cite{fcc2015mba} and the Norway/Nornet mobile-broadband mobility logs~\cite{kvalbein2014} (27 held-out test traces, source-disjoint from training). Both are widely used public datasets in ABR research. They provided a real-network counterpart to the primary \mbox{5G} suite and showed where banking offered limited headroom.

\textbf{Hosts.} Five host families covered the ABR design space: bitrate-greedy (always at the top rung), buffer-based ABR (BBA-like), BOLA (the Lyapunov buffer-based rule of the dash.js reference player~\cite{spiteri2018bola,spiteri2018dashjs}), robust model-predictive control (RobustMPC~\cite{yin2015control}), and PPO policies (content-aware ``proposed'' and content-blind Pensieve-style). RobustMPC was a five-segment planner over a throughput prediction discounted by recent worst-case error. CPS carried no host bias: one wrapper, applied at inference across all five families, tuned to none of them.

\textbf{Arms and pairing.} Every host rolled out \textsc{Raw}, \textsc{Safety}, and \textsc{Certified} on identical Python seeds---the same video--trace pairs.
Pairing fixes the random seed and the \((\text{video},\text{trace})\) assignment across arms, so metrics differ only because of shielding, not because of resampling.
Each paired contrast is the per-episode metric difference on the same \((\text{video},\text{trace})\) seed across arms (e.g., \textsc{Certified} minus \textsc{Safety} on episode \(i\)).
We reported mean delivered bitrate, mean rebuffering per episode, stall-free rate, mean VMAF, intervention rate, and empirical conformal coverage. Significance rests on paired Wilcoxon signed-rank tests (zero-method \texttt{zsplit}) and on two one-sided tests (TOST) for equivalence of VMAF within \(\pm\varepsilon{=}\CPSepsilon\)~\cite{lakens2017equivalence}. Defaults were \(\alpha{=}\CPSalpha\) and coverage target \(\CPScovTarget\).
We set \(\varepsilon{=}\CPSepsilon\) as both the knee budget and the TOST margin. One VMAF point is a small fraction of typical adjacent-rung and top-rung gaps on our ladders (\tabref{tab:ladder:spacing}) and is conservative relative to perceptually aware per-title encoding practice~\cite{menon2022perceptual}. It is an engineering design choice aligned with~\eqref{eq:knee}, not a preregistered clinical equivalence margin~\cite{lakens2017equivalence}. Because the knee rule and this TOST both use the session-mean ladder, a mean change inside \(\pm\varepsilon\) is partly by construction. The per-chunk ablation checks the true chunk VMAF instead (Section~\ref{sec:eval:perchunk}).
We report bitrate, rebuffering, and VMAF separately. The scalar in~\eqref{eq:qoe-rep} is the logging definition. Only the co-design sweep uses the linear score \(\mathrm{QoE}(w)\) defined in Section~\ref{sec:eval:codesign}.
The \(p\)-values we quoted for the primary arms were raw and two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank tests (zero-method \texttt{zsplit}). Identical \(p\)-values across hosts can arise when two contrasts share the same signed-rank statistic. The co-design values \(\CPScoPVM\) and \(\CPScoPReb\) equal \(1/20{,}000\), the floor of a \(20{,}000\)-draw sign-flip test. They are not signed-rank tail probabilities. The bootstrap intervals in Section~\ref{sec:eval:codesign} are the magnitude statement for that contrast. Since several paired contrasts ran at once, we checked that every reported bitrate and rebuffering effect on the primary arms survived Holm--Bonferroni correction at \(0.05\) over the reported family.
One caveat concerns content diversity. Each episode is one \((\text{video},\text{trace})\) pair: the video is drawn uniformly from the twelve titles and the trace from the held-out pool. Observations therefore clustered by title, and effective independent-content diversity was twelve, not \(\CPSepisodes\). We treat the reported \(p\)-values as within-pair evidence on that paired design, not as evidence from \(204\) independent titles.

\subsection{Model-agnostic banking on \mbox{5G} (RQ1--RQ2)}
\label{sec:eval:main}

\tabref{tab:cps:full} reports absolute metrics for all three arms---\textsc{Raw}, \textsc{Safety}, and \textsc{Certified}---on the primary \mbox{5G} test traces for five host families: greedy, BBA, BOLA, RobustMPC, and a Pensieve-style negative control.
\figref{fig:cps:overview} distills only the certified-vs.-safety banking contrast in bar form (not \textsc{Raw}); the table lists the underlying episode means and significance tests for every arm.
Banking behaved consistently across all four deterministic hosts on the twelve-title suite. Certified banking vs.\ safety saved 4.5\%, 4.1\%, 4.1\%, and 3.8\% bitrate on greedy, BBA, BOLA, and RobustMPC, respectively, and reduced rebuffering by 9.3\%, 8.9\%, 8.9\%, and 9.2\% (\tabref{tab:cps:full}; greedy \(p{=}\CPSGreedyPRebCs\)).
On steep, non-saturated ladders (\tabref{tab:ladder:spacing}) the absolute savings stay modest. This is what the mechanism predicts: banking pays off only where the ladder saturates. Every host nevertheless remains perceptually near-lossless.
Partitioning the greedy certified--safety contrast by session-mean top-rung VMAF gain (\tabref{tab:ladder:spacing}) made that dependence explicit. On titles with top gain at most \(2\) VMAF points (Big Buck Bunny, Sintel, Sunflower, Kristen \& Sara; \(n{=}60\) episodes) banking saved \(4.6\%\) bitrate and \(9.9\%\) rebuffering. On steep titles with top gain at least \(9\) (Park Joy, Ducks Take Off, Into Tree; \(n{=}51\)) the same contrast shrank to \(2.1\%\) bitrate and \(3.7\%\) rebuffering. The pooled greedy headline (4.5\% bitrate saved and 9.3\% rebuffering reduced) therefore mixed both regimes rather than reflecting a saturation-only subset.
Mean VMAF differences were perceptually negligible, at \(\CPSGreedyVMcs\), \(\CPSBbaVMcs\), \(\CPSBolaVMcs\), and \(\CPSMpcVMcs\) points across greedy, BBA, BOLA, and RobustMPC, with every value inside \(\pm\varepsilon\) by TOST.
The two shield layers split the work. On greedy, mean rebuffering per \(\sim\!192\,\mathrm{s}\) episode (\(48\) chunks) falls from \(\CPSGreedyRawReb\)\,s (\textsc{Raw}) to \(\CPSGreedySafReb\)\,s (\textsc{Safety}) and \(\CPSGreedyCertReb\)\,s (\textsc{Certified}). Against \textsc{Raw}, certified greedy reduced rebuffering by 55.5\% (BBA: 51.0\%). Conformal safety removed most of the stall time relative to \textsc{Raw}; banking added the certified--safety margin on top.
\figref{fig:cps:tradeoff} and \figref{fig:cps:cdf} show per-episode operating points and rebuffering CDFs for greedy and BBA.
Coverage followed a distinct pattern on the primary \mbox{5G} pool. Empirical coverage there was \(\CPSGreedyCov\) (greedy) and \(\CPSBbaCov\) (BBA) against a \(\CPScovTarget\) target, at or above the nominal level on this pool, consistent with the \((n{+}1)\) conformal calibration at these episode lengths (Section~\ref{sec:method:conformal}).
Because coverage depends on the throughput trace and the conformal calibrator rather than the base policy, it was identical (\(\CPSGreedyCov\)) across every host and shield variant on the \mbox{5G} test set (\figref{fig:cps:coverage}).
Proposition~\ref{prop:feas} is a per-chunk probabilistic certificate, not a per-episode stall-free one. On the greedy certified arm we attribute every second of residual rebuffering on the primary \mbox{5G} pool (\(\CPSepisodes\) paired episodes) to an exclusive cause. When the residual window is shorter than the conformal warm-up (\(W_{\mathrm{calib}}{=}20\)), \(\CPSGreedyStallColdPct\%\) of that stall time occurs; the bound then uses the fixed fallback scale and is not the finite-sample certificate. After warm-up, \(\CPSGreedyStallMissPct\%\) falls on chunks where realized throughput misses \(\underline{C}_k\). \(\CPSGreedyStallHeldPct\%\) falls on chunks where the bound holds, matching Proposition~\ref{prop:feas}. The \(B_k\le B_{\mathrm{crit}}\) emergency path contributes \(\CPSGreedyStallEmergPct\%\) of stall seconds on this suite.
Pensieve, the negative control, degraded to near-floor bitrates with near-zero stalls, so CPS rarely intervened. Banking could not rescue a policy that never requested saturated rungs.

The intervention rate under the greedy host is high by construction (\tabref{tab:cps:full}). It does not imply that the shield dominates an informed base policy. Greedy proposed the top rung every time, so nearly every banking downshift or feasibility repair appeared as an intervention. The rate gauged how far a host sat from the certified operating point, not how much of the outcome to attribute to the shield. This is why we ran the model-agnostic contrast across hosts spanning the spectrum.

The certified intervention rate falls as the host becomes more conservative: \(\CPSGreedyCertInt\%\) (greedy), \(\CPSBbaCertInt\%\) (BBA), \(\CPSBolaCertInt\%\) (BOLA), \(\CPSMpcCertInt\%\) (RobustMPC), and \(\CPSPenCertInt\%\) (Pensieve-style). The sharper the base policy, the lighter the shield's load. Coverage nevertheless holds at \(\CPSGreedyCov\) throughout. The certified metrics therefore depend increasingly on the base policy's own decisions, and the shield acts only where banking headroom remains. For RQ1 and RQ2 the message is simple: on every deterministic host, banking saves bitrate and cuts rebuffering with mean VMAF inside \(\pm\varepsilon\), and empirical coverage meets the \(\CPScovTarget\) target on the primary \mbox{5G} pool when warm-up chunks that use \(\rho_{\mathrm{fb}}\) are included in the average.

\subsection{Predictive banking and broadband boundary (RQ3)}
\label{sec:eval:improved}

With risk-aware \(\varepsilon(B)\) and dip forecasting enabled (CPS-P; budget widening up to \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{risk}}{=}4\) VMAF points), certified banking vs.\ safety reaches a 14.6\% bitrate reduction (greedy) and 12.5\% (BBA), and cuts rebuffering by 12.6\% and 13.0\%, respectively. These gains are relative to the default budget \(\varepsilon{=}\CPSepsilon\).
A static budget \(\varepsilon{=}2\) on the same greedy host already saves 14.4\% bitrate and 30.8\% rebuffering, at a mean VMAF change of \(-0.17\) (\tabref{tab:abl:eps}). CPS-P therefore matches that bitrate saving but does not improve on the static rebuffering cut. Its role is to keep \(\varepsilon\) at the base value while the buffer is comfortable, and to widen it only under risk. Mean VMAF changes under CPS-P are \(\CPSGreedyImpVMcs\) and \(\CPSBbaImpVMcs\) points.

The broadband suite marked the edge of these gains. On source-disjoint real-measured broadband traces (FCC MBA and Norway/Nornet~\cite{fcc2015mba,kvalbein2014}; 27 held-out traces, \(\CPSepisodes\) paired video--trace episodes), greedy certified--safety bitrate savings shrank to 0.9\% (\tabref{tab:cps:bb}). Top rungs were seldom jointly feasible and saturated, so banking had little remaining headroom.
Even so, safety vs.\ raw still reduces rebuffering by 98.7\%. Raw greedy already stalls for most of each broadband session (\tabref{tab:cps:bb}). The cut is what a feasibility projection does against that baseline. It is not evidence that the conformal bound, as opposed to any conservative rung cut, transfers across regimes.
On these 27 held-out traces at \(\alpha{=}\CPSalpha\), empirical conformal coverage was \(\CPSbbCov\) (below the \(\CPScovTarget\) target). The primary \mbox{5G} pool, over \(\CPSepisodes\) episodes, reached \(\CPSGreedyCov\) (\tabref{tab:cps:full}, \figref{fig:cps:coverage}).
The ``BB greedy'' group in \figref{fig:cps:overview} made the regime split explicit: banking savings were largest on the primary \mbox{5G} suite.
This split was by design: CPS banking paid off precisely when the ladder knee sat within reach, and broadband placed it out of reach.

\paragraph{Coverage under injected drift.}
To stress-test exchangeability without new traces, we replayed the primary \mbox{5G} pool with a mid-episode throughput shock (chunks \(28\)--\(37\): multiply trace throughput by \(0.3\), mimicking a sudden loss of line-of-sight (LOS) or a non-line-of-sight (NLOS) dip).
We report two related views. First, phase-mean coverage over valid chunks after a \(20\)-chunk conformal warm-up: \(0.928\) pre-shock (\(n{=}1{,}632\)), \(0.929\) during the shock (\(n{=}2{,}040\)), and \(0.944\) post-shock (\(n{=}2{,}040\)), all against a \(1{-}\alpha{=}0.90\) target (greedy certified arm, \(\CPSepisodes\) episodes).

The sliding residual window then keeps phase-mean coverage on target after calibration, consistent with Section~\ref{sec:method:conformal}.

\label{sec:eval:codesign}

We report two PPO settings separately. The primary host was a content-aware policy trained on source-disjoint broadband traces and evaluated, without further training, on \mbox{5G}---the model-agnostic deployment case (\tabref{tab:cps:ppo}). The co-design pair comprised two policies trained on matched \mbox{5G} traces---one shield-at-eval-only, one co-trained with CPS in the loop---compared on their certified arms (\tabref{tab:cps:codesign} and \figref{fig:cps:codesign}).
On the primary broadband-trained policy, certified banking vs.\ safety saved 35.3\% bitrate and reduced rebuffering by 79.3\% (\(p{=}\CPSPropPRebCs\)), at VMAF \(\Delta{=}\CPSPropVMcs\) (TOST within \(\pm\varepsilon\)).

\subsection{Per-chunk (non-monotone) ladder ablation (RQ4)}
\label{sec:eval:perchunk}

The headline runs applied the knee rule to each title's pooled session-mean ladder (\tabref{tab:ladder:spacing}): four of twelve ladders are fully nondecreasing, while the other eight are non-monotone only at mid-ladder (\(750{\to}1200\,\mathrm{kbps}\)); the top three rungs remain nondecreasing on every title. We therefore tested whether banking remained effective on the true per-chunk ladders, which were often non-monotone (\(\PCinvPct\%\) of the \(\PCnChunks\) chunks held at least one inverted adjacent pair; \figref{fig:ladder}).
The per-chunk ablation uses the four titles with frame-level VMAF logs (Big Buck Bunny, Tears of Steel, Sintel, CrowdRun), the same family as \figref{fig:ladder}. CrowdRun is not in the twelve-title session-mean roster of \tabref{tab:ladder:spacing}. The ablation reads \(\PCnChunks\) chunks from the aggregated per-chunk export; the figure aggregates \(\LadNChunks\) chunks from raw per-frame JSON, so the two counts differ.
To test this, we replay the deterministic banking stage on the per-chunk logs. We compare a shield that sees only the pooled ladder with one that sees the per-chunk ladder (\tabref{tab:perchunk}, \figref{fig:cps:perchunk}). Banking alone depended on VMAF; the feasibility projection ignored it and stayed identical in both cases, which isolated the effect of the pooling simplification.
Three findings held at the default \(\varepsilon{=}\PCeps\), spanning correctness, savings, and pooling error.
First, the knee rule remained well defined on inverted ladders and banked more, not less: mean saved bitrate was \(\PCbwPerchunk\%\) per-chunk against \(\PCbwPooled\%\) pooled, because a lower rung that inverted above the proposal satisfied the budget immediately---inversions widened banking opportunity rather than breaking the rule.
Second, a per-chunk shield held realized per-chunk perceptual cost inside budget by construction (mean \(\PCcostPerchunk\) VMAF points, never above \(\varepsilon\)), so the ablation validated chunk-wise enforcement when true ladders were available.
Third, pooling was not free. Judged against the true per-chunk VMAF, the pooled-ladder shield overran the budget on \(\PCviolPooled\%\) of chunks (mean cost \(\PCcostPooled\) points), which explains why session-mean evaluation could understate perceptual spend even when banking bytes looked similar.
Taken together, the pooled-ladder headline numbers were, if anything, conservative with respect to banking savings.
Per-chunk operation was sound: it eliminated residual budget violations at the cost of a per-chunk VMAF feed, and it aligned the content-diversity motivation with the pooled evaluation.

\subsection{Sensitivity to \(\varepsilon\) and \(\alpha\) (RQ4)}
\label{sec:eval:sensitivity}

The headline runs fixed the pair at \(\varepsilon{=}\CPSepsilon\) and \(\alpha{=}\CPSalpha\). \tabref{tab:abl:eps}, \tabref{tab:abl:alpha}, and \figref{fig:cps:ablation} vary one parameter at a time on the greedy host and the primary \mbox{5G} pool (\(\CPSepisodes\) paired episodes; twelve-title roster). The default cell (\(\varepsilon{=}\CPSepsilon\), \(\alpha{=}\CPSalpha\)) is the same evaluation as \tabref{tab:cps:full}; the other cells are a one-parameter sensitivity sweep under the same seeding protocol.
We first considered the perceptual budget. Sweeping \(\varepsilon\in\{\AblEpsLo,\dots,\AblEpsHi\}\) (at \(\alpha{=}\CPSalpha\)) traded banking for fidelity.
Bitrate saved vs.\ \textsc{Safety} climbed from 2.0\% to 24.6\%, while the mean VMAF change remained negligible (at most \(\AblVmMaxAbs\) points) and never left the matching \(\pm\varepsilon\) envelope---so wider budgets traded fidelity slack for banking headroom in a predictable way.
Empirical conformal coverage remained invariant with \(\varepsilon\). Coverage depended on the throughput trace and calibrator alone, not the banking budget, which showed that banking and the feasibility certificate operated independently by design.
We next considered miscoverage. Sweeping \(\alpha\in\{0.05,0.10,0.20\}\) (at \(\varepsilon{=}\CPSepsilon\)) on the same primary \mbox{5G} pool moved empirical coverage in step with the finite-sample target \(1{-}\alpha\): from \(\AblCovMax\) at target \(0.95\) through the default \(0.919\) at target \(0.90\) to \(\AblCovMin\) at target \(0.80\).
Tightening \(\alpha\) raises both coverage and the intervention rate; loosening it lowers both, as the \((n{+}1)\) construction predicts.
At \(\alpha{=}0.05\) the mean was \(0.945\), marginally below the \(0.95\) target at \(n{=}204\) (Section~\ref{sec:eval:improved}). The default operating point therefore lay on a smooth, well-behaved frontier rather than a narrowly tuned optimum, with headline savings stable once \(\varepsilon\) and \(\alpha\) were fixed.

\section{Discussion and limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

\textbf{Scope.}
CPS is an auditable, model-agnostic mechanism with two stages. First, it banks bandwidth on VMAF-saturated ladder knees inside a stated perceptual budget. Second, it enforces download feasibility under a conformal throughput lower bound with target coverage \(1{-}\alpha\), where \(\alpha\) is the tolerated miss rate.
We state the limits of the claim plainly. CPS does not claim dominance over every ABR controller. Its feasibility guarantee is probabilistic rather than deterministic: coverage holds at the \(1{-}\alpha\) level when calibration residuals are exchangeable, and we claim no pathwise worst-case bound.
The benefit also depends on the network regime and ladder shape. On the primary \mbox{5G} suite, conformal safety already cuts mean greedy rebuffering from \(\CPSGreedyRawReb\)\,s to \(\CPSGreedySafReb\)\,s per \(\sim\!192\,\mathrm{s}\) episode; certified banking trims it further to \(\CPSGreedyCertReb\)\,s. Banking gains accrue where session-mean ladders saturate at the top and the host requests those rungs, not because high rungs are jointly feasible on every chunk. Under broadband stress, the safety projection dominates and banking headroom decreases.

\textbf{Local ladder inversions.}
\figref{fig:ladder} shows that, chunk by chunk, a sizable share of adjacent rungs in titles such as Sintel and Big Buck Bunny run near-flat or inverted. A higher bitrate then scores no better, and sometimes worse, in VMAF.
CPS handles all of this without a special case. The headline shield uses each title's session-mean ladder (\tabref{tab:ladder:spacing}). Proposition~\ref{prop:collapse} therefore applies in full only on the four fully nondecreasing titles. On the eight titles with mid-ladder inversions, it still applies to the upper three rungs, where greedy banking concentrates. Even on a fully per-chunk, non-monotone ladder, both stages remain well defined. Each stage, moreover, handles inversions on its own terms. The feasibility projection~\eqref{eq:asafe} uses download time, never VMAF, so inversions leave safety untouched.
The knee rule~\eqref{eq:knee} picks the smallest rung within \(\varepsilon\) of the proposal's VMAF. When an inversion lets a lower rung score at least as high, that rung satisfies the budget on its own, and banking captures the extra headroom rather than failing on it.
Local inversions, then, can only widen the banking opportunity, and the algorithm handles them by construction. The per-chunk ablation in Section~\ref{sec:eval:perchunk} confirms this: banked bytes match, and realized per-chunk perceptual cost stays inside budget. Per-chunk ladders improve accuracy; they are not required for correctness.

\textbf{Threats to validity.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item The broadband suite replays real-measured FCC and Norway/Nornet traces~\cite{fcc2015mba,kvalbein2014}, whereas the \mbox{5G} suite is synthetic. In both cases the chunk-level emulator omits transport-stack dynamics and CDN caching~\cite{narayanan2021variegated}. We leave live-player validation to future work.
  \item Twelve publicly available titles and finite trace pools still cap catalogue diversity~\cite{kvalbein2014}; three titles are held out from PPO training but recur in CPS evaluation.
  \item QoE here is a VMAF-based surrogate. Subjective rankings of stall against fidelity may diverge from it~\cite{seufert2015survey}.
  \item Conformal coverage presumes exchangeable residuals (Section~\ref{sec:method:conformal}). Empirical coverage can sit below the nominal \(1{-}\alpha\) target on the 27 broadband traces (\(\CPSbbCov\) at \(\alpha{=}\CPSalpha\)), or leave only a tight miscoverage margin on the primary \mbox{5G} suite (\(0.945\) vs.\ \(0.95\) at \(\alpha{=}0.05\), \(n{=}\CPSepisodes\); \tabref{tab:abl:alpha}). Headline RQ2 reports the default \mbox{5G} operating point (\(\CPSGreedyCov\) vs.\ \(\CPScovTarget\)).
  \item Every arm---greedy, BBA, the PPO hosts---runs locally under the same finite-sample conformal correction (Section~\ref{sec:method:conformal}). Co-design magnitudes hinge on the checkpoint, so we report the best-validation model of each policy. The \(\Delta\) rows of \tabref{tab:cps:codesign} come from certified-arm means. The paired Wilcoxon test and bootstrap intervals use per-episode exports over the same \(\CPSepisodes\) pairs, with both co-design policies re-evaluated together. The co-design contrast uses a single training seed per policy. The \(95\%\) intervals therefore reflect evaluation variance only, not training-seed variance. Establishing training-seed variance for the crossover weight \(w^{\star}\) would require retraining many seeds, which we leave to future work. Co-design therefore stands as a supporting result, not a headline claim.
  \item Banking savings scale with ladder saturation (\tabref{tab:ladder:spacing}; see also the top-gain split in Section~\ref{sec:eval:main}). The twelve-title mix deliberately includes steep titles, so pooled effect sizes are conservative relative to a saturation-rich subset alone.
  \item The Pensieve-style no-op shows that host aggressiveness sets the banking opportunity. Results therefore do not transfer to arbitrary policies without the paired-arm protocol.
  \item The margin for the two one-sided tests (TOST) equals the configured banking budget \(\varepsilon\) (Section~\ref{sec:eval:setup}); we justify \(\varepsilon{=}\CPSepsilon\) relative to ladder spacing and perceptual encoding practice~\cite{menon2022perceptual}, but it remains a design parameter rather than a clinical equivalence margin~\cite{lakens2017equivalence}.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Future work.}
Several directions remain open: packet-level or field \mbox{5G} evaluation; applying CPS to RobustMPC and Comyco hosts; conditional value-at-risk (CVaR) and other tail-aware objectives~\cite{rockafellar2000}; telemetry-driven adaptation of \(\varepsilon\) and \(\alpha\); and preregistered non-inferiority studies with a fixed perceptual margin.
Real-player deployment is the highest-priority direction. Every result here comes from a chunk-level emulator that omits transport-stack dynamics. Inserting CPS into a production player such as \texttt{dash.js}~\cite{spiteri2018dashjs} therefore raises concrete challenges that the simulation never sees. Three challenges are central.
First, throughput must be estimated from noisy, bursty HTTP/TCP (or QUIC) samples. Their exchangeability---the assumption behind the conformal bound---can break under slow-start, congestion-control transients, and CDN re-routing. The calibration residuals then require periodic re-estimation, or a sliding, weighted-conformal variant that provably bounds coverage loss under drift~\cite{barber2023beyond}.
Second, the feasibility check must reconcile the player's own buffer-occupancy signal, which is segment-level and delayed, with the certificate's buffer-margin accounting.
Third, the per-decision budget tightens from our \(\sim\!100\,\mu\mathrm{s}\) micro\-benchmark into a soft real-time constraint running beside the player's ABR loop.
Showing that the finite-sample coverage guarantee withstands these real-world perturbations---and measuring any coverage slack they open up---is the natural next step.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We present the Certified Perceptual Shield (CPS), a conformal shielding layer for client-side ABR. CPS combines three parts: bandwidth banking at the VMAF knee, an online conformal throughput lower bound with finite-sample coverage under residual exchangeability, and a certified buffer-feasibility test.
We evaluate CPS on \(\CPSepisodes\) paired \mbox{5G} episodes from twelve titles with diverse ladders. Relative to the safety-only conformal projection, banking lowers delivered bitrate by 3.8--4.5\% and rebuffering by 8.9--9.3\%. This holds for every deterministic host, from greedy to RobustMPC. Mean VMAF stays inside the tolerance \(\pm\CPSepsilon\) VMAF points. Empirical coverage of the throughput lower bound is \(\CPSGreedyCov\), against a target of \(\CPScovTarget\), including warm-up chunks that use the fallback scale.
Optional CPS-P and static budget sweeps (Section~\ref{sec:eval:improved}) show that widening \(\varepsilon\) via look-ahead or a fixed larger value can deepen bitrate savings; static \(\varepsilon{=}2\) still cuts greedy rebuffering more than CPS-P at the default budget. Two regimes therefore appear. Banking supplies the savings while the ladder knee lies inside the observable operating range. Under broadband stress the knee moves beyond that range, and a feasibility projection---not a transferred conformal bound---accounts for the stall cut against raw greedy.
A supporting single-seed experiment co-trains PPO with CPS in the loop (Section~\ref{sec:eval:codesign}). It gains \(+\CPScoDVM\) VMAF at \(+\CPScoDReb\)\,s of rebuffering, with a QoE crossover weight \(w^{\star}{=}\CPScoWstar\).
CPS attaches to an existing ABR controller as a lightweight wrapper and needs no retraining, and it can also wrap a learned policy. Spare perceptual quality therefore becomes a budget that the client can measure and spend under a finite-sample guarantee.
