\title{Certified Perceptual Shielding for Adaptive Bitrate Streaming}

\author[uta]{Saeed Zarbi}
\author[uta]{Leili Farzinvash}
\author[uta]{Pedram Salehpour\corref{c1}}
\ead{psalehpour@tabrizu.ac.ir}
\cortext[c1]{Corresponding author}

\address[uta]{Department of Computer Engineering, Faculty of Electrical and Computer Engineering, University of Tabriz, Tabriz, Iran}

\begin{abstract}
Client-side adaptive bitrate (ABR) controllers---whether heuristic, model-based, or learned---can request representations that are either unsafe under volatile throughput or wasteful on rate--quality ladders that saturate in Video Multimethod Assessment Fusion (VMAF).
We introduce a \emph{Certified Perceptual Shield} (CPS): a model-agnostic runtime projection with three ingredients.
First, \emph{VMAF-knee bandwidth banking} downshifts a feasible proposal to the lowest rung whose VMAF lies within a perceptual budget \(\varepsilon\), converting ladder saturation into banked buffer without a material quality loss.
Second, an online \emph{conformal} lower bound on throughput provides distribution-free coverage \(1{-}\alpha\) under exchangeability, using a finite-sample \((n{+}1)\) rank correction.
Third, \emph{certified feasibility} never admits a download whose time under that bound exceeds the available buffer margin, yielding a per-chunk \emph{probabilistic} stall certificate---coverage \(1{-}\alpha\) under residual exchangeability, a strong distribution-free guarantee rather than a deterministic promise of zero stalls (Proposition~\ref{prop:feas}).
On \(\CPSepisodes\) paired synthetic \mbox{5G} episodes, CPS banking versus a conformal safety-only baseline reduces delivered bitrate by \(\CPSGreedyBWcs\%\) (greedy) and \(\CPSBbaBWcs\%\) (buffer-based) while cutting rebuffering by \(\CPSGreedyRebCs\%\) and \(\CPSBbaRebCs\%\), with mean VMAF differences inside \(\pm\CPSepsilon\) (TOST) and empirical coverage \(\CPSGreedyCov\) against target \(\CPScovTarget\).
A risk-aware / predictive extension deepens banking before forecast dips.
Co-training a PPO policy with the shield in the loop raises mean VMAF by \(\CPScoDVM\) points (paired-episode bootstrap 95\% CI \([\CPScoDVMlo,\CPScoDVMhi]\), positive in \(\CPScoDVMposPct\%\) of resamples) at \(+\CPScoDReb\)\,s of stall relative to shield-at-eval only (\(p{=}\CPScoPVM\) for VMAF, \(p{=}\CPScoPReb\) for rebuffering); the fidelity-favoring regime wins for stall weights below the QoE crossover \(w^{\star}{=}\CPScoWstar\) VMAF points per second.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Client-side adaptive bitrate (ABR) policies---buffer heuristics~\cite{huang2014buffer}, model-predictive control~\cite{yin2015control}, and deep reinforcement learning (DRL)~\cite{mao2017neural,schulman2017proximal}---optimize average quality of experience (QoE), yet individual chunk decisions can still stall under volatile sub-6\,GHz and \mbox{5G/mmWave} throughput~\cite{narayanan2021variegated,cisco2023,seufert2015survey}.
A complementary failure mode is waste: commercial rate ladders often \emph{saturate} in Video Multimethod Assessment Fusion (VMAF)~\cite{li2016vmaf}, so the top rungs buy little or no perceptual quality while consuming far more bytes.
Downloading those rungs when the buffer is comfortable leaves the client exposed to the next throughput dip.

Runtime \emph{shields} sit between a base policy and the player and project each proposal before download~\cite{alshiekh2018shielding}.
Legacy shields typically decrement the representation index until a buffer-feasibility test passes.
That repair is content-blind: it does not exploit VMAF saturation to bank bandwidth \emph{proactively}, and its throughput pessimism is usually a hand-tuned factor without a stated coverage guarantee.
Prior VMAF-aware ranking shields that maximize retained VMAF among feasible indices are, on monotone ladders, equivalent to highest-feasible-index projection; a perceptual-loss budget cannot change any decision when VMAF is nondecreasing in the rung index (Proposition~\ref{prop:collapse}).
The structure that \emph{does} exist in practice is rate--quality saturation at the top of the ladder---a lever for perceptually near-lossless downshifts that free buffer headroom.

We introduce a \emph{Certified Perceptual Shield} (CPS) with three ingredients that exploit that structure and admit a formal coverage statement:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{VMAF-knee bandwidth banking.} Even when the policy action is feasible, CPS lowers it to the smallest rung \(j\) whose VMAF lies within a budget \(\varepsilon\) of the proposal. The downshift is perceptually bounded by construction and converts saved bytes into banked buffer that protects future chunks.
  \item \textbf{Conformal throughput lower bound.} Download-time tests use an online split-conformal lower bound on throughput with target coverage \(1{-}\alpha\), calibrated from residual ratios with a finite-sample \((n{+}1)\) rank correction~\cite{vovk2005algorithmic,angelopoulos2023gentle}.
  \item \textbf{Certified feasibility.} After banking, CPS never admits a rung whose download time under the lower bound exceeds buffer minus a safety margin, yielding a per-chunk \emph{probabilistic} stall certificate---i.e., coverage \(1{-}\alpha\) under residual exchangeability, not a deterministic \(100\%\) no-stall guarantee (Proposition~\ref{prop:feas}).
\end{enumerate}
CPS is model-agnostic: the same wrapper applies to bitrate-greedy, buffer-based (BBA), and learned PPO controllers.
We evaluate three paired arms on identical episode seeds: \textsc{Raw} (unshielded), \textsc{Safety} (conformal feasibility only), and \textsc{Certified} (feasibility plus banking).
The banking contribution is isolated by the \textsc{Certified}--\textsc{Safety} contrast; the safety contribution by \textsc{Safety}/\textsc{Certified} versus \textsc{Raw}.

On \(\CPSepisodes\) paired synthetic \mbox{5G} episodes with \(\varepsilon{=}\CPSepsilon\) and \(\alpha{=}\CPSalpha\), certified banking versus safety-only reduces mean bitrate by \(\CPSGreedyBWcs\%\) under greedy and \(\CPSBbaBWcs\%\) under BBA, while reducing rebuffering by \(\CPSGreedyRebCs\%\) and \(\CPSBbaRebCs\%\) (paired Wilcoxon \(p{=}\CPSGreedyPRebCs\)).
Mean VMAF differences remain inside \(\pm\varepsilon\) by TOST equivalence.
Empirical conformal coverage is \(\CPSGreedyCov\) against target \(\CPScovTarget\).
A risk-aware / predictive extension that widens \(\varepsilon\) when the buffer (or a short look-ahead under a throughput floor) enters a danger zone deepens savings (greedy: \(\CPSGreedyImpBWcs\%\) bitrate, \(\CPSGreedyImpRebCs\%\) rebuffering).
On source-disjoint \emph{real-measured} broadband traces (FCC and Norway/Nornet), banking headroom shrinks (\(\CPSbbBWcs\%\) bitrate) while safety still cuts stalls sharply (\(\CPSbbRebSr\%\) vs.\ raw greedy), clarifying the operating regime where perceptual banking pays off.
Co-training PPO with CPS in the loop yields a bolder certified operating point: \(+\CPScoDVM\) mean VMAF and \(+\CPScoDReb\)\,s rebuffering versus shield-at-eval only, with QoE crossover \(w^{\star}{=}\CPScoWstar\) VMAF points per second of stall.

The main contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Certified Perceptual Shield.} A model-agnostic runtime projection combining VMAF-knee bandwidth banking, a conformal throughput lower bound with finite-sample coverage, and certified buffer feasibility (Section~\ref{sec:method}).

  \item \textbf{Isolated banking evaluation.} Paired \textsc{Raw}/\textsc{Safety}/\textsc{Certified} rollouts on greedy, BBA, and PPO hosts, with Wilcoxon tests on bitrate and rebuffering and TOST non-inferiority on VMAF within \(\pm\varepsilon\) (Section~\ref{sec:eval}).

  \item \textbf{Predictive / risk-aware banking.} A buffer- and look-ahead-conditioned perceptual budget that pre-banks before forecast dips, improving the bandwidth--stall trade-off under the same \(\varepsilon\) ceiling at comfortable buffers (Section~\ref{sec:method:predictive}).

  \item \textbf{Regime characterization.} We show that banking gains concentrate where top rungs are often feasible (synthetic \mbox{5G} moderate buffer), whereas real-trace broadband stress is dominated by safety projection (Section~\ref{sec:eval}).

  \item \textbf{Policy--shield co-design.} Training with CPS in the loop shifts the certified operating point toward higher fidelity; we report the QoE crossover weight \(w^{\star}\) that separates fidelity-favoring from stall-averse regimes (Section~\ref{sec:eval:codesign}).
\end{itemize}

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows.
Section~\ref{sec:related} reviews related work.
Section~\ref{sec:system} formalizes the streaming model.
Section~\ref{sec:method} presents CPS and optional co-training.
Section~\ref{sec:eval} reports the evaluation, Section~\ref{sec:limits} discusses limitations, and Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} concludes.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

We review three areas relevant to client-side representation selection: model-based ABR, learned ABR, and constrained control with runtime shielding.
Our focus is the control policy and its runtime action projection, not throughput prediction.
Predictors such as CS2P~\cite{sun2016cs2p} can provide inputs to model-based or learned controllers~\cite{yin2015control,mao2017neural}.
ABR surveys~\cite{bentaleb2018survey,seufert2015survey,peroni2025survey} and \mbox{5G/mmWave} measurements~\cite{narayanan2021variegated,fezeu2024midband} further motivate control mechanisms that remain effective under volatile throughput.

\subsection{Heuristic and model-based ABR}

MPEG Dynamic Adaptive Streaming over HTTP (DASH) decouples packaging from transport; clients run local policies informed by ladder metadata and download observations~\cite{sodagar2011mpeg}.
Buffer-threshold heuristics such as BBA~\cite{huang2014buffer} are simple and transparent, whereas BOLA~\cite{spiteri2018bola} derives its decisions from a Lyapunov-based utility formulation.
RobustMPC~\cite{yin2015control} selects representations by solving a short-horizon model predictive control (MPC) problem under estimated future throughput.
These approaches expose interpretable thresholds and horizons, but their performance can depend on manual calibration and prediction accuracy across networks and devices~\cite{narayanan2021variegated,cisco2023}.
Conventional feasibility rules also generally do not use VMAF saturation to bank bandwidth proactively~\cite{li2016vmaf}.
SODA~\cite{chen2024soda} addresses smoothness-aware online control, while edge assistance~\cite{mehrabi2019edge} and Oboe-style automatic tuning~\cite{akhtar2018oboe} modify complementary parts of the delivery system.

\subsection{Deep reinforcement learning for ABR}

Pensieve~\cite{mao2017neural} established a trace-driven DRL formulation for ABR.
Related methods include D-DASH~\cite{gadaleta2017ddash} and more recent PPO-based controllers~\cite{naresh2023ppoabr}.
Their neural policies combine throughput histories, buffer state, and manifest features~\cite{sutton2018reinforcement}.
Comyco~\cite{huang2019comyco} uses imitation learning with a VMAF-based QoE objective, demonstrating the value of perceptual information within the learned policy.
Loki~\cite{zhang2021loki} combines learned features with a rule-based model to reduce long-tail failures in production.
Fugu~\cite{yan2019learning} evaluates learned control in live networks and illustrates the importance of distribution shift between simulation and deployment.
These systems motivate complementary constrained-learning and decision-time mechanisms because an expected-return objective alone does not impose an explicit per-episode stall or smoothness budget~\cite{hu2023corel,gu2024safereview}.
Even when a learned controller is trained with explicit CMDP constraints, its guarantees and empirical performance are defined with respect to the sampled trajectory distribution.
A previously unseen blockage pattern can still induce an unsafe proposal at an individual chunk boundary.
This observation motivates a separation between a learned performance layer and a runtime enforcement layer; additional policy training is not a substitute for runtime validation.

\subsection{Safe/constrained RL, shields, and the content-blindness gap}

CMDPs and Lagrangian primal--dual methods provide a framework for optimizing expected return subject to explicit constraints~\cite{altman1999constrained,paternain2019dual,achiam2017constrained}.
Safe-RL surveys discuss related shield, Lyapunov, and risk-sensitive approaches~\cite{gu2024safereview,chow2018lyapunov}.
COREL~\cite{hu2023corel} is closest to our training formulation.
It models ABR over commercial \mbox{5G/mmWave} traces as a CMDP with a stall-time constraint and applies a primal--dual actor--critic method.
Its constraint mechanism is embedded in training rather than implemented as a separate runtime projection with intervention telemetry.
Runtime shielding instead repairs a proposed action before execution and can record when such repair occurs~\cite{alshiekh2018shielding}; recent surveys of shielding distinguish \emph{pre-shielding} (restricting the action set) from \emph{post-shielding} (overwriting an unsafe proposal)~\cite{konighofer2025shields}, and CPS is a post-shield whose safety set is defined by a conformal feasibility test rather than a temporal-logic model.

Our method separates an optional learned performance layer from a deterministic runtime CPS.
Learned policies such as Lagrangian PPO can optimize expected stall and smoothness budgets over training rollouts~\cite{paternain2019dual,hu2023corel}, but they do not certify per-chunk feasibility under conformal throughput bounds.
CPS addresses a different requirement: before every download it (i)~banks bandwidth on VMAF-saturated rungs within a perceptual budget \(\varepsilon\) and (ii)~projects onto actions that are feasible under a distribution-free throughput lower bound with coverage \(1{-}\alpha\)~\cite{vovk2005algorithmic,angelopoulos2023gentle,alshiekh2018shielding}.
Unlike Comyco, where VMAF enters only the learned objective~\cite{huang2019comyco}, CPS uses the ladder at decision time for \emph{proactive} banking, not only for emergency index repair.

\subsection{Streaming architecture and control loop}

We adopt the standard Hypertext Transfer Protocol (HTTP) adaptive-streaming abstraction~\cite{yin2015control,mao2017neural,sodagar2011mpeg}.
A video origin or content delivery network (CDN) exposes \(L\) encoded representations.
We denote their indices by \(\mathcal{B}=\{0,\ldots,L{-}1\}\); the DASH/HTTP Live Streaming (HLS) manifest provides the corresponding per-chunk sizes and perceptual metadata.
At decision step \(k\), the client requests chunk \(k\) while maintaining a playout buffer \(B_k\).
The effective trace throughput \(\hat{C}_k\) is exogenous to the controller.
On sub-6\,GHz and \mbox{5G/mmWave} access, this throughput can change abruptly because of blockage, LOS/NLOS transitions, scheduling, and mobility~\cite{narayanan2021variegated,cisco2023}.

A base policy \(\pi\) (heuristic or learned) maps an augmented state \(s_k\) to a proposed action \(a^{\mathrm{raw}}_k\in\mathcal{B}\).
Before chunk \(k\) is downloaded, the Certified Perceptual Shield \(\mathcal{S}\) applies the deterministic projection
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:shield-map}
    \big(a^{\mathrm{exec}}_k,\iota_k\big)=\mathcal{S}\!\left(s_k,a^{\mathrm{raw}}_k\right),\qquad a^{\mathrm{exec}}_k\in\mathcal{B},\ \ \iota_k\in\{0,1\},
\end{equation}
yielding the executed representation index \(a^{\mathrm{exec}}_k\) and an audit bit \(\iota_k\) that records whether the projection altered the proposal.
Only \(a^{\mathrm{exec}}_k\) enters the chunk download and buffer dynamics~\eqref{eq:buffer}; Figure~\ref{fig:arch} highlights the \(a^{\mathrm{raw}}_k\!\rightarrow\!\mathcal{S}\!\rightarrow\!a^{\mathrm{exec}}_k\) path.
Decisions evolve on the chunk clock with playback duration \(T_{\mathrm{c}}\) per step (e.g., \(4\,\mathrm{s}\) in our harness)~\cite{mao2017neural,yin2015control}.

Optional Lagrangian PPO training addresses stall and smoothness budgets in expectation over rollouts~\cite{paternain2019dual,achiam2017constrained}.
It does not provide a per-step feasibility guarantee under conformal throughput bounds.
CPS provides complementary decision-time banking and certified repair~\cite{alshiekh2018shielding,vovk2005algorithmic}.

\subsection{State, action, reward, and nonlinear perceptual mapping}

\emph{State \(s_k\).}
The observation includes buffer occupancy, past throughput samples, and the previous decision.
It also includes manifest lookahead: upcoming chunk sizes across the representation ladder and spatial/temporal complexity tags (SI/TI)~\cite{yin2015control,sodagar2011mpeg,mao2017neural}.
The state also includes the VMAF ladder \(\{V_k(j)\}_{j\in\mathcal{B}}\), where \(V_k(j)\) is the perceptual score for chunk \(k\) at representation \(j\)~\cite{li2016vmaf}.
Without \(\{V_k(j)\}\), neither a learned host nor CPS could exploit content-dependent ladder saturation for bandwidth banking.

\emph{Nonlinear, content-dependent fidelity.}
For each chunk \(k\), the map \(j\mapsto V_k(j)\) is not affine in the discrete index \(j\).
It also varies across chunks because different group-of-pictures (GOP) and texture structures produce different marginal VMAF gains between adjacent rungs.
The mapping from nominal bitrate to VMAF is therefore nonlinear and content dependent.
As a result, adjacent rungs can differ by a few VMAF points or by more than ten, and top rungs often saturate---the structure CPS banking exploits~\cite{li2016vmaf}. This saturation is precisely what perceptually-aware, JND-based per-title encoding seeks to remove at authoring time~\cite{menon2022perceptual}; CPS is complementary, exploiting whatever saturation remains in a deployed ladder at decision time.

\emph{Action.}
\(a^{\mathrm{raw}}_k\in\mathcal{B}\) is the policy's proposed representation index for chunk \(k\); the environment executes only \(a^{\mathrm{exec}}_k\).

\emph{QoE surrogate.}
Following RobustMPC/Pensieve reporting conventions~\cite{yin2015control,mao2017neural}, we log a scalar per-chunk surrogate with fidelity \(q_k \equiv V_k\!\left(a^{\mathrm{exec}}_k\right)\):
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:qoe-rep}
    \mathrm{QoE}^{\mathrm{(rep)}}_{k} = q_k - \mu\, \text{smooth}_k - \beta\, \tau^{\mathrm{stall}}_k,
\end{equation}
where \(\text{smooth}_k=\lvert q_k-q_{k-1}\rvert\) is the absolute VMAF change between adjacent chunks and \(\tau^{\mathrm{stall}}_k\) is the stall duration at step \(k\).
For the first chunk, we set the smoothness term to zero.
The shared evaluation harness fixes \(\beta{=}4.3\) per stall second and \(\mu{=}1.0\) per absolute VMAF-point change (the symbol \(\alpha\) is reserved throughout for the conformal miscoverage).
The rebuffering weight follows a Pensieve-style penalty rescaled to the VMAF fidelity axis.
Session QoE is \(\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}_e}\mathrm{QoE}^{\mathrm{(rep)}}_{k}\) (Section~\ref{sec:eval}).
Optional RL training uses a Lagrangian CMDP objective over rollouts (Appendix~\ref{app:cmdp})~\cite{altman1999constrained,paternain2019dual}.

\subsection{Ladder saturation}
\label{sec:system:ladder}

The evaluated system supplies one VMAF value per representation and video (pooled per-frame scores), so shield decisions use a session-mean ladder that does not vary with chunk index \(k\).
Figure~\ref{fig:ladder} and Table~\ref{tab:ladder:spacing} quantify spacing from per-chunk VMAF logs (\(\LadNChunks\) chunks across four titles).
Two properties motivate CPS banking: (\emph{i})~\(\LadPctPairsFlat\%\) of adjacent pairs differ by at most \(\LadFlatThresh\) VMAF point---a one-step downgrade is perceptually almost free; (\emph{ii})~top rungs often saturate, so the knee rule~\eqref{eq:knee} can bank bytes without a material quality loss.
On the monotone per-video ladder used in evaluation, highest-VMAF emergency ranking among feasible indices coincides with highest-feasible-index projection; CPS instead selects the \emph{smallest} within-budget rung, which activates when saturation makes downshifts near-lossless.
Chunk-level inversions (\(\LadPctPairsInverted\%\) of adjacent pairs) are documented in Figure~\ref{fig:ladder}; banking does not require them, but Section~\ref{sec:eval:perchunk} shows the knee rule remains sound and, if anything, banks more when the shield sees the raw per-chunk ladder.

\subsection{Chunk-timescale dynamics}

\subsubsection{Throughput and latency}
Let \(\hat{C}_k>0\) denote the trace-derived effective throughput, in kb/s, for chunk \(k\).
All methods receive the same throughput trace in paired evaluations.
Choosing \(a^{\mathrm{exec}}_k\) determines the manifest payload size \(\sigma_{k}(a^{\mathrm{exec}}_k)\) in bits.
With \(\tilde{C}_k=\max\{\hat{C}_k,\varepsilon_0\}\) for a small constant \(\varepsilon_0{>}0\) (the same numerical guard used in~\eqref{eq:dl}), the nominal download duration is \(d_k=\sigma_{k}(a^{\mathrm{exec}}_k)/(10^3\tilde{C}_k)\) seconds~\cite{yin2015control}.

\subsubsection{Buffer and stall}
Let \(B_k\ge 0\) denote the buffered playback time at the start of the download of chunk \(k\)~\cite{yin2015control,mao2017neural}.
Rebuffering (stall) and buffer update follow
\begin{equation}
    \tau^{\mathrm{stall}}_k=\max\{0,\, d_k-B_k\},
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:buffer}
    B_{k+1}=\max\{0,B_k-d_k\}+T_{\mathrm{c}}.
\end{equation}
Randomness across episodes arises from trace and initial-condition sampling~\cite{fcc2015mba,kvalbein2014,bentaleb2018survey}.
Conditional on \((s_k,a^{\mathrm{exec}}_k,\hat{C}_k)\), each transition is deterministic.

\subsection{Optional Lagrangian training for RL hosts}
\label{sec:system:cmdp}

When the base policy is learned, we optionally train PPO with a Lagrangian-relaxed constrained MDP (CMDP) objective that penalizes rebuffering and smoothness alongside fidelity~\cite{altman1999constrained,paternain2019dual,achiam2017constrained}.
Dual variables adapt during training; optional buffer-deviation and Lyapunov stabilizers are detailed in Appendix~\ref{app:cmdp}~\cite{neely2010stochastic}.
Finite-sample PPO with clipped objectives does not certify per-chunk feasibility~\cite{schulman2017proximal}; CPS supplies that layer at deployment time.
Co-design wraps the training environment with CPS so the actor--critic sees the projected action distribution; shield-at-eval-only policies train without the wrapper.

\subsection{Certified Perceptual Shield as a hard projection}
\label{sec:system:proj}

Let \(V_k(j)\) denote the VMAF of chunk \(k\) at rung \(j\), \(\sigma_k(j)\) its size in bits, and \(B_k\) the buffer in seconds.
CPS computes a conformal throughput lower bound \(\underline{C}_k\) (kb/s) and the implied download time
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:dl}
    d_k(j)=\min\!\left\{\frac{\sigma_k(j)}{\underline{C}_k\cdot 10^{3}+\varepsilon_0},\ 60\right\}.
\end{equation}
\emph{Safety set.} Indices that never exceed the proposal and fit the buffer margin \(m\) form
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:asafe}
    \mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{safe},k}(a)=\bigl\{j\le a:\ d_k(j)\le \max\{B_k-m,0.1\}\bigr\}.
\end{equation}
\emph{VMAF-knee banking.} Even when \(a\) is feasible, CPS selects the \emph{smallest} rung within a perceptual budget \(\varepsilon\) of the proposal,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:knee}
    j^{\mathrm{knee}}_k(a)=\min\bigl\{j\le a:\ V_k(a)-V_k(j)\le\varepsilon\bigr\},
\end{equation}
then projects \(j^{\mathrm{knee}}_k(a)\) onto \(\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{safe},k}(a)\) (safety wins over \(\varepsilon\)).
Unlike highest-VMAF emergency ranking---which coincides with highest-feasible-index on monotone ladders---banking exploits \emph{saturation}: when top rungs share nearly equal VMAF,~\eqref{eq:knee} frees bytes without a material perceptual loss and banks them as buffer.

\section{Method: Certified Perceptual Shield}
\label{sec:method}

CPS is a deterministic, model-agnostic wrapper around any base policy that proposes \(a^{\mathrm{raw}}_k\in\mathcal{B}\).
We describe the conformal estimator, the banking and feasibility projection (Algorithm~\ref{alg:cps}), a predictive extension, optional PPO co-training, and evaluation arms.

\subsection{Conformal throughput lower bound}
\label{sec:method:conformal}

The point predictor is the harmonic mean of the last \(\kappa\) observed throughputs (RobustMPC-style)~\cite{yin2015control}.
After each step we record the multiplicative residual \(r=C^{\mathrm{act}}/\hat{C}\) in a rolling window of size \(W\).
With \(n\) residuals, the split-conformal \(\alpha\)-quantile uses the finite-sample rank \(\lfloor\alpha(n{+}1)\rfloor\) (1-based) rather than a linearly interpolated empirical quantile, which under-covers in short episodes~\cite{vovk2005algorithmic,angelopoulos2023gentle}.
If that rank is undefined (\(n\) too small), we fall back to a fixed scale \(\rho_{\mathrm{fb}}{=}0.80\).
The lower bound is \(\underline{C}_k=\hat{C}_k\cdot q_{\alpha}\) (clipped to not exceed \(\hat{C}_k\)).
Under residual exchangeability, \(\mathbb{P}(C^{\mathrm{act}}\ge\underline{C}_k)\ge 1-\alpha\).

\begin{proposition}[Per-chunk stall certificate]
\label{prop:feas}
Fix chunk \(k\), executed index \(j\), buffer \(B_k\), and conformal lower bound \(\underline{C}_k\) computed with miscoverage \(\alpha\).
If \(d_k(j)\le \max\{B_k-m,0.1\}\) via~\eqref{eq:dl} and the realized throughput satisfies \(C^{\mathrm{act}}_k\ge \underline{C}_k\), then \(\tau^{\mathrm{stall}}_k=0\).
Under exchangeability of conformal residuals, \(\mathbb{P}(C^{\mathrm{act}}_k\ge \underline{C}_k)\ge 1-\alpha\) at each step where the bound is defined.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
When \(C^{\mathrm{act}}_k\ge \underline{C}_k\), the realized download time is at most \(d_k(j)\), which by assumption does not exceed \(B_k\); hence~\eqref{eq:buffer} gives \(\tau^{\mathrm{stall}}_k=0\).
Coverage follows from standard split-conformal prediction with the \((n{+}1)\) rank correction~\cite{vovk2005algorithmic,angelopoulos2023gentle}.
\end{proof}
\emph{Remark.} Banking may execute a lower rung than the proposal, which can only shorten \(d_k(\cdot)\); safety projection therefore preserves the certificate once~\eqref{eq:asafe} is satisfied.

\emph{Scope of the guarantee.} The certificate is \emph{marginal} coverage over the exchangeable residual window---\(\mathbb{P}(C^{\mathrm{act}}_k\ge\underline{C}_k)\ge 1-\alpha\) averaged over draws---not coverage \emph{conditional} on the current buffer or throughput regime, which distribution-free split conformal cannot provide without stronger assumptions~\cite{angelopoulos2023gentle}. When exchangeability is violated by distribution drift, coverage degrades gracefully by a term controlled by the total-variation distance between the calibration and test residuals~\cite{barber2023beyond}, and a weighted-conformal variant can restore coverage by down-weighting stale residuals.
Moreover, the online sliding window makes residuals only \emph{approximately} exchangeable under nonstationarity: recent residuals both calibrate the quantile and precede the predicted chunk, so the finite-sample bound holds exactly only when the window is exchangeable.
We therefore treat Proposition~\ref{prop:feas} as the design target and validate it empirically through the reported coverage (Section~\ref{sec:eval}), which meets \(1-\alpha\) on the test episodes.

\subsection{Banking and certified feasibility}
\label{sec:method:shield}

Banking is what distinguishes CPS from prior VMAF-\emph{ranking} shields, and the distinction is not cosmetic: on the monotone ladders used in practice, a ranking shield provably cannot bank at all.

\begin{proposition}[Ranking shields collapse on monotone ladders]
\label{prop:collapse}
Fix chunk \(k\) and suppose the VMAF ladder is nondecreasing in the rung index, \(V_k(0)\le\cdots\le V_k(L-1)\), and that download time \(d_k(\cdot)\) is nondecreasing in the rung index. Let \(\mathcal{F}=\{j: d_k(j)\le B_k-m\}\) be the feasible set. Then any shield that returns \(\arg\max_{j\in\mathcal{F}} V_k(j)\) returns the highest feasible index \(\max\mathcal{F}\), independently of any perceptual-loss budget \(\varepsilon\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Because \(d_k\) is nondecreasing, \(\mathcal{F}\) is a prefix \(\{0,\dots,j_{\max}\}\) with \(j_{\max}=\max\mathcal{F}\). Since \(V_k\) is nondecreasing, \(\arg\max_{j\in\mathcal{F}} V_k(j)=j_{\max}\); the budget \(\varepsilon\) never enters the maximization.
\end{proof}
Thus retaining-VMAF ranking is equivalent to highest-feasible-index projection and frees no bandwidth. CPS instead references the \emph{proposal's} quality and downshifts to the smallest rung within \(\varepsilon\) of it (\eqref{eq:knee}), exploiting rate--quality \emph{saturation}: when top rungs share near-identical VMAF, the knee lies well below \(j_{\max}\) and the saved bytes become banked buffer.

Algorithm~\ref{alg:cps} implements~\eqref{eq:knee}--\eqref{eq:asafe}.
If \(B_k\le B_{\mathrm{crit}}\), CPS forces the lowest rung.
Otherwise it computes the knee under the effective budget \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{eff}}\) (equal to \(\varepsilon\) unless predictive mode is on; Section~\ref{sec:method:predictive}), then decrements until~\eqref{eq:dl} respects the margin \(m\).
The shield never upgrades the proposal.
Diagnostics record banked bits, VMAF given up, and whether the realized throughput met \(\underline{C}_k\) (empirical coverage).

\begin{algorithm}[!t]
\footnotesize
\caption{Certified Perceptual Shield (compact)}
\label{alg:cps}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\STATE \textbf{Input:} \(a^{\mathrm{raw}}\), \(B_k\), \(\{\sigma_k(j),V_k(j)\}\), conformal estimator; parameters \((\varepsilon,m,B_{\mathrm{crit}})\)
\STATE \textbf{Output:} \((a^{\mathrm{exec}},\iota)\) with \(\iota=\mathbf{1}[a^{\mathrm{exec}}\neq a^{\mathrm{raw}}]\)
\STATE \(a\leftarrow\mathrm{clip}(a^{\mathrm{raw}})\); \(\underline{C}_k\leftarrow\) conformal lower bound; define \(d_k(\cdot)\) via~\eqref{eq:dl}
\IF{\(B_k\le B_{\mathrm{crit}}\)} \STATE \textbf{return} \((0,\mathbf{1}[a\neq0])\) \ENDIF
\STATE compute \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{eff}}\) (Section~\ref{sec:method:predictive}); \(j\leftarrow j^{\mathrm{knee}}_k(a)\) via~\eqref{eq:knee} with \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{eff}}\)
\WHILE{\(j>0\) and \(d_k(j)>\max\{B_k-m,0.1\}\)} \STATE \(j\leftarrow j-1\) \ENDWHILE
\STATE \(a^{\mathrm{exec}}\leftarrow\min\{a,j\}\); \textbf{return} \((a^{\mathrm{exec}},\mathbf{1}[a^{\mathrm{exec}}\neq a])\)
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\paragraph{Evaluation arms.}
\textsc{Raw}: execute \(a^{\mathrm{raw}}\) unshielded.
\textsc{Safety}: Algorithm~\ref{alg:cps} with banking disabled (\(j^{\mathrm{knee}}{=}a\)), so only conformal feasibility remains.
\textsc{Certified}: full CPS (banking + feasibility).
Paired seeds make Wilcoxon and TOST tests valid across arms.

\subsection{Predictive and risk-aware perceptual budget}
\label{sec:method:predictive}

When the buffer is comfortable, \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{eff}}{=}\varepsilon\) preserves quality.
As \(B_k\) falls below a risk threshold \(B_{\mathrm{risk}}\), or a short \(H\)-step look-ahead under a planning throughput floor (low quantile of recent observations) predicts a dip below \(B_{\mathrm{risk}}\), we widen
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:eps-eff}
    \varepsilon_{\mathrm{eff}}=\varepsilon+(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{risk}}-\varepsilon)\cdot f(B_k,\mathrm{lookahead}),
\end{equation}
with \(f\in[0,1]\), so the shield \emph{pre-banks} deeper before stalls force emergency cuts.
Defaults used in the ``improved'' experiments: \(\varepsilon{=}\CPSepsilon\), \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{risk}}{=}4\), \(B_{\mathrm{risk}}{=}8\,\mathrm{s}\), \(H{\ge}6\) with forecast quantile \(0.2\).

\subsection{Optional learned hosts and co-design}
\label{sec:method:codesign}

CPS does not require a learned policy.
For RL hosts we train PPO (optionally Lagrangian-shaped) with Stable-Baselines~3~\cite{raffin2021sb3,schulman2017proximal} on content-aware observations (throughput history, buffer, SI/TI, VMAF ladder).
\emph{Co-design:} wrapping the training environment with CPS changes the action distribution seen by the actor--critic, so the policy can learn to propose bolder rungs that banking will safely trim.
We compare, under identical \mbox{5G} train/test provenance, a policy trained without the shield (shield only at eval) against one co-trained with CPS, both deployed under the improved shield at test time.

\subsection{Implementation notes}
\label{sec:method:impl}

Defaults: \(\alpha{=}\CPSalpha\) (coverage target \(\CPScovTarget\)), window \(W{=}200\), \(\kappa{=}5\), margin \(m{=}0.5\,\mathrm{s}\), \(B_{\mathrm{crit}}{=}0.3\,\mathrm{s}\), buffer cap \(12\,\mathrm{s}\) in the primary \mbox{5G} operating point.
Table~\ref{tab:cps:hypers} lists the shield and predictive-banking defaults used in the hero experiments.
Empirical coverage is the fraction of steps with \(C^{\mathrm{act}}\ge\underline{C}_k\).
The projection is cheap: a decision costs \(O(L{+}H)\) over the \(L\) ladder rungs and an \(H\)-step look-ahead, plus \(O(1)\) amortized conformal-quantile maintenance on the length-\(W\) residual window.
Over \(20{,}000\) rollout decisions on a commodity CPU (\(L{=}6\), full predictive shield) the per-decision wall time is \(131\,\mu\mathrm{s}\) median and \(310\,\mu\mathrm{s}\) at the \(99\)th percentile---about \(10^{-5}\) of the \(4\,\mathrm{s}\) chunk budget---so CPS is deployable inline without perceptibly affecting the control loop.

\section{Experimental evaluation}
\label{sec:eval}

We organize the evaluation around four research questions:

\begin{itemize}
  \item[\textbf{RQ1}] \emph{Banking vs.\ safety.} Does VMAF-knee banking reduce bitrate and rebuffering relative to conformal safety-only projection, with VMAF inside \(\pm\varepsilon\)?
  \item[\textbf{RQ2}] \emph{Coverage.} Does empirical conformal coverage meet the finite-sample target \(1{-}\alpha\)?
  \item[\textbf{RQ3}] \emph{Predictive banking.} Does a risk-aware / look-ahead budget deepen savings without leaving the perceptual envelope?
  \item[\textbf{RQ4}] \emph{Co-design.} How does training with CPS in the loop change the certified operating point relative to shield-at-eval only?
  \item[\textbf{RQ5}] \emph{Per-chunk robustness.} Does banking remain sound and near-lossless when the shield operates on the true (non-monotone) per-chunk ladder rather than the pooled session-mean ladder?
  \item[\textbf{RQ6}] \emph{Hyperparameter sensitivity.} Are the results stable across the perceptual budget \(\varepsilon\) and miscoverage \(\alpha\), and does coverage track the target \(1{-}\alpha\)?
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Experimental setup and datasets}
\label{sec:eval:setup}

We use a multi-video, chunk-timescale emulator (\(K_e{=}48\) chunks/episode, \(T_{\mathrm{c}}{=}4\,\mathrm{s}\), buffer cap \(12\,\mathrm{s}\) unless noted) with four reference titles (Sintel, Big Buck Bunny, CrowdRun, Tears of Steel short) and per-chunk VMAF ladders~\cite{li2016vmaf}.
These titles are chosen not for catalogue breadth but because they span the \emph{ladder structure} CPS depends on: they cover a wide spatial/temporal-complexity (SI/TI) range---from low-motion animation (Big Buck Bunny, Sintel) where top rungs saturate strongly, to high-motion natural content (CrowdRun) whose ladder is steeper and exhibits frequent chunk-level VMAF inversions (Table~\ref{tab:ladder:spacing}). CrowdRun contributes only two usable chunks after per-chunk VMAF pooling, so it acts as a high-complexity stress case rather than a bulk contributor; the banking claim is driven by the saturation-rich titles, and the negative result on broadband (Section~\ref{sec:eval:improved}) further shows we do not cherry-pick regimes favourable to banking. Because CPS is a deterministic per-chunk projection whose behaviour is fully determined by the observed VMAF ladder and buffer state, its effect is governed by ladder shape rather than semantic content, and these four titles exercise both the saturated and the steep/inverted extremes.

\textbf{Primary \mbox{5G} suite.} Synthetic \mbox{5G} traces with intermittent dips and outages, held out from co-design training seeds (\(\CPSepisodes\) paired episodes). We use synthetic traces here because public per-chunk 5G/mmWave throughput logs suitable for chunk-level ABR replay remain scarce; the generator is calibrated to reported 5G/mmWave dip-and-outage dynamics~\cite{narayanan2021variegated,fezeu2024midband}, and the broadband suite below supplies a real-network counterpart.
Train and test trace pools are source-disjoint; co-design policies are trained only on the training pool and evaluated on held-out test seeds.

\textbf{Broadband suite.} \emph{Real measured} broadband traces: the FCC Measuring-Broadband-America fixed-line logs~\cite{fcc2015mba} and the Norway/Nornet mobile-broadband mobility logs~\cite{kvalbein2014} (27 held-out test traces, source-disjoint from training). These are standard public datasets in ABR research and provide a real-network complement to the synthetic \mbox{5G} suite, used here to characterize when banking has little headroom.

\textbf{Hosts.} We span the ABR design space: bitrate-greedy (always top rung), buffer-based ABR (BBA-like), BOLA (the Lyapunov buffer-based rule of the dash.js reference player~\cite{spiteri2018bola,spiteri2018dashjs}), robust model-predictive control (RobustMPC~\cite{yin2015control}, a five-segment planner over a throughput prediction discounted by recent worst-case error), and PPO policies (content-aware ``proposed''; content-blind Pensieve-style). CPS is model-agnostic: the identical wrapper is applied at inference over all five host families, with no host-specific tuning.

\textbf{Arms and pairing.} For each host we roll out \textsc{Raw}, \textsc{Safety}, and \textsc{Certified} on identical Python seeds (same video--trace pairs). Metrics: mean delivered bitrate, total rebuffering, stall-free rate, mean VMAF, intervention rate, and empirical conformal coverage. Paired Wilcoxon signed-rank tests (zero-method \texttt{zsplit}) and TOST equivalence of VMAF within \(\pm\varepsilon{=}\CPSepsilon\)~\cite{lakens2017equivalence}. Defaults: \(\alpha{=}\CPSalpha\), coverage target \(\CPScovTarget\).
We report raw two-sided \(p\)-values; because we run several paired contrasts (bitrate and rebuffering across the host families and two shield settings), we verified that every reported bitrate and rebuffering effect remains significant at \(0.05\) under Holm--Bonferroni correction over the reported family, and the co-design VMAF and rebuffering contrasts are additionally supported by the paired-episode bootstrap.
Each episode is one \((\text{video},\text{trace})\) pair, with the video drawn from the four titles and the trace from the held-out pool; because the four titles recur across episodes, observations are \emph{clustered by title} and the effective content diversity is four, not \(\CPSepisodes\). We therefore treat the paired tests as evidence on \emph{within-pair} shield effects (the shield is compared against itself-off on the identical video--trace pair) rather than as claims of independent-content generalization, and we report per-title behavior (Table~\ref{tab:ladder:spacing}, Section~\ref{sec:eval:perchunk}) and effect sizes alongside \(p\)-values. Extremely small \(p\)-values (e.g.\ \(<10^{-4}\)) reflect the sign-consistency of a paired difference over \(\CPSepisodes\) pairs and should be read as ``strong evidence for a within-pair effect,'' not as precise magnitudes.

\subsection{Model-agnostic banking on \mbox{5G} (RQ1--RQ2)}
\label{sec:eval:main}

Table~\ref{tab:cps:full} reports absolute paired-arm metrics on synthetic \mbox{5G} test traces for five host families---greedy, BBA, BOLA, RobustMPC, and a Pensieve-style negative control.
Figure~\ref{fig:cps:overview} summarizes the \textsc{Certified} vs.\ \textsc{Safety} contrast across hosts and regimes.
Under greedy, banking cuts bitrate by \(\CPSGreedyBWcs\%\) and rebuffering by \(\CPSGreedyRebCs\%\) (\(p{=}\CPSGreedyPRebCs\)); under BBA the corresponding figures are \(\CPSBbaBWcs\%\) and \(\CPSBbaRebCs\%\).
The two strong deterministic baselines follow the same pattern: BOLA banks \(\CPSBolaBWcs\%\) bitrate and \(\CPSBolaRebCs\%\) rebuffering, and RobustMPC \(\CPSMpcBWcs\%\) and \(\CPSMpcRebCs\%\), each at \(p{=}\CPSMpcPRebCs\) or below.
Mean VMAF differences are \(\CPSGreedyVMcs\), \(\CPSBbaVMcs\), \(\CPSBolaVMcs\), and \(\CPSMpcVMcs\) points across greedy, BBA, BOLA, and RobustMPC---all inside \(\pm\varepsilon\) by TOST.
Relative to \textsc{Raw}, certified greedy reduces rebuffering by \(\CPSGreedyRebCr\%\) (BBA: \(\CPSBbaRebCr\%\)), showing that conformal safety already removes most catastrophic stalls while banking supplies additional headroom.
Empirical coverage is \(\CPSGreedyCov\) (greedy) and \(\CPSBbaCov\) (BBA) against target \(\CPScovTarget\), validating the \((n{+}1)\) conformal calibration on these episode lengths.
Because coverage is a property of the throughput trace and the conformal calibrator---not of the base policy---it is identical (\(\CPSGreedyCov\)) across every host and shield variant evaluated on this test set (Figure~\ref{fig:cps:coverage}).
Proposition~\ref{prop:feas} is a \emph{per-chunk} probabilistic certificate, not a per-episode stall-free guarantee: with coverage \(\CPSGreedyCov\), roughly \(8\%\) of chunks may see the realized throughput fall below \(\underline{C}_k\), and the forced downshift at \(B_{\mathrm{crit}}\) and the cold-start of the first chunk contribute the residual rebuffering reported for the certified arm.
Pensieve collapses to near-floor bitrates with near-zero stalls, so CPS interventions are rare---banking cannot help a policy that never requests saturated rungs.

The high intervention rate under greedy (Table~\ref{tab:cps:full}) is expected \emph{by construction} and should not be read as the shield dominating an informed base policy: greedy always proposes the top rung, so essentially every banking downshift or feasibility repair registers as an intervention. Intervention rate thus measures how far a host sits from the certified operating point, not how much ``credit'' belongs to the shield. This is precisely why we report the model-agnostic contrast on hosts that span the spectrum: the certified intervention rate falls monotonically from \(\CPSGreedyCertInt\%\) for always-top greedy, to \(\CPSBolaCertInt\%\) for BOLA, to just \(\CPSMpcCertInt\%\) for RobustMPC, which already plans conservatively against a discounted throughput estimate---and to near zero for the Pensieve-style control that sits at the floor. The smarter the base policy, the less the shield has to do, yet the certified arm still holds coverage at \(\CPSGreedyCov\) throughout, and its metrics increasingly reflect the base policy's own choices modulated only where banking headroom exists.

\subsection{Predictive banking and broadband boundary (RQ3)}
\label{sec:eval:improved}

Enabling risk-aware \(\varepsilon(B)\) and dip forecasting increases certified-vs-safety bitrate reduction to \(\CPSGreedyImpBWcs\%\) (greedy) and \(\CPSBbaImpBWcs\%\) (BBA), with rebuffering reductions \(\CPSGreedyImpRebCs\%\) and \(\CPSBbaImpRebCs\%\).
VMAF costs remain small (\(\CPSGreedyImpVMcs\), \(\CPSBbaImpVMcs\)).

On source-disjoint \emph{real-measured} broadband traces (FCC MBA and Norway/Nornet~\cite{fcc2015mba,kvalbein2014}), greedy certified-vs-safety bitrate change shrinks to \(\CPSbbBWcs\%\): top rungs are rarely jointly feasible and saturated, so banking has little room.
Safety vs.\ raw still cuts rebuffering by \(\CPSbbRebSr\%\), confirming that the conformal feasibility layer---not banking---dominates under broadband stress.
The broadband ``BB greedy'' group in Figure~\ref{fig:cps:overview} makes this regime split explicit: banking savings concentrate on synthetic \mbox{5G}, while broadband leaves little knee headroom.
This split is intentional: CPS banking is most valuable when the ladder knee is often reachable.

\subsection{Learned hosts and co-design (RQ4)}
\label{sec:eval:codesign}

We report two distinct PPO settings. The \emph{primary} host is a content-aware policy trained on source-disjoint broadband traces and evaluated, unchanged, on \mbox{5G} (the model-agnostic deployment case). The \emph{co-design} pair are two policies trained on matched \mbox{5G} traces---one with the shield only at eval, one co-trained with CPS in the loop---compared on their certified arms (Table~\ref{tab:cps:codesign} and Figure~\ref{fig:cps:codesign}).
On the primary broadband-trained policy, certified banking vs.\ safety yields \(\CPSPropBWcs\%\) bitrate and \(\CPSPropRebCs\%\) rebuffering (\(p{=}\CPSPropPRebCs\)), with VMAF \(\Delta{=}\CPSPropVMcs\); the improved shield reaches \(\CPSPropImpBWcs\%\) / \(\CPSPropImpRebCs\%\).
Gains are smaller than under greedy/BBA because the learned policy already avoids the top rung much of the time---consistent with CPS intervening only when proposals leave banking headroom.

\emph{Co-design.} Both policies are trained on matched \mbox{5G} traces (source-disjoint test). Under the improved shield at eval, the co-trained policy's certified arm reaches VMAF \(\CPScoCpsVM\) and bitrate \(\CPScoCpsBr\)\,kb/s vs.\ \(\CPScoRegVM\) / \(\CPScoRegBr\) for shield-at-eval only (\(\Delta\)VMAF \(+\CPScoDVM\), \(p{=}\CPScoPVM\); \(\Delta\)reb \(+\CPScoDReb\)\,s, \(p{=}\CPScoPReb\)).
Defining \(\mathrm{QoE}(w)=\overline{\mathrm{VMAF}}-w\cdot\mathrm{rebuffer(s)}\), the mean-QoE crossover is \(w^{\star}{=}\CPScoWstar\) VMAF points per second: co-design wins for fidelity-favoring and balanced stall weights \(w<w^{\star}\); shield-at-eval wins only in strongly stall-averse regimes.
A paired-episode bootstrap (\(\CPScoBootN\) episodes, \(20{,}000\) resamples) puts the VMAF gain at \([\CPScoDVMlo,\CPScoDVMhi]\) with \(\CPScoDVMposPct\%\) of resamples positive, the stall cost at \([\CPScoDRebLo,\CPScoDRebHi]\)\,s, and the crossover at \(w^{\star}\in[\CPScoWstarLo,\CPScoWstarHi]\) (median \(\CPScoWstarMed\)); the fidelity gain is thus robust, while \(w^{\star}\) is positive but wide because the denominator (stall cost) is small.

\subsection{Per-chunk (non-monotone) ladder ablation (RQ5)}
\label{sec:eval:perchunk}

The headline runs apply the knee rule to the pooled session-mean ladder, which is monotone; a reviewer may reasonably ask whether banking survives on the \emph{true per-chunk} ladders, which are frequently non-monotone (\(\PCinvPct\%\) of the \(\PCnChunks\) chunks contain at least one inverted adjacent pair; Table~\ref{tab:ladder:spacing}).
We therefore replay the deterministic banking stage on the per-chunk logs, comparing a shield that sees only the pooled ladder against one that sees the per-chunk ladder (Table~\ref{tab:perchunk}, Figure~\ref{fig:cps:perchunk}). Only banking depends on VMAF; the feasibility projection is VMAF-independent and thus identical in both cases, so this isolates the effect of the pooling simplification.
Three findings hold at the default \(\varepsilon{=}\PCeps\).
First, the knee rule remains well defined on inverted ladders and banks \emph{more}, not less: mean saved bitrate is \(\PCbwPerchunk\%\) per-chunk versus \(\PCbwPooled\%\) pooled, because a lower rung that inverts above the proposal trivially satisfies the budget.
Second, a per-chunk shield keeps the realized per-chunk perceptual cost within budget \emph{by construction} (mean \(\PCcostPerchunk\) VMAF points, always \(\le\varepsilon\)).
Third, the pooling simplification is not free: judged against the true per-chunk VMAF, the pooled-ladder shield exceeds the budget on \(\PCviolPooled\%\) of chunks (mean cost \(\PCcostPooled\) points).
Thus the pooled-ladder headline results are, if anything, \emph{conservative} about banking magnitude, and per-chunk operation is sound---removing the residual budget violations at the cost of a per-chunk VMAF feed. This closes the gap between the content-diversity motivation and the pooled evaluation.

\subsection{Sensitivity to \(\varepsilon\) and \(\alpha\) (RQ6)}
\label{sec:eval:sensitivity}

The headline runs fix the two hyperparameters at \(\varepsilon{=}\CPSepsilon\) and \(\alpha{=}\CPSalpha\); Tables~\ref{tab:abl:eps}--\ref{tab:abl:alpha} and Figure~\ref{fig:cps:ablation} show the greedy host is not overfit to that choice.
Sweeping the perceptual budget \(\varepsilon\in\{\AblEpsLo,\dots,\AblEpsHi\}\) (at \(\alpha{=}\CPSalpha\)) trades banking for fidelity monotonically and predictably: bitrate saved versus safety grows from \(\AblBwLo\%\) to \(\AblBwHi\%\), while the mean VMAF change stays negligible (at most \(\AblVmMaxAbs\) points in magnitude) and always inside the corresponding \(\pm\varepsilon\) envelope.
Empirical conformal coverage is \emph{invariant} to \(\varepsilon\) (it depends only on the throughput trace and calibrator, not the banking budget), confirming that banking and the feasibility certificate are decoupled by design.
Sweeping \(\alpha\in\{0.05,0.10,0.20\}\) (at \(\varepsilon{=}\CPSepsilon\)) moves empirical coverage in lock-step with the finite-sample target \(1{-}\alpha\) (coverage \(\AblCovMax\), \(0.919\), \(\AblCovMin\) for targets \(0.95, 0.90, 0.80\)): a stricter \(\alpha\) raises coverage and the intervention rate, a looser \(\alpha\) relaxes both, exactly as the \((n{+}1)\) construction predicts. The default operating point therefore sits on a smooth, well-behaved frontier rather than at a tuned optimum.

\section{Discussion and limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

\textbf{Scope.}
CPS provides an auditable, model-agnostic mechanism that (i)~banks bandwidth on VMAF-saturated ladder knees within a stated perceptual budget and (ii)~enforces download feasibility under a conformal throughput lower bound with target coverage \(1{-}\alpha\).
It does not claim dominance over every ABR controller or a pathwise worst-case guarantee beyond the conformal assumption (residual exchangeability).
Banking gains concentrate in regimes where high rungs are often feasible (moderate-buffer synthetic \mbox{5G}); under harsh broadband stress, safety projection dominates and banking headroom shrinks.

\textbf{Local ladder inversions.}
Table~\ref{tab:ladder:spacing} shows that, at the chunk level, a substantial fraction of adjacent rungs in titles such as Sintel and Big Buck Bunny are near-flat or even \emph{inverted} (a higher bitrate scoring no better---or worse---in VMAF).
CPS requires no special handling for these cases.
The evaluated shield operates on the monotone session-mean ladder, so Proposition~\ref{prop:collapse} applies directly; and even under a fully per-chunk, non-monotone ladder the two CPS stages remain well defined.
The feasibility projection~\eqref{eq:asafe} depends only on download time, not on VMAF, so inversions cannot affect safety.
The knee rule~\eqref{eq:knee} selects the smallest rung within \(\varepsilon\) of the proposal's VMAF; when an inversion makes a lower rung score at least as high, that rung trivially satisfies the budget, so banking simply captures the extra headroom rather than being confused by it.
In other words, local inversions can only enlarge the banking opportunity, and the algorithm passes through them by construction; the per-chunk ablation in Section~\ref{sec:eval:perchunk} confirms this empirically---banked bytes are comparable and the realized per-chunk perceptual cost stays within budget---so adopting per-chunk ladders is an accuracy refinement, not a correctness requirement.

\textbf{Threats to validity.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item The broadband suite replays \emph{real measured} FCC and Norway/Nornet traces~\cite{fcc2015mba,kvalbein2014}, but the \mbox{5G} suite is synthetic and, in both cases, the chunk-level emulator abstracts away transport-stack dynamics and CDN caching~\cite{narayanan2021variegated}; live-player validation is future work.
  \item Four videos and finite trace pools limit catalogue diversity~\cite{kvalbein2014}.
  \item QoE is a VMAF-based surrogate; subjective rankings of stall vs.\ fidelity may differ~\cite{seufert2015survey}.
  \item Conformal coverage assumes exchangeable residuals; strong nonstationarity can weaken the guarantee, though the online window adapts locally and the coverage loss is bounded by the residual distribution shift~\cite{barber2023beyond}.
  \item All arms (greedy, BBA, and the PPO hosts) are evaluated locally under the same finite-sample \((n{+}1)\) conformal correction; co-design magnitudes depend on the specific checkpoint and are reported for the best-validation model of each policy. The co-design contrast uses a \emph{single} training seed per policy: the reported \(95\%\) intervals are a paired-episode bootstrap over the \(\CPScoBootN\) test episodes and therefore capture evaluation---not training-seed---variance. Establishing training-seed variance for \(w^{\star}\) requires retraining multiple seeds and is left to future work; we consequently frame co-design as a supporting result rather than a headline claim.
  \item Pensieve-style no-op shows that host aggressiveness modulates banking opportunity; results should not be extrapolated to arbitrary policies without the paired-arm protocol.
  \item The TOST margin equals the configured banking budget \(\varepsilon\); it is a design parameter, not a preregistered clinical equivalence margin~\cite{lakens2017equivalence}.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Future work.}
Packet-level or field \mbox{5G} evaluation; applying CPS to RobustMPC and Comyco hosts; CVaR / tail-aware objectives~\cite{rockafellar2000}; telemetry-driven adaptation of \(\varepsilon\) and \(\alpha\); and preregistered non-inferiority studies with a fixed perceptual margin.
A first-class direction is a real-player deployment. All results here come from a chunk-level emulator that abstracts away transport-stack dynamics; integrating CPS into a production player such as \texttt{dash.js}~\cite{spiteri2018dashjs} raises concrete challenges that our simulation does not capture. Throughput must be estimated from noisy, bursty HTTP/TCP (or QUIC) samples whose exchangeability---the assumption underpinning the conformal bound---may be violated by slow-start, congestion-control transients, and CDN re-routing, so the calibration residuals would need periodic re-estimation or a sliding, weighted-conformal variant that provably bounds coverage loss under drift~\cite{barber2023beyond}. The feasibility check must also reconcile the player's own buffer-occupancy signal (segment-level, delayed) with the certificate's buffer-margin accounting, and the per-decision budget shifts from our \(\sim\!100\,\mu\mathrm{s}\) micro\-benchmark to a soft real-time constraint alongside the player's ABR loop. Validating that the finite-sample coverage guarantee survives these real-world perturbations---and quantifying any coverage slack it introduces---is the natural next step.

% =========================================
\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We introduced the Certified Perceptual Shield (CPS) for client-side ABR: VMAF-knee bandwidth banking, an online conformal throughput lower bound with finite-sample coverage, and certified buffer feasibility.
On \(\CPSepisodes\) paired synthetic \mbox{5G} episodes, banking versus conformal safety-only reduces bitrate by \(\CPSGreedyBWcs\%\) (greedy) and \(\CPSBbaBWcs\%\) (BBA) while cutting rebuffering by \(\CPSGreedyRebCs\%\) and \(\CPSBbaRebCs\%\), with VMAF inside \(\pm\CPSepsilon\) and empirical coverage \(\CPSGreedyCov\) against target \(\CPScovTarget\).
Predictive banking deepens savings; broadband stress shows the complementary role of safety when the ladder knee is rarely reachable.
Co-training PPO with CPS shifts the certified point toward higher fidelity---a robust \(+\CPScoDVM\) VMAF gain (bootstrap 95\% CI \([\CPScoDVMlo,\CPScoDVMhi]\))---with QoE crossover \(w^{\star}{=}\CPScoWstar\) VMAF points per second of stall (\(p{=}\CPScoPVM\) for VMAF).
CPS is deployable as a lightweight wrapper around existing ABR controllers without retraining, and pairs naturally with learned policies when co-design is desired.
