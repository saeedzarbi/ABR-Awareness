\title{Certified Perceptual Shielding for Adaptive Bitrate Streaming}

\author[uta]{Saeed Zarbi}
\author[uta]{Leili Farzinvash}
\author[uta]{Pedram Salehpour\corref{c1}}
\ead{psalehpour@tabrizu.ac.ir}
\cortext[c1]{Corresponding author}

\address[uta]{Department of Computer Engineering, Faculty of Electrical and Computer Engineering, University of Tabriz, Tabriz, Iran}

\begin{abstract}
Client-side adaptive bitrate (ABR) controllers---whether heuristic, model-based, or learned---can request representations that are either unsafe under volatile throughput or wasteful on rate--quality ladders that saturate in Video Multimethod Assessment Fusion (VMAF).
We introduce a \emph{Certified Perceptual Shield} (CPS): a model-agnostic runtime projection with three ingredients.
First, \emph{VMAF-knee bandwidth banking} downshifts a feasible proposal to the lowest rung whose VMAF lies within a perceptual budget \(\varepsilon\), converting ladder saturation into banked buffer without a material quality loss.
Second, an online \emph{conformal} lower bound on throughput provides distribution-free coverage \(1{-}\alpha\) under exchangeability, using a finite-sample \((n{+}1)\) rank correction.
Third, \emph{certified feasibility} never admits a download whose time under that bound exceeds the available buffer margin, yielding a per-step rebuffering guarantee with probability at least \(1{-}\alpha\).
On \(\CPSepisodes\) paired synthetic \mbox{5G} episodes, CPS banking versus a conformal safety-only baseline reduces delivered bitrate by \(\CPSGreedyBWcs\%\) (greedy) and \(\CPSBbaBWcs\%\) (buffer-based) while cutting rebuffering by \(\CPSGreedyRebCs\%\) and \(\CPSBbaRebCs\%\), with mean VMAF differences inside \(\pm\CPSepsilon\) (TOST) and empirical coverage \(\CPSGreedyCov\) against target \(\CPScovTarget\).
A risk-aware / predictive extension deepens banking before forecast dips.
Co-training a PPO policy with the shield in the loop raises mean VMAF by \(\CPScoDVM\) points at \(+\CPScoDReb\)\,s of stall relative to shield-at-eval only; the fidelity-favoring regime wins for stall weights below the QoE crossover \(w^{\star}{=}\CPScoWstar\) VMAF points per second.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Client-side adaptive bitrate (ABR) policies---buffer heuristics~\cite{huang2014buffer}, model-predictive control~\cite{yin2015control}, and deep reinforcement learning (DRL)~\cite{mao2017neural,schulman2017proximal}---optimize average quality of experience (QoE), yet individual chunk decisions can still stall under volatile sub-6\,GHz and \mbox{5G/mmWave} throughput~\cite{narayanan2021variegated,cisco2023,seufert2015survey}.
A complementary failure mode is waste: commercial rate ladders often \emph{saturate} in Video Multimethod Assessment Fusion (VMAF)~\cite{li2016vmaf}, so the top rungs buy little or no perceptual quality while consuming far more bytes.
Downloading those rungs when the buffer is comfortable leaves the client exposed to the next throughput dip.

Runtime \emph{shields} sit between a base policy and the player and project each proposal before download~\cite{alshiekh2018shielding}.
Legacy shields typically decrement the representation index until a buffer-feasibility test passes.
That repair is content-blind: it does not exploit VMAF saturation to bank bandwidth \emph{proactively}, and its throughput pessimism is usually a hand-tuned factor without a stated coverage guarantee.
Prior VMAF-aware ranking shields that maximize retained VMAF among feasible indices are, on monotone ladders, equivalent to highest-feasible-index projection; a perceptual-loss budget cannot change any decision when VMAF is nondecreasing in the rung index.
The structure that \emph{does} exist in practice is rate--quality saturation at the top of the ladder---a lever for perceptually near-lossless downshifts that free buffer headroom.

We introduce a \emph{Certified Perceptual Shield} (CPS) with three ingredients that exploit that structure and admit a formal coverage statement:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{VMAF-knee bandwidth banking.} Even when the policy action is feasible, CPS lowers it to the smallest rung \(j\) whose VMAF lies within a budget \(\varepsilon\) of the proposal. The downshift is perceptually bounded by construction and converts saved bytes into banked buffer that protects future chunks.
  \item \textbf{Conformal throughput lower bound.} Download-time tests use an online split-conformal lower bound on throughput with target coverage \(1{-}\alpha\), calibrated from residual ratios with a finite-sample \((n{+}1)\) rank correction~\cite{vovk2005algorithmic,angelopoulos2023gentle}.
  \item \textbf{Certified feasibility.} After banking, CPS never admits a rung whose download time under the lower bound exceeds buffer minus a safety margin, yielding a per-step rebuffering bound with probability at least \(1{-}\alpha\) under residual exchangeability.
\end{enumerate}
CPS is model-agnostic: the same wrapper applies to bitrate-greedy, buffer-based (BBA), and learned PPO controllers.
We evaluate three paired arms on identical episode seeds: \textsc{Raw} (unshielded), \textsc{Safety} (conformal feasibility only), and \textsc{Certified} (feasibility plus banking).
The banking contribution is isolated by the \textsc{Certified}--\textsc{Safety} contrast; the safety contribution by \textsc{Safety}/\textsc{Certified} versus \textsc{Raw}.

On \(\CPSepisodes\) paired synthetic \mbox{5G} episodes with \(\varepsilon{=}\CPSepsilon\) and \(\alpha{=}\CPSalpha\), certified banking versus safety-only reduces mean bitrate by \(\CPSGreedyBWcs\%\) under greedy and \(\CPSBbaBWcs\%\) under BBA, while reducing rebuffering by \(\CPSGreedyRebCs\%\) and \(\CPSBbaRebCs\%\) (paired Wilcoxon \(p{=}\CPSGreedyPRebCs\)).
Mean VMAF differences remain inside \(\pm\varepsilon\) by TOST equivalence.
Empirical conformal coverage is \(\CPSGreedyCov\) against target \(\CPScovTarget\).
A risk-aware / predictive extension that widens \(\varepsilon\) when the buffer (or a short look-ahead under a throughput floor) enters a danger zone deepens savings (greedy: \(\CPSGreedyImpBWcs\%\) bitrate, \(\CPSGreedyImpRebCs\%\) rebuffering).
On source-disjoint broadband traces, banking headroom shrinks (\(\CPSbbBWcs\%\) bitrate) while safety still cuts stalls sharply (\(\CPSbbRebSr\%\) vs.\ raw greedy), clarifying the operating regime where perceptual banking pays off.
Co-training PPO with CPS in the loop yields a bolder certified operating point: \(+\CPScoDVM\) mean VMAF and \(+\CPScoDReb\)\,s rebuffering versus shield-at-eval only, with QoE crossover \(w^{\star}{=}\CPScoWstar\) VMAF points per second of stall.

The main contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Certified Perceptual Shield.} A model-agnostic runtime projection combining VMAF-knee bandwidth banking, a conformal throughput lower bound with finite-sample coverage, and certified buffer feasibility (Section~\ref{sec:method}).

  \item \textbf{Isolated banking evaluation.} Paired \textsc{Raw}/\textsc{Safety}/\textsc{Certified} rollouts on greedy, BBA, and PPO hosts, with Wilcoxon tests on bitrate and rebuffering and TOST non-inferiority on VMAF within \(\pm\varepsilon\) (Section~\ref{sec:eval}).

  \item \textbf{Predictive / risk-aware banking.} A buffer- and look-ahead-conditioned perceptual budget that pre-banks before forecast dips, improving the bandwidth--stall trade-off under the same \(\varepsilon\) ceiling at comfortable buffers (Section~\ref{sec:method:predictive}).

  \item \textbf{Regime characterization.} We show that banking gains concentrate where top rungs are often feasible (synthetic \mbox{5G} moderate buffer), whereas broadband stress is dominated by safety projection (Section~\ref{sec:eval}).

  \item \textbf{Policy--shield co-design.} Training with CPS in the loop shifts the certified operating point toward higher fidelity; we report the QoE crossover weight \(w^{\star}\) that separates fidelity-favoring from stall-averse regimes (Section~\ref{sec:eval:codesign}).
\end{itemize}

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows.
Section~\ref{sec:related} reviews related work.
Section~\ref{sec:system} formalizes the streaming model.
Section~\ref{sec:method} presents CPS and optional co-training.
Section~\ref{sec:eval} reports the evaluation, Section~\ref{sec:limits} discusses limitations, and Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} concludes.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

We review three areas relevant to client-side representation selection: model-based ABR, learned ABR, and constrained control with runtime shielding.
Our focus is the control policy and its runtime action projection, not throughput prediction.
Predictors such as CS2P~\cite{sun2016cs2p} can provide inputs to model-based or learned controllers~\cite{yin2015control,mao2017neural}.
ABR surveys~\cite{bentaleb2018survey,seufert2015survey} and \mbox{5G/mmWave} measurements~\cite{narayanan2021variegated} further motivate control mechanisms that remain effective under volatile throughput.

\subsection{Heuristic and model-based ABR}

MPEG Dynamic Adaptive Streaming over HTTP (DASH) decouples packaging from transport; clients run local policies informed by ladder metadata and download observations~\cite{sodagar2011mpeg}.
Buffer-threshold heuristics such as BBA~\cite{huang2014buffer} are simple and transparent, whereas BOLA~\cite{spiteri2018bola} derives its decisions from a Lyapunov-based utility formulation.
RobustMPC~\cite{yin2015control} selects representations by solving a short-horizon model predictive control (MPC) problem under estimated future throughput.
These approaches expose interpretable thresholds and horizons, but their performance can depend on manual calibration and prediction accuracy across networks and devices~\cite{narayanan2021variegated,cisco2023}.
Conventional feasibility rules also generally do not use VMAF saturation to bank bandwidth proactively~\cite{li2016vmaf}.
SODA~\cite{chen2024soda} addresses smoothness-aware online control, while edge assistance~\cite{mehrabi2019edge} and Oboe-style automatic tuning~\cite{akhtar2018oboe} modify complementary parts of the delivery system.

\subsection{Deep reinforcement learning for ABR}

Pensieve~\cite{mao2017neural} established a trace-driven DRL formulation for ABR.
Related methods include D-DASH~\cite{gadaleta2017ddash} and more recent PPO-based controllers~\cite{naresh2023ppoabr}.
Their neural policies combine throughput histories, buffer state, and manifest features~\cite{sutton2018reinforcement}.
Comyco~\cite{huang2019comyco} uses imitation learning with a VMAF-based QoE objective, demonstrating the value of perceptual information within the learned policy.
Loki~\cite{zhang2021loki} combines learned features with a rule-based model to reduce long-tail failures in production.
Fugu~\cite{yan2019learning} evaluates learned control in live networks and illustrates the importance of distribution shift between simulation and deployment.
These systems motivate complementary constrained-learning and decision-time mechanisms because an expected-return objective alone does not impose an explicit per-episode stall or smoothness budget~\cite{hu2023corel,gu2024safereview}.
Even when a learned controller is trained with explicit CMDP constraints, its guarantees and empirical performance are defined with respect to the sampled trajectory distribution.
A previously unseen blockage pattern can still induce an unsafe proposal at an individual chunk boundary.
This observation motivates a separation between a learned performance layer and a runtime enforcement layer; additional policy training is not a substitute for runtime validation.

\subsection{Safe/constrained RL, shields, and the content-blindness gap}

CMDPs and Lagrangian primal--dual methods provide a framework for optimizing expected return subject to explicit constraints~\cite{altman1999constrained,paternain2019dual,achiam2017constrained}.
Safe-RL surveys discuss related shield, Lyapunov, and risk-sensitive approaches~\cite{gu2024safereview,chow2018lyapunov}.
COREL~\cite{hu2023corel} is closest to our training formulation.
It models ABR over commercial \mbox{5G/mmWave} traces as a CMDP with a stall-time constraint and applies a primal--dual actor--critic method.
Its constraint mechanism is embedded in training rather than implemented as a separate runtime projection with intervention telemetry.
Runtime shielding instead repairs a proposed action before execution and can record when such repair occurs~\cite{alshiekh2018shielding}.

Our method separates a (optional) learned performance layer from a deterministic runtime CPS.
Learned policies such as Lagrangian PPO can optimize expected stall and smoothness budgets over training rollouts~\cite{paternain2019dual,hu2023corel}, but they do not certify per-chunk feasibility under conformal throughput bounds.
CPS addresses a different requirement: before every download it (i)~banks bandwidth on VMAF-saturated rungs within a perceptual budget \(\varepsilon\) and (ii)~projects onto actions that are feasible under a distribution-free throughput lower bound with coverage \(1{-}\alpha\)~\cite{vovk2005algorithmic,angelopoulos2023gentle,alshiekh2018shielding}.
Unlike Comyco, where VMAF enters only the learned objective~\cite{huang2019comyco}, CPS uses the ladder at decision time for \emph{proactive} banking, not only for emergency index repair.

\section{System model and problem formulation}
\label{sec:system}

\subsection{Streaming architecture and control loop}

We adopt the standard Hypertext Transfer Protocol (HTTP) adaptive-streaming abstraction~\cite{yin2015control,mao2017neural,sodagar2011mpeg}.
A video origin or content delivery network (CDN) exposes \(L\) encoded representations.
We denote their indices by \(\mathcal{B}=\{0,\ldots,L{-}1\}\); the DASH/HTTP Live Streaming (HLS) manifest provides the corresponding per-chunk sizes and perceptual metadata.
At decision step \(k\), the client requests chunk \(k\) while maintaining a playout buffer \(B_k\).
The effective trace throughput \(\hat{C}_k\) is exogenous to the controller.
On sub-6\,GHz and \mbox{5G/mmWave} access, this throughput can change abruptly because of blockage, LOS/NLOS transitions, scheduling, and mobility~\cite{narayanan2021variegated,cisco2023}.

A base policy \(\pi\) (heuristic or learned) maps an augmented state \(s_k\) to a proposed action \(a^{\mathrm{raw}}_k\in\mathcal{B}\).
Before chunk \(k\) is downloaded, the Certified Perceptual Shield \(\mathcal{S}\) applies the deterministic projection
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:shield-map}
    \big(a^{\mathrm{exec}}_k,\iota_k\big)=\mathcal{S}\!\left(s_k,a^{\mathrm{raw}}_k\right),\qquad a^{\mathrm{exec}}_k\in\mathcal{B},\ \ \iota_k\in\{0,1\},
\end{equation}
yielding the executed representation index \(a^{\mathrm{exec}}_k\) and an audit bit \(\iota_k\) that records whether the projection altered the proposal.
Only \(a^{\mathrm{exec}}_k\) enters the chunk download and buffer dynamics~\eqref{eq:buffer}; Figure~\ref{fig:arch} highlights the \(a^{\mathrm{raw}}_k\!\rightarrow\!\mathcal{S}\!\rightarrow\!a^{\mathrm{exec}}_k\) path.
Decisions evolve on the chunk clock with playback duration \(T_{\mathrm{c}}\) per step (e.g., \(4\,\mathrm{s}\) in our harness)~\cite{mao2017neural,yin2015control}.

Optional Lagrangian PPO training addresses stall and smoothness budgets in expectation over rollouts~\cite{paternain2019dual,achiam2017constrained}.
It does not provide a per-step feasibility guarantee under conformal throughput bounds.
CPS provides complementary decision-time banking and certified repair~\cite{alshiekh2018shielding,vovk2005algorithmic}.

\subsection{State, action, reward, and nonlinear perceptual mapping}

\emph{State \(s_k\).}
The observation includes buffer occupancy, past throughput samples, and the previous decision.
It also includes manifest lookahead: upcoming chunk sizes across the representation ladder and spatial/temporal complexity tags (SI/TI)~\cite{yin2015control,sodagar2011mpeg,mao2017neural}.
The state also includes the VMAF ladder \(\{V_k(j)\}_{j\in\mathcal{B}}\), where \(V_k(j)\) is the perceptual score for chunk \(k\) at representation \(j\)~\cite{li2016vmaf}.
Without \(\{V_k(j)\}\), neither a learned host nor CPS could exploit content-dependent ladder saturation for bandwidth banking.

\emph{Nonlinear, content-dependent fidelity.}
For each chunk \(k\), the map \(j\mapsto V_k(j)\) is not affine in the discrete index \(j\).
It also varies across chunks because different group-of-pictures (GOP) and texture structures produce different marginal VMAF gains between adjacent rungs.
The mapping from nominal bitrate to VMAF is therefore nonlinear and content dependent.
As a result, adjacent rungs can differ by a few VMAF points or by more than ten, and top rungs often saturate---the structure CPS banking exploits~\cite{li2016vmaf}.

\emph{Action.}
\(a^{\mathrm{raw}}_k\in\mathcal{B}\) is the policy's proposed representation index for chunk \(k\); the environment executes only \(a^{\mathrm{exec}}_k\).

\emph{QoE surrogate.}
Following RobustMPC/Pensieve reporting conventions~\cite{yin2015control,mao2017neural}, we log a scalar per-chunk surrogate with fidelity \(q_k \equiv V_k\!\left(a^{\mathrm{exec}}_k\right)\):
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:qoe-rep}
    \mathrm{QoE}^{\mathrm{(rep)}}_{k} = q_k - \alpha\, \text{smooth}_k - \beta\, \tau^{\mathrm{stall}}_k,
\end{equation}
where \(\text{smooth}_k=\lvert q_k-q_{k-1}\rvert\) is the absolute VMAF change between adjacent chunks and \(\tau^{\mathrm{stall}}_k\) is the stall duration at step \(k\).
For the first chunk, we set the smoothness term to zero.
The shared evaluation harness fixes \(\beta{=}4.3\) per stall second and \(\alpha{=}1.0\) per absolute VMAF-point change.
The rebuffering weight follows a Pensieve-style penalty rescaled to the VMAF fidelity axis.
Session QoE is \(\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}_e}\mathrm{QoE}^{\mathrm{(rep)}}_{k}\) (Section~\ref{sec:eval}).
The CMDP objective below separates the fidelity objective \(\sum q_k\) from explicit constraints on rebuffering and smoothness~\cite{altman1999constrained,paternain2019dual}.

\subsection{Ladder structure and the per-video approximation}
\label{sec:system:ladder}

The evaluated system supplies one VMAF value per representation and video, obtained by pooling the per-frame scores of the encode, so the ladder entries it consumes do not vary with \(k\).
To quantify what this approximation discards, we recomputed \(V_k(j)\) directly from the per-frame VMAF logs, aggregating frames into the same four-second chunks that the simulator uses.
This yields \(\LadNChunks\) chunks and \(\LadNPairs\) adjacent-rung gaps across the four test videos.
The per-frame logs cover a prefix of each encode, and CrowdRun contributes only two usable chunks, so its stability figures are not informative; BigBuckBunny and TearsOfSteel supply \(165\) of the \(\LadNChunks\) chunks.

Figure~\ref{fig:ladder} and Table~\ref{tab:ladder:spacing} summarize the outcome.
Spacing between adjacent rungs is strongly nonuniform and, more consequentially for projection, unstable across chunks: for \LadWidestVideo{} the \(\LadWidestPair\)~kb/s gap ranges from \(\LadWidestMin\) to \(\LadWidestMax\) VMAF points depending on the chunk.
Using the per-video gap to predict the chunk-level gap incurs a mean absolute error of \(\LadGapErrMean\) VMAF points, rising to \(\LadGapErrPNinety\) at the \(90\)th percentile and \(\LadGapErrMax\) in the worst chunk.

Two structural properties matter for the projection rule.
First, \(\LadPctPairsFlat\%\) of adjacent pairs differ by at most \(\LadFlatThresh\) VMAF point, so in those chunks a one-step downgrade is perceptually almost free and the index order carries little information about perceptual cost.
Second, the per-chunk ladder is not monotone: \(\LadPctPairsInverted\%\) of adjacent pairs are inverted, and at least one inversion appears in \(\LadPctChunksInverted\%\) of chunks.
For \LadCheapVideo{}, the \(\LadCheapPair\)~kb/s step has a mean gap of \(\LadCheapMean\) VMAF points, meaning the higher rung is on average no better than the lower one.

The per-video ladder is monotone by construction. Highest-VMAF emergency ranking among feasible indices therefore coincides with highest-feasible-index selection, so a max-VMAF loss budget cannot change executed actions on this ladder.
CPS instead uses the \emph{smallest} within-budget rung~\eqref{eq:knee}, which becomes active precisely when the ladder saturates (near-flat VMAF at the top).
Chunk-level measurements motivate exposing \(\{V_k(j)\}\) to the shield even when the session-mean ladder is monotone.
The per-chunk ladder is not monotone, so a per-chunk instantiation of the same rule would admit decisions that no index-based rule can express.
We did not retrain or re-evaluate the controller with per-chunk ladders; we therefore report this as a scoped limitation of the evaluated system and a concrete extension, not as a result.

\subsection{Chunk-timescale dynamics}

\subsubsection{Throughput and latency}
Let \(\hat{C}_k>0\) denote the trace-derived effective throughput, in kb/s, for chunk \(k\).
All methods receive the same throughput trace in paired evaluations.
Choosing \(a^{\mathrm{exec}}_k\) determines the manifest payload size \(\sigma_{k}(a^{\mathrm{exec}}_k)\) in bits.
With \(\tilde{C}_k=\max\{\hat{C}_k,\epsilon\}\), \(\epsilon{>}0\), the nominal download duration is \(d_k=\sigma_{k}(a^{\mathrm{exec}}_k)/(10^3\tilde{C}_k)\) seconds~\cite{yin2015control}.

\subsubsection{Buffer and stall}
Let \(B_k\ge 0\) denote the buffered playback time at the start of the download of chunk \(k\)~\cite{yin2015control,mao2017neural}.
Rebuffering (stall) and buffer update follow
\begin{equation}
    \tau^{\mathrm{stall}}_k=\max\{0,\, d_k-B_k\},
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:buffer}
    B_{k+1}=\max\{0,B_k-d_k\}+T_{\mathrm{c}}.
\end{equation}
Randomness across episodes arises from trace and initial-condition sampling~\cite{fcc2015mba,kvalbein2014,bentaleb2018survey}.
Conditional on \((s_k,a^{\mathrm{exec}}_k,\hat{C}_k)\), each transition is deterministic.

\subsection{CMDP costs, safety budgets, and Lagrangian relaxation}

An unconstrained MDP maximizes \(\mathbb{E}[\sum_k \gamma^k r_k]\) alone.
We instead model streaming as a constrained MDP (CMDP) that maximizes expected fidelity subject to budgets on trajectory-level auxiliary costs~\cite{altman1999constrained}.
Let \(c^{\mathrm{stall}}_k \equiv \tau^{\mathrm{stall}}_k\) denote the instantaneous stall cost (seconds of stall on step \(k\)) and \(c^{\mathrm{sm}}_k \equiv \text{smooth}_k\) the smoothness cost.
A canonical discounted formulation uses discount \(\gamma\in(0,1]\), trajectory distribution \(\mathcal{D}\), and budgets \(d_{\mathrm{rf}}\) and \(d_{\mathrm{sm}}\):
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cmdp-disc}
    \begin{aligned}
    \mathbb{E}_{\pi_{\theta},\mathcal{D}}
    \Bigl[\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}_{e}}\gamma^{k-1}c^{\mathrm{stall}}_{k}\Bigr]
    &\le d_{\mathrm{rf}},\\
    \mathbb{E}_{\pi_{\theta},\mathcal{D}}
    \Bigl[\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}_{e}}\gamma^{k-1}c^{\mathrm{sm}}_{k}\Bigr]
    &\le d_{\mathrm{sm}}.
    \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
For logging and dual updates, we use the undiscounted episodic rebuffering ratio \(\mathrm{RB}(e)\) and mean smoothness cost \(\mathrm{SM}(e)\) over \(\mathcal{K}_e=\{1,\ldots,K_e\}\)~\cite{mao2017neural,paternain2019dual}.
Let \(T^{\mathrm{play}}_{e}=K_{e}T_{\mathrm{c}}\). Then
\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{RB}(e)=\frac{1}{T^{\mathrm{play}}_{e}}\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}_{e}} c^{\mathrm{stall}}_k,
    \qquad
    \mathrm{SM}(e)=\frac{1}{K_{e}}\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}_{e}} c^{\mathrm{sm}}_k.
\end{equation}
The constrained program motivating training is
\begin{align}
\label{eq:prim}
    \max_{\pi_\theta} ~ & \mathbb{E}_{\pi_\theta,\,\mathcal{D}}\Big[\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}_{e}} q_k\Big],\\
\label{eq:c1}
    \text{s.t.} ~~ & \mathbb{E}_{\pi_\theta,\,\mathcal{D}}\Big[\mathrm{RB}(e)\Big] \le \delta_{\mathrm{rf}},\\
\label{eq:c2}
    & \mathbb{E}_{\pi_\theta,\,\mathcal{D}}\Big[\mathrm{SM}(e)\Big] \le \delta_{\mathrm{sm}},
\end{align}
with targets fixed at \(\delta_{\mathrm{rf}}{=}0.05\) (rebuffering ratio) and \(\delta_{\mathrm{sm}}{=}3.5\) (mean absolute VMAF change per chunk)~\cite{yin2015control}.

Because these constraints are generally difficult to enforce directly during nonconvex policy optimization, we form a Lagrangian relaxation.
We define the per-step normalized costs \(\widetilde{\mathrm{RB}}_k=c^{\mathrm{stall}}_k/T^{\mathrm{play}}_e\) and \(\widetilde{\mathrm{SM}}_k=c^{\mathrm{sm}}_k/K_e\), whose episodic sums equal \(\mathrm{RB}(e)\) and \(\mathrm{SM}(e)\), respectively.
The relaxed objective is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:L}
    \mathcal{L}(\theta,\lambda_{r},\lambda_{s})
    =\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{\theta},\mathcal{D}}\!\left[
    \sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}_{e}}
    \bigl(
        q_{k}
        -\lambda_{r}\widetilde{\mathrm{RB}}_{k}
        -\lambda_{s}\widetilde{\mathrm{SM}}_{k}
    \bigr)\right],
\end{equation}
where \(\lambda_r,\lambda_s\ge 0\) are adapted by projected dual ascent on empirical constraint gaps \(g_r=\hat{\mathrm{RB}}-\delta_{\mathrm{rf}}\), \(g_s=\hat{\mathrm{SM}}-\delta_{\mathrm{sm}}\)~\cite{paternain2019dual,altman1999constrained}.
The PPO reward is defined as \(q_k-\lambda_r\widetilde{\mathrm{RB}}_k-\lambda_s\widetilde{\mathrm{SM}}_k\), with optional stabilizers described in Section~\ref{sec:method}.
Finite-sample PPO with clipped objectives does not certify constraint satisfaction at every iterate~\cite{schulman2017proximal,achiam2017constrained}; this gap motivates the hard CPS projection in~\eqref{eq:shield-map}.

\emph{Remark (auxiliary Lyapunov cues).}
Training optionally subtracts buffer-deviation and simulator Lyapunov penalties~\cite{neely2010stochastic} outside \((\lambda_r,\lambda_s)\); these serve as stabilizers, not formal certificates.

\subsection{Certified Perceptual Shield as a hard projection}
\label{sec:system:proj}

Let \(V_k(j)\) denote the VMAF of chunk \(k\) at rung \(j\), \(\sigma_k(j)\) its size in bits, and \(B_k\) the buffer in seconds.
CPS computes a conformal throughput lower bound \(\underline{C}_k\) (kb/s) and the implied download time
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:dl}
    d_k(j)=\min\!\left\{\frac{\sigma_k(j)}{\underline{C}_k\cdot 10^{3}+\varepsilon_0},\ 60\right\}.
\end{equation}
\emph{Safety set.} Indices that never exceed the proposal and fit the buffer margin \(m\) form
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:asafe}
    \mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{safe},k}(a)=\bigl\{j\le a:\ d_k(j)\le \max\{B_k-m,0.1\}\bigr\}.
\end{equation}
\emph{VMAF-knee banking.} Even when \(a\) is feasible, CPS selects the \emph{smallest} rung within a perceptual budget \(\varepsilon\) of the proposal,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:knee}
    j^{\mathrm{knee}}_k(a)=\min\bigl\{j\le a:\ V_k(a)-V_k(j)\le\varepsilon\bigr\},
\end{equation}
then projects \(j^{\mathrm{knee}}_k(a)\) onto \(\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{safe},k}(a)\) (safety wins over \(\varepsilon\)).
Unlike highest-VMAF emergency ranking---which coincides with highest-feasible-index on monotone ladders---banking exploits \emph{saturation}: when top rungs share nearly equal VMAF,~\eqref{eq:knee} frees bytes without a material perceptual loss and banks them as buffer.

\section{Method: Certified Perceptual Shield}
\label{sec:method}

CPS is a deterministic, model-agnostic wrapper around any base policy that proposes \(a^{\mathrm{raw}}_k\in\mathcal{B}\).
We describe the conformal estimator, the banking and feasibility projection (Algorithm~\ref{alg:cps}), a predictive extension, optional PPO co-training, and evaluation arms.

\subsection{Conformal throughput lower bound}
\label{sec:method:conformal}

The point predictor is the harmonic mean of the last \(\kappa\) observed throughputs (RobustMPC-style)~\cite{yin2015control}.
After each step we record the multiplicative residual \(r=C^{\mathrm{act}}/\hat{C}\) in a rolling window of size \(W\).
With \(n\) residuals, the split-conformal \(\alpha\)-quantile uses the finite-sample rank \(\lfloor\alpha(n{+}1)\rfloor\) (1-based) rather than a linearly interpolated empirical quantile, which under-covers in short episodes~\cite{vovk2005algorithmic,angelopoulos2023gentle}.
If that rank is undefined (\(n\) too small), we fall back to a fixed scale \(\rho_{\mathrm{fb}}{=}0.80\).
The lower bound is \(\underline{C}_k=\hat{C}_k\cdot q_{\alpha}\) (clipped to not exceed \(\hat{C}_k\)).
Under residual exchangeability, \(\mathbb{P}(C^{\mathrm{act}}\ge\underline{C}_k)\ge 1-\alpha\).

\subsection{Banking and certified feasibility}
\label{sec:method:shield}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:cps} implements~\eqref{eq:knee}--\eqref{eq:asafe}.
If \(B_k\le B_{\mathrm{crit}}\), CPS forces the lowest rung.
Otherwise it computes the knee under the effective budget \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{eff}}\) (equal to \(\varepsilon\) unless predictive mode is on; Section~\ref{sec:method:predictive}), then decrements until~\eqref{eq:dl} respects the margin \(m\).
The shield never upgrades the proposal.
Diagnostics record banked bits, VMAF given up, and whether the realized throughput met \(\underline{C}_k\) (empirical coverage).

\begin{algorithm}[!t]
\footnotesize
\caption{Certified Perceptual Shield (compact)}
\label{alg:cps}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\STATE \textbf{Input:} \(a^{\mathrm{raw}}\), \(B_k\), \(\{\sigma_k(j),V_k(j)\}\), conformal estimator; parameters \((\varepsilon,m,B_{\mathrm{crit}})\)
\STATE \textbf{Output:} \((a^{\mathrm{exec}},\iota)\) with \(\iota=\mathbf{1}[a^{\mathrm{exec}}\neq a^{\mathrm{raw}}]\)
\STATE \(a\leftarrow\mathrm{clip}(a^{\mathrm{raw}})\); \(\underline{C}_k\leftarrow\) conformal lower bound; define \(d_k(\cdot)\) via~\eqref{eq:dl}
\IF{\(B_k\le B_{\mathrm{crit}}\)} \STATE \textbf{return} \((0,\mathbf{1}[a\neq0])\) \ENDIF
\STATE compute \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{eff}}\) (Section~\ref{sec:method:predictive}); \(j\leftarrow j^{\mathrm{knee}}_k(a)\) via~\eqref{eq:knee} with \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{eff}}\)
\WHILE{\(j>0\) and \(d_k(j)>\max\{B_k-m,0.1\}\)} \STATE \(j\leftarrow j-1\) \ENDWHILE
\STATE \(a^{\mathrm{exec}}\leftarrow\min\{a,j\}\); \textbf{return} \((a^{\mathrm{exec}},\mathbf{1}[a^{\mathrm{exec}}\neq a])\)
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\paragraph{Evaluation arms.}
\textsc{Raw}: execute \(a^{\mathrm{raw}}\) unshielded.
\textsc{Safety}: Algorithm~\ref{alg:cps} with banking disabled (\(j^{\mathrm{knee}}{=}a\)), so only conformal feasibility remains.
\textsc{Certified}: full CPS (banking + feasibility).
Paired seeds make Wilcoxon and TOST tests valid across arms.

\subsection{Predictive and risk-aware perceptual budget}
\label{sec:method:predictive}

When the buffer is comfortable, \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{eff}}{=}\varepsilon\) preserves quality.
As \(B_k\) falls below a risk threshold \(B_{\mathrm{risk}}\), or a short \(H\)-step look-ahead under a planning throughput floor (low quantile of recent observations) predicts a dip below \(B_{\mathrm{risk}}\), we widen
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:eps-eff}
    \varepsilon_{\mathrm{eff}}=\varepsilon+(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{risk}}-\varepsilon)\cdot f(B_k,\mathrm{lookahead}),
\end{equation}
with \(f\in[0,1]\), so the shield \emph{pre-banks} deeper before stalls force emergency cuts.
Defaults used in the ``improved'' experiments: \(\varepsilon{=}\CPSepsilon\), \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{risk}}{=}4\), \(B_{\mathrm{risk}}{=}8\,\mathrm{s}\), \(H{\ge}6\) with forecast quantile \(0.2\).

\subsection{Optional learned hosts and co-design}
\label{sec:method:codesign}

CPS does not require a learned policy.
For RL hosts we train PPO (optionally Lagrangian-shaped) with Stable-Baselines~3~\cite{raffin2021sb3,schulman2017proximal} on content-aware observations (throughput history, buffer, SI/TI, VMAF ladder).
\emph{Co-design:} wrapping the training environment with CPS changes the action distribution seen by the actor--critic, so the policy can learn to propose bolder rungs that banking will safely trim.
We compare, under identical \mbox{5G} train/test provenance, a policy trained without the shield (shield only at eval) against one co-trained with CPS, both deployed under the improved shield at test time.

\subsection{Implementation notes}
\label{sec:method:impl}

Defaults: \(\alpha{=}\CPSalpha\) (coverage target \(\CPScovTarget\)), window \(W{=}200\), \(\kappa{=}5\), margin \(m{=}0.5\,\mathrm{s}\), \(B_{\mathrm{crit}}{=}0.3\,\mathrm{s}\), buffer cap \(12\,\mathrm{s}\) in the primary \mbox{5G} operating point.
Empirical coverage is the fraction of steps with \(C^{\mathrm{act}}\ge\underline{C}_k\).
The wrapper is pure Python/NumPy and adds negligible latency relative to chunk duration.

\section{Experimental evaluation}
\label{sec:eval}

We organize the evaluation around four research questions:

\begin{itemize}
  \item[\textbf{RQ1}] \emph{Banking vs.\ safety.} Does VMAF-knee banking reduce bitrate and rebuffering relative to conformal safety-only projection, with VMAF inside \(\pm\varepsilon\)?
  \item[\textbf{RQ2}] \emph{Coverage.} Does empirical conformal coverage meet the finite-sample target \(1{-}\alpha\)?
  \item[\textbf{RQ3}] \emph{Predictive banking.} Does a risk-aware / look-ahead budget deepen savings without leaving the perceptual envelope?
  \item[\textbf{RQ4}] \emph{Co-design.} How does training with CPS in the loop change the certified operating point relative to shield-at-eval only?
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Experimental setup and datasets}
\label{sec:eval:setup}

We use a multi-video, chunk-timescale emulator (\(K_e{=}48\) chunks/episode, \(T_{\mathrm{c}}{=}4\,\mathrm{s}\), buffer cap \(12\,\mathrm{s}\) unless noted) with four reference titles (Sintel, Big Buck Bunny, CrowdRun, Tears of Steel short) and per-chunk VMAF ladders~\cite{li2016vmaf}.

\textbf{Primary \mbox{5G} suite.} Synthetic \mbox{5G}-style traces with intermittent dips and outages~\cite{narayanan2021variegated}, held out from co-design training seeds (\(\CPSepisodes\) paired episodes).

\textbf{Broadband suite.} Source-disjoint FCC/Norway-style traces~\cite{fcc2015mba,kvalbein2014} used to characterize when banking has little headroom.

\textbf{Hosts.} Bitrate-greedy (always top rung), buffer-based ABR (BBA-like), and PPO policies (content-aware ``proposed''; content-blind Pensieve-style). CPS is model-agnostic: the same wrapper is applied at inference.

\textbf{Arms and pairing.} For each host we roll out \textsc{Raw}, \textsc{Safety}, and \textsc{Certified} on identical Python seeds (same video--trace pairs). Metrics: mean delivered bitrate, total rebuffering, stall-free rate, mean VMAF, intervention rate, and empirical conformal coverage. Paired Wilcoxon signed-rank tests (zero-method \texttt{zsplit}) and TOST equivalence of VMAF within \(\pm\varepsilon{=}\CPSepsilon\)~\cite{lakens2017equivalence}. Defaults: \(\alpha{=}\CPSalpha\), coverage target \(\CPScovTarget\).

\subsection{Predictive banking and broadband boundary (RQ3)}
\label{sec:eval:improved}

Enabling risk-aware \(\varepsilon(B)\) and dip forecasting increases certified-vs-safety bitrate reduction to \(\CPSGreedyImpBWcs\%\) (greedy) and \(\CPSBbaImpBWcs\%\) (BBA), with rebuffering reductions \(\CPSGreedyImpRebCs\%\) and \(\CPSBbaImpRebCs\%\).
VMAF costs remain small (\(\CPSGreedyImpVMcs\), \(\CPSBbaImpVMcs\)).

On source-disjoint broadband traces, greedy certified-vs-safety bitrate change shrinks to \(\CPSbbBWcs\%\): top rungs are rarely jointly feasible and saturated, so banking has little room.
Safety vs.\ raw still cuts rebuffering by \(\CPSbbRebSr\%\), confirming that the conformal feasibility layer---not banking---dominates under broadband stress.
This regime split is intentional: CPS banking is most valuable when the ladder knee is often reachable.

\section{Discussion and limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

\textbf{Scope.}
CPS provides an auditable, model-agnostic mechanism that (i)~banks bandwidth on VMAF-saturated ladder knees within a stated perceptual budget and (ii)~enforces download feasibility under a conformal throughput lower bound with target coverage \(1{-}\alpha\).
It does not claim dominance over every ABR controller or a pathwise worst-case guarantee beyond the conformal assumption (residual exchangeability).
Banking gains concentrate in regimes where high rungs are often feasible (moderate-buffer synthetic \mbox{5G}); under harsh broadband stress, safety projection dominates and banking headroom shrinks.

\textbf{Threats to validity.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Simulation may miss live cellular dynamics, transport-stack effects, and CDN caching~\cite{narayanan2021variegated}.
  \item Four videos and finite trace pools limit catalogue diversity~\cite{kvalbein2014}.
  \item QoE is a VMAF-based surrogate; subjective rankings of stall vs.\ fidelity may differ~\cite{seufert2015survey}.
  \item Conformal coverage assumes exchangeable residuals; strong nonstationarity can weaken the guarantee, though the online window adapts locally.
  \item RL co-design results use pulled checkpoints; model-agnostic greedy/BBA tables were regenerated under the finite-sample conformal correction used in the paper.
  \item Pensieve-style no-op shows that host aggressiveness modulates banking opportunity; results should not be extrapolated to arbitrary policies without the paired-arm protocol.
  \item The TOST margin equals the configured banking budget \(\varepsilon\); it is a design parameter, not a preregistered clinical equivalence margin~\cite{lakens2017equivalence}.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Future work.}
Packet-level or field \mbox{5G} evaluation; applying CPS to RobustMPC and Comyco hosts; CVaR / tail-aware objectives~\cite{rockafellar2000}; telemetry-driven adaptation of \(\varepsilon\) and \(\alpha\); and preregistered non-inferiority studies with a fixed perceptual margin.

% =========================================
\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We introduced the Certified Perceptual Shield (CPS) for client-side ABR: VMAF-knee bandwidth banking, an online conformal throughput lower bound with finite-sample coverage, and certified buffer feasibility.
On \(\CPSepisodes\) paired synthetic \mbox{5G} episodes, banking versus conformal safety-only reduces bitrate by \(\CPSGreedyBWcs\%\) (greedy) and \(\CPSBbaBWcs\%\) (BBA) while cutting rebuffering by \(\CPSGreedyRebCs\%\) and \(\CPSBbaRebCs\%\), with VMAF inside \(\pm\CPSepsilon\) and empirical coverage \(\CPSGreedyCov\) against target \(\CPScovTarget\).
Predictive banking deepens savings; broadband stress shows the complementary role of safety when the ladder knee is rarely reachable.
Co-training PPO with CPS shifts the certified point toward higher fidelity, with QoE crossover \(w^{\star}{=}\CPScoWstar\) VMAF points per second of stall.
CPS is deployable as a lightweight wrapper around existing ABR controllers without retraining, and pairs naturally with learned policies when co-design is desired.
