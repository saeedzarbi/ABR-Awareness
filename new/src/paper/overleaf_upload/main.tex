\title{Certified Perceptual Shielding for Adaptive Bitrate Streaming}

\author[uta]{Saeed Zarbi}
\ead{szarbi@tabrizu.ac.ir}
\author[uta]{Leili Farzinvash}
\ead{l.farzinvash@tabrizu.ac.ir}
\author[uta]{Pedram Salehpour\corref{c1}}
\ead{psalehpour@tabrizu.ac.ir}
\cortext[c1]{Corresponding author.}

\address[uta]{Department of Computer Engineering, Faculty of Electrical and Computer Engineering, University of Tabriz, Tabriz, Iran}

\begin{abstract}
Client-side adaptive bitrate (ABR) controllers share one blind spot, whether heuristic, model-based, or learned. The trouble surfaces chunk by chunk. A single request turns unsafe when throughput swings or turns wasteful once the rate--quality ladder has already saturated in Video Multimethod Assessment Fusion (VMAF).
We answer both faults with a \emph{Certified Perceptual Shield} (CPS), a model-agnostic runtime projection resting on three ingredients.
First comes \emph{VMAF-knee bandwidth banking}. Even a feasible proposal gets lowered to the smallest rung whose VMAF sits within a perceptual budget \(\varepsilon\); saturation then becomes banked buffer, and quality barely moves.
Second is an online \emph{conformal} lower bound on throughput. Under exchangeability, it yields distribution-free coverage \(1{-}\alpha\), calibrated with a finite-sample \((n{+}1)\) rank correction.
Third, \emph{certified feasibility}. CPS refuses any download whose time under that bound would outrun the buffer margin. What emerges is a per-chunk \emph{probabilistic} stall certificate: coverage \(1{-}\alpha\) under residual exchangeability (Proposition~\ref{prop:feas}). Call it a strong distribution-free guarantee, not a deterministic promise of zero stalls.
Our tests span \(\CPSepisodes\) paired synthetic \mbox{5G} episodes over twelve reference titles chosen for \emph{ladder} diversity---from saturation-rich animation to steep natural motion. Against a conformal safety-only baseline, banking significantly trims delivered bitrate and rebuffering on bitrate-hungry hosts (greedy: \(\CPSGreedyBWcs\%\)/\(\CPSGreedyRebCs\%\); buffer-based: \(\CPSBbaBWcs\%\)/\(\CPSBbaRebCs\%\)), with mean VMAF differences inside \(\pm\CPSepsilon\) (TOST) and empirical coverage at \(\CPSGreedyCov\) against a \(\CPScovTarget\) target. Effect sizes track top-rung saturation, so the diversified roster yields conservative but credible savings.
A risk-aware, predictive variant deepens banking ahead of forecast dips (greedy: \(\CPSGreedyImpBWcs\%\) bitrate, \(\CPSGreedyImpRebCs\%\) rebuffering).
One result deserves its own line. Co-training a PPO policy with the shield in the loop lifts mean VMAF by \(\CPScoDVM\) points (paired-episode bootstrap 95\% CI \([\CPScoDVMlo,\CPScoDVMhi]\), positive in \(\CPScoDVMposPct\%\) of resamples), paying \(+\CPScoDReb\)\,s of stall against shield-at-eval-only operation (\(p{=}\CPScoPVM\) for VMAF, \(p{=}\CPScoPReb\) for rebuffering). The fidelity-favoring regime prevails whenever the stall weight drops below the QoE crossover \(w^{\star}{=}\CPScoWstar\) VMAF points per second.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Client-side adaptive bitrate (ABR) policies chase one target: average quality of experience (QoE). The usual designs are buffer heuristics~\cite{huang2014buffer}, model-predictive control~\cite{yin2015control}, and deep reinforcement learning (DRL)~\cite{mao2017neural,schulman2017proximal}.
Averages, though, hide the tail. A single chunk can still stall under volatile sub-6\,GHz and \mbox{5G/mmWave} throughput~\cite{narayanan2021variegated,cisco2023,seufert2015survey}.
Stalling is only half the story. Waste is the other half.
Commercial rate ladders tend to \emph{saturate} in Video Multimethod Assessment Fusion (VMAF)~\cite{li2016vmaf}: the top rungs swallow far more bytes yet add little or no perceptual quality.
Reaching for those rungs while the buffer is comfortable burns bandwidth that could have padded the next throughput dip.

A runtime \emph{shield} is the natural place to step in. It sits between a base policy and the player, projecting each proposal before download~\cite{alshiekh2018shielding}.
The legacy recipe is blunt: decrement the representation index until a buffer-feasibility test passes.
Such repair is content-blind. It ignores VMAF saturation, so it never banks bandwidth \emph{proactively}, and its throughput pessimism reduces to a hand-tuned factor with no stated coverage guarantee.
Earlier VMAF-aware shields fare no better. Maximizing retained VMAF among feasible indices collapses, on monotone ladders, to highest-feasible-index projection, since a perceptual-loss budget alters nothing once VMAF is nondecreasing in the rung index (Proposition~\ref{prop:collapse}).
Real ladders do carry usable structure: rate--quality saturation near the top. That saturation is the lever. It permits perceptually near-lossless downshifts, and those downshifts free buffer headroom.

We introduce a \emph{Certified Perceptual Shield} (CPS). Three ingredients exploit the structure above and carry a formal coverage statement:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{VMAF-knee bandwidth banking.} Even a feasible policy action gets lowered to the smallest rung \(j\) whose VMAF lands within a budget \(\varepsilon\) of the proposal. The downshift is perceptually bounded by construction, and the reclaimed bytes bank as buffer that shields later chunks.
  \item \textbf{Conformal throughput lower bound.} Download-time tests lean on an online split-conformal lower bound on throughput with target coverage \(1{-}\alpha\). Residual ratios calibrate it, with a finite-sample \((n{+}1)\) rank correction~\cite{vovk2005algorithmic,angelopoulos2023gentle}.
  \item \textbf{Certified feasibility.} Once banking is done, CPS admits no rung whose download time under the lower bound exceeds buffer minus a safety margin. Out of this comes a per-chunk \emph{probabilistic} stall certificate: coverage \(1{-}\alpha\) under residual exchangeability, not a deterministic \(100\%\) no-stall guarantee (Proposition~\ref{prop:feas}).
\end{enumerate}
Nothing here is host-specific. The same wrapper drops onto bitrate-greedy, buffer-based (BBA), and learned PPO controllers alike.
To tease the two mechanisms apart, we run three paired arms on identical episode seeds: \textsc{Raw} (unshielded), \textsc{Safety} (conformal feasibility only), and \textsc{Certified} (feasibility plus banking).
Banking shows up in the \textsc{Certified}--\textsc{Safety} contrast; safety shows up in \textsc{Safety}/\textsc{Certified} against \textsc{Raw}.

We put these claims to the test on \(\CPSepisodes\) paired synthetic \mbox{5G} episodes over twelve ladder-diverse titles with \(\varepsilon{=}\CPSepsilon\) and \(\alpha{=}\CPSalpha\). Certified banking against safety-only significantly reduces mean bitrate and rebuffering on greedy and BBA (\(\CPSGreedyBWcs\%\)/\(\CPSGreedyRebCs\%\) and \(\CPSBbaBWcs\%\)/\(\CPSBbaRebCs\%\); paired Wilcoxon \(p{=}\CPSGreedyPRebCs\)). Magnitudes are moderated by steep and non-saturated ladders in the mix, yet the direction holds and mean VMAF differences stay inside \(\pm\varepsilon\) by TOST equivalence---perceptually near-lossless savings.
Empirical conformal coverage lands at \(\CPSGreedyCov\) against a \(\CPScovTarget\) target.
A risk-aware, predictive extension pushes the savings further, widening \(\varepsilon\) once the buffer---or a short look-ahead under a throughput floor---slips into a danger zone (greedy: \(\CPSGreedyImpBWcs\%\) bitrate, \(\CPSGreedyImpRebCs\%\) rebuffering).
Real networks tell a different story. On source-disjoint \emph{real-measured} broadband traces (FCC and Norway/Nornet), banking headroom collapses (\(\CPSbbBWcs\%\) bitrate), yet safety keeps slashing stalls (\(\CPSbbRebSr\%\) vs.\ raw greedy). The split pins down exactly when perceptual banking earns its keep.
Co-training PPO with CPS in the loop then buys a bolder certified operating point: \(+\CPScoDVM\) mean VMAF and \(+\CPScoDReb\)\,s rebuffering against shield-at-eval-only operation, with a QoE crossover at \(w^{\star}{=}\CPScoWstar\) VMAF points per second of stall.

The main contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Certified Perceptual Shield.} A model-agnostic runtime projection combining VMAF-knee bandwidth banking, a conformal throughput lower bound with finite-sample coverage, and certified buffer feasibility (Section~\ref{sec:method}).

  \item \textbf{Isolated banking evaluation.} Paired \textsc{Raw}/\textsc{Safety}/\textsc{Certified} rollouts on greedy, BBA, and PPO hosts, with Wilcoxon tests on bitrate and rebuffering and TOST non-inferiority on VMAF within \(\pm\varepsilon\) (Section~\ref{sec:eval}).

  \item \textbf{Risk-aware, predictive banking.} A buffer- and look-ahead-conditioned perceptual budget that pre-banks before forecast dips, improving the bandwidth--stall trade-off under the same \(\varepsilon\) ceiling at comfortable buffers (Section~\ref{sec:method:predictive}).

  \item \textbf{Regime characterization.} We show that banking gains concentrate where top rungs are often feasible (synthetic \mbox{5G} moderate buffer), whereas real-trace broadband stress is dominated by safety projection (Section~\ref{sec:eval}).

  \item \textbf{Policy--shield co-design.} Training with CPS in the loop shifts the certified operating point toward higher fidelity; we report the QoE crossover weight \(w^{\star}\) that separates fidelity-favoring from stall-averse regimes (Section~\ref{sec:eval:codesign}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

Three areas bear on client-side representation selection: model-based ABR, learned ABR, and constrained control with runtime shielding.
Our lens is the control policy and its runtime action projection. Throughput prediction sits outside it.
Predictors like CS2P~\cite{sun2016cs2p} play a complementary role, feeding inputs to model-based or learned controllers~\cite{yin2015control,mao2017neural}.
ABR surveys~\cite{bentaleb2018survey,seufert2015survey,peroni2025survey} and \mbox{5G/mmWave} measurements~\cite{narayanan2021variegated,fezeu2024midband} make the same case from data: control has to hold up when throughput lurches.

\subsection{Heuristic and model-based ABR}

MPEG Dynamic Adaptive Streaming over HTTP (DASH) splits packaging from transport. Clients then run local policies off ladder metadata and download observations~\cite{sodagar2011mpeg}.
Buffer-threshold heuristics like BBA~\cite{huang2014buffer} stay simple and legible. BOLA~\cite{spiteri2018bola} instead reads its decisions off a Lyapunov-based utility formulation.
RobustMPC~\cite{yin2015control} plans ahead, picking representations by solving a short-horizon model predictive control (MPC) problem under estimated future throughput.
Interpretable thresholds and horizons are the payoff here. The price is fragility: performance leans on manual calibration and on prediction accuracy that wanders across networks and devices~\cite{narayanan2021variegated,cisco2023}.
And conventional feasibility rules almost never mine VMAF saturation to bank bandwidth ahead of time~\cite{li2016vmaf}.
Adjacent work tightens other stages of the pipeline. SODA~\cite{chen2024soda} chases smoothness-aware online control; edge assistance~\cite{mehrabi2019edge} and Oboe-style automatic tuning~\cite{akhtar2018oboe} touch different parts of the delivery system altogether.

\subsection{Deep reinforcement learning for ABR}

Pensieve~\cite{mao2017neural} set the template: a trace-driven DRL formulation for ABR. D-DASH~\cite{gadaleta2017ddash} and newer PPO-based controllers~\cite{naresh2023ppoabr} followed.
Their neural policies fold together throughput histories, buffer state, and manifest features~\cite{sutton2018reinforcement}.
From there, the recipe grew. Comyco~\cite{huang2019comyco} trains by imitation against a VMAF-based QoE objective---proof that perceptual information pays off inside the learned policy.
Loki~\cite{zhang2021loki} welds learned features to a rule-based model, trimming long-tail failures in production.
Fugu~\cite{yan2019learning} runs learned control in live networks and lays bare the distribution shift between simulation and deployment.
Read together, these systems argue for constrained learning and decision-time machinery, because an expected-return objective on its own writes no explicit per-episode stall or smoothness budget~\cite{hu2023corel,gu2024safereview}.
Explicit constraints do not close the gap. Train a controller under a CMDP, and its guarantees still hold only over the sampled trajectory distribution.
Hand it a blockage pattern it never saw, and it can still emit an unsafe proposal at a single chunk boundary.
That is the case for splitting the stack: a learned performance layer on one side, a runtime enforcement layer on the other. More training does not stand in for runtime validation.

\subsection{Safe/constrained RL, shields, and the content-blindness gap}

CMDPs and Lagrangian primal--dual methods optimize expected return under explicit constraints~\cite{altman1999constrained,paternain2019dual,achiam2017constrained}. Safe-RL surveys catalogue the neighbours: shield, Lyapunov, and risk-sensitive schemes~\cite{gu2024safereview,chow2018lyapunov}.
COREL~\cite{hu2023corel} sits nearest to our training formulation.
It casts ABR over commercial \mbox{5G/mmWave} traces as a CMDP with a stall-time constraint, solved by a primal--dual actor--critic.
The constraint, though, lives inside training. It is not a standalone runtime projection with intervention telemetry.
Runtime shielding flips that: it repairs a proposed action before execution and can log the moment a repair fires~\cite{alshiekh2018shielding}. Surveys split the family into \emph{pre-shielding}, which prunes the action set, and \emph{post-shielding}, which overwrites an unsafe proposal~\cite{konighofer2025shields}. By that taxonomy, CPS is a post-shield, its safety set drawn by a conformal feasibility test rather than a temporal-logic model.

This distinction shapes our design. We keep an optional learned performance layer apart from a deterministic runtime CPS.
Learned policies such as Lagrangian PPO can chase expected stall and smoothness budgets across training rollouts~\cite{paternain2019dual,hu2023corel}. What they cannot do is certify per-chunk feasibility under conformal throughput bounds.
CPS aims squarely at that. Before every download it (i)~banks bandwidth on VMAF-saturated rungs inside a perceptual budget \(\varepsilon\), and (ii)~projects onto actions feasible under a distribution-free throughput lower bound with coverage \(1{-}\alpha\)~\cite{vovk2005algorithmic,angelopoulos2023gentle,alshiekh2018shielding}.
Its use of perceptual data also parts ways with Comyco, where VMAF touches only the learned objective~\cite{huang2019comyco}; CPS reads the ladder at decision time for \emph{proactive} banking, not just emergency index repair.

\subsection{Streaming architecture and control loop}

We work in the standard Hypertext Transfer Protocol (HTTP) adaptive-streaming abstraction~\cite{yin2015control,mao2017neural,sodagar2011mpeg}.
A video origin or content delivery network (CDN) publishes \(L\) encoded representations.
We index them by \(\mathcal{B}=\{0,\ldots,L{-}1\}\); the DASH/HTTP Live Streaming (HLS) manifest carries the matching per-chunk sizes and perceptual metadata.
At decision step \(k\), the client fetches chunk \(k\) while holding a playout buffer \(B_k\).
The effective trace throughput \(\hat{C}_k\) is exogenous to the controller.
That is not a mere modeling nicety. On sub-6\,GHz and \mbox{5G/mmWave} access, throughput can jump without warning, driven by blockage, LOS/NLOS transitions, scheduling, and mobility~\cite{narayanan2021variegated,cisco2023}.

A base policy \(\pi\) (heuristic or learned) maps an augmented state \(s_k\) to a proposed action \(a^{\mathrm{raw}}_k\in\mathcal{B}\).
Before chunk \(k\) is downloaded, the Certified Perceptual Shield \(\mathcal{S}\) applies the deterministic projection
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:shield-map}
    \big(a^{\mathrm{exec}}_k,\iota_k\big)=\mathcal{S}\!\left(s_k,a^{\mathrm{raw}}_k\right),\qquad a^{\mathrm{exec}}_k\in\mathcal{B},\ \ \iota_k\in\{0,1\},
\end{equation}
Out come the executed representation index \(a^{\mathrm{exec}}_k\) and an audit bit \(\iota_k\), the latter flagging whether the projection touched the proposal.
Only \(a^{\mathrm{exec}}_k\) feeds the chunk download and buffer dynamics~\eqref{eq:buffer}; Figure~\ref{fig:arch} traces the \(a^{\mathrm{raw}}_k\!\rightarrow\!\mathcal{S}\!\rightarrow\!a^{\mathrm{exec}}_k\) path.
Decisions tick on the chunk clock, one step per playback duration \(T_{\mathrm{c}}\) (e.g., \(4\,\mathrm{s}\) in our harness)~\cite{mao2017neural,yin2015control}.

Each layer owns a different job. Optional Lagrangian PPO training handles stall and smoothness budgets in expectation over rollouts~\cite{paternain2019dual,achiam2017constrained}, yet it promises nothing about per-step feasibility under conformal throughput bounds.
CPS fills that void with decision-time banking and certified repair~\cite{alshiekh2018shielding,vovk2005algorithmic}.

\subsection{State, action, reward, and nonlinear perceptual mapping}

\emph{State \(s_k\).}
The observation holds buffer occupancy, past throughput samples, and the previous decision.
Manifest lookahead rides along too: upcoming chunk sizes across the representation ladder, plus spatial/temporal complexity tags (SI/TI)~\cite{yin2015control,sodagar2011mpeg,mao2017neural}.
The VMAF ladder \(\{V_k(j)\}_{j\in\mathcal{B}}\) rounds it out, where \(V_k(j)\) is the perceptual score for chunk \(k\) at representation \(j\)~\cite{li2016vmaf}.
That last piece is not optional. Strip out \(\{V_k(j)\}\), and neither a learned host nor CPS can read content-dependent ladder saturation for bandwidth banking.

\emph{Nonlinear, content-dependent fidelity.}
Saturation exists for a simple reason: quality does not track rate linearly. For each chunk \(k\), the map \(j\mapsto V_k(j)\) is not affine in the discrete index \(j\).
It shifts chunk to chunk as well, because differing group-of-pictures (GOP) and texture structures hand back different marginal VMAF gains between adjacent rungs.
So the map from nominal bitrate to VMAF is at once nonlinear and content dependent.
The upshot: adjacent rungs may sit a few VMAF points apart or more than ten, and the top rungs often flatten out---exactly the structure CPS banking feeds on~\cite{li2016vmaf}. Perceptually-aware, JND-based per-title encoding tries to strip that flattening away at authoring time~\cite{menon2022perceptual}. CPS works the other end: it harvests whatever saturation a deployed ladder still carries at decision time.

\emph{Action.}
\(a^{\mathrm{raw}}_k\in\mathcal{B}\) is the policy's proposed representation index for chunk \(k\); the environment executes only \(a^{\mathrm{exec}}_k\).

\emph{QoE surrogate.}
Keeping to RobustMPC/Pensieve reporting conventions~\cite{yin2015control,mao2017neural}, we log a scalar per-chunk surrogate with fidelity \(q_k \equiv V_k\!\left(a^{\mathrm{exec}}_k\right)\):
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:qoe-rep}
    \mathrm{QoE}^{\mathrm{(rep)}}_{k} = q_k - \mu\, \text{smooth}_k - \beta\, \tau^{\mathrm{stall}}_k,
\end{equation}
where \(\text{smooth}_k=\lvert q_k-q_{k-1}\rvert\) is the absolute VMAF change between adjacent chunks and \(\tau^{\mathrm{stall}}_k\) is the stall duration at step \(k\).
The first chunk gets a zero smoothness term.
Our shared harness pins \(\beta{=}4.3\) per stall second and \(\mu{=}1.0\) per absolute VMAF-point change; the symbol \(\alpha\) stays reserved for the conformal miscoverage throughout.
The rebuffering weight is a Pensieve-style penalty, rescaled onto the VMAF fidelity axis.
Session QoE sums the chunks: \(\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}_e}\mathrm{QoE}^{\mathrm{(rep)}}_{k}\) (Section~\ref{sec:eval}).
For a learned host, optional RL training swaps in a Lagrangian CMDP objective over rollouts (Appendix~\ref{app:cmdp})~\cite{altman1999constrained,paternain2019dual}.

\subsection{Ladder saturation}
\label{sec:system:ladder}

The evaluated system hands back one VMAF value per representation and video (pooled per-frame scores), so shield decisions run on a session-mean ladder that never varies with chunk index \(k\).
Table~\ref{tab:ladder:spacing} summarizes session-mean ladders for \(\LadNVideos\) publicly available reference titles (six Blender animations, six Xiph DERF clips); Figure~\ref{fig:ladder} illustrates per-chunk spread on a legacy four-title subset where frame-level VMAF logs were retained.
Across the twelve-title suite, \(\LadPctNonMonotone\%\) exhibit a non-monotone session-mean ladder and mean top-rung gain (6000 vs.\ 2850\,kbps) is only \(\LadTopGainMean\) VMAF points---strong saturation on animation titles (e.g.\ Big Buck Bunny at \(+0.03\)), steeper climbs on natural content (e.g.\ Park Joy at \(+14.3\)).
Two traits make banking worthwhile. One: many adjacent pairs differ by at most one VMAF point on saturated titles, so a single-step downgrade costs almost nothing perceptually. Two: top rungs saturate often, which lets the knee rule~\eqref{eq:knee} bank bytes with no material quality loss.
On the monotone per-video ladder we evaluate, highest-VMAF emergency ranking among feasible indices lands on the same choice as highest-feasible-index projection. CPS goes the other way and takes the \emph{smallest} within-budget rung---a move that only bites once saturation renders downshifts near-lossless.

\subsection{Chunk-timescale dynamics}

\subsubsection{Throughput and latency}
Let \(\hat{C}_k>0\) be the trace-derived effective throughput, in kb/s, for chunk \(k\).
Every method sees the identical throughput trace in paired evaluations.
The choice of \(a^{\mathrm{exec}}_k\) sets the manifest payload size \(\sigma_{k}(a^{\mathrm{exec}}_k)\) in bits.
Write \(\tilde{C}_k=\max\{\hat{C}_k,\varepsilon_0\}\) for a small constant \(\varepsilon_0{>}0\) (the same numerical guard as in~\eqref{eq:dl}). The nominal download duration is \(d_k=\sigma_{k}(a^{\mathrm{exec}}_k)/(10^3\tilde{C}_k)\) seconds~\cite{yin2015control}.

\subsubsection{Buffer and stall}
Let \(B_k\ge 0\) be the buffered playback time when the download of chunk \(k\) begins~\cite{yin2015control,mao2017neural}.
Rebuffering (stall) and the buffer update follow
\begin{equation}
    \tau^{\mathrm{stall}}_k=\max\{0,\, d_k-B_k\},
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:buffer}
    B_{k+1}=\max\{0,B_k-d_k\}+T_{\mathrm{c}}.
\end{equation}
Randomness across episodes has one source only: trace and initial-condition sampling~\cite{fcc2015mba,kvalbein2014,bentaleb2018survey}.
Fix \((s_k,a^{\mathrm{exec}}_k,\hat{C}_k)\), and each transition turns deterministic.

\subsection{Optional Lagrangian training for RL hosts}
\label{sec:system:cmdp}

When the base policy is learned, we may train PPO with a Lagrangian-relaxed constrained MDP (CMDP) objective that penalizes rebuffering and smoothness next to fidelity~\cite{altman1999constrained,paternain2019dual,achiam2017constrained}.
The dual variables adapt as training proceeds; Appendix~\ref{app:cmdp} spells out the optional buffer-deviation and Lyapunov stabilizers~\cite{neely2010stochastic}.
None of that certifies per-chunk feasibility. Finite-sample PPO with clipped objectives simply cannot~\cite{schulman2017proximal}, and CPS delivers the missing layer at deployment time.
Co-design wraps the training environment in CPS, so the actor--critic sees the projected action distribution; shield-at-eval-only policies train bare.

\subsection{Certified Perceptual Shield as a hard projection}
\label{sec:system:proj}

Let \(V_k(j)\) be the VMAF of chunk \(k\) at rung \(j\), \(\sigma_k(j)\) its size in bits, and \(B_k\) the buffer in seconds.
CPS first computes a conformal throughput lower bound \(\underline{C}_k\) (kb/s), then the download time it implies,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:dl}
    d_k(j)=\min\!\left\{\frac{\sigma_k(j)}{\underline{C}_k\cdot 10^{3}+\varepsilon_0},\ 60\right\}.
\end{equation}
\emph{Safety set.} Indices that never exceed the proposal and fit the buffer margin \(m\) form
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:asafe}
    \mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{safe},k}(a)=\bigl\{j\le a:\ d_k(j)\le \max\{B_k-m,0.1\}\bigr\}.
\end{equation}
\emph{VMAF-knee banking.} Even when \(a\) is feasible, CPS selects the \emph{smallest} rung within a perceptual budget \(\varepsilon\) of the proposal,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:knee}
    j^{\mathrm{knee}}_k(a)=\min\bigl\{j\le a:\ V_k(a)-V_k(j)\le\varepsilon\bigr\},
\end{equation}
then projects \(j^{\mathrm{knee}}_k(a)\) onto \(\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{safe},k}(a)\). Safety wins over \(\varepsilon\), always.
Here banking parts company with highest-VMAF emergency ranking, which on monotone ladders lands on the highest feasible index. Banking mines \emph{saturation} instead: when the top rungs carry near-equal VMAF,~\eqref{eq:knee} frees bytes at no material perceptual loss and banks them as buffer.

\section{Method: Certified Perceptual Shield}
\label{sec:method}

CPS is a deterministic, model-agnostic wrapper. It sits around any base policy that proposes \(a^{\mathrm{raw}}_k\in\mathcal{B}\).
What follows: the conformal estimator, the banking and feasibility projection (Algorithm~\ref{alg:cps}), a predictive extension, optional PPO co-training, and the evaluation arms.

\subsection{Conformal throughput lower bound}
\label{sec:method:conformal}

The point predictor is the harmonic mean of the last \(\kappa\) observed throughputs, RobustMPC-style~\cite{yin2015control}.
After each step we drop the multiplicative residual \(r=C^{\mathrm{act}}/\hat{C}\) into a rolling window of size \(W\).
With \(n\) residuals in hand, the split-conformal \(\alpha\)-quantile takes the finite-sample rank \(\lfloor\alpha(n{+}1)\rfloor\) (1-based). A linearly interpolated empirical quantile is off the table: it under-covers on short episodes~\cite{vovk2005algorithmic,angelopoulos2023gentle}.
Should that rank be undefined---\(n\) too small---we drop back to a fixed scale \(\rho_{\mathrm{fb}}{=}0.80\).
The lower bound reads \(\underline{C}_k=\hat{C}_k\cdot q_{\alpha}\), clipped to stay at or below \(\hat{C}_k\).
Under residual exchangeability, the construction earns \(\mathbb{P}(C^{\mathrm{act}}\ge\underline{C}_k)\ge 1-\alpha\).

\begin{proposition}[Per-chunk stall certificate]
\label{prop:feas}
Fix chunk \(k\), executed index \(j\), buffer \(B_k\), and conformal lower bound \(\underline{C}_k\) computed with miscoverage \(\alpha\).
If \(d_k(j)\le \max\{B_k-m,0.1\}\) via~\eqref{eq:dl} and the realized throughput satisfies \(C^{\mathrm{act}}_k\ge \underline{C}_k\), then \(\tau^{\mathrm{stall}}_k=0\).
Under exchangeability of conformal residuals, \(\mathbb{P}(C^{\mathrm{act}}_k\ge \underline{C}_k)\ge 1-\alpha\) at each step where the bound is defined.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
When \(C^{\mathrm{act}}_k\ge \underline{C}_k\), the realized download time is at most \(d_k(j)\), which by assumption does not exceed \(B_k\); hence~\eqref{eq:buffer} gives \(\tau^{\mathrm{stall}}_k=0\).
Coverage follows from standard split-conformal prediction with the \((n{+}1)\) rank correction~\cite{vovk2005algorithmic,angelopoulos2023gentle}.
\end{proof}
\emph{Remark.} Banking may run a lower rung than the proposal, and a lower rung can only shorten \(d_k(\cdot)\). So once~\eqref{eq:asafe} holds, safety projection keeps the certificate intact.

\emph{Scope of the guarantee.} Let us be precise about what the certificate promises and what it does not. It gives \emph{marginal} coverage over the exchangeable residual window: \(\mathbb{P}(C^{\mathrm{act}}_k\ge\underline{C}_k)\ge 1-\alpha\), averaged over draws. It does not give coverage \emph{conditional} on the current buffer or throughput regime; distribution-free split conformal cannot, absent stronger assumptions~\cite{angelopoulos2023gentle}. Let drift break exchangeability, and coverage decays gently, by a term the total-variation distance between calibration and test residuals bounds~\cite{barber2023beyond}; a weighted-conformal variant can claw it back by down-weighting stale residuals.
One more wrinkle. The online sliding window leaves residuals only \emph{approximately} exchangeable under nonstationarity: the recent ones both calibrate the quantile and precede the predicted chunk, so the finite-sample bound is exact only on an exchangeable window.
Given all this, we read Proposition~\ref{prop:feas} as the design target and check it empirically through reported coverage (Section~\ref{sec:eval}), which clears \(1-\alpha\) on the test episodes.

\subsection{Banking and certified feasibility}
\label{sec:method:shield}

Banking is the line between CPS and prior VMAF-\emph{ranking} shields, and it is no cosmetic one. On the monotone ladders seen in practice, a ranking shield provably banks nothing.

\begin{proposition}[Ranking shields collapse on monotone ladders]
\label{prop:collapse}
Fix chunk \(k\) and suppose the VMAF ladder is nondecreasing in the rung index, \(V_k(0)\le\cdots\le V_k(L-1)\), and that download time \(d_k(\cdot)\) is nondecreasing in the rung index. Let \(\mathcal{F}=\{j: d_k(j)\le B_k-m\}\) be the feasible set. Then any shield that returns \(\arg\max_{j\in\mathcal{F}} V_k(j)\) returns the highest feasible index \(\max\mathcal{F}\), independently of any perceptual-loss budget \(\varepsilon\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Because \(d_k\) is nondecreasing, \(\mathcal{F}\) is a prefix \(\{0,\dots,j_{\max}\}\) with \(j_{\max}=\max\mathcal{F}\). Since \(V_k\) is nondecreasing, \(\arg\max_{j\in\mathcal{F}} V_k(j)=j_{\max}\); the budget \(\varepsilon\) never enters the maximization.
\end{proof}
So retaining-VMAF ranking is just highest-feasible-index projection under another name, and it frees no bandwidth. CPS reads the \emph{proposal's} quality instead and slides down to the smallest rung within \(\varepsilon\) of it (\eqref{eq:knee}). That is the trick that lets it exploit rate--quality \emph{saturation}: when top rungs carry near-identical VMAF, the knee sits well below \(j_{\max}\), and the freed bytes bank as buffer.

Algorithm~\ref{alg:cps} realizes~\eqref{eq:knee}--\eqref{eq:asafe}.
The logic is short. Should \(B_k\le B_{\mathrm{crit}}\), CPS forces the lowest rung.
Otherwise it computes the knee under the effective budget \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{eff}}\) (equal to \(\varepsilon\) unless predictive mode is on; Section~\ref{sec:method:predictive}), then walks the index down until~\eqref{eq:dl} respects the margin \(m\).
The shield never upgrades a proposal. Not once.
All the while, diagnostics log banked bits, VMAF surrendered, and whether realized throughput met \(\underline{C}_k\) (empirical coverage).

\begin{algorithm}[!t]
\footnotesize
\caption{Certified Perceptual Shield (compact)}
\label{alg:cps}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\STATE \textbf{Input:} \(a^{\mathrm{raw}}\), \(B_k\), \(\{\sigma_k(j),V_k(j)\}\), conformal estimator; parameters \((\varepsilon,m,B_{\mathrm{crit}})\)
\STATE \textbf{Output:} \((a^{\mathrm{exec}},\iota)\) with \(\iota=\mathbf{1}[a^{\mathrm{exec}}\neq a^{\mathrm{raw}}]\)
\STATE \(a\leftarrow\mathrm{clip}(a^{\mathrm{raw}})\); \(\underline{C}_k\leftarrow\) conformal lower bound; define \(d_k(\cdot)\) via~\eqref{eq:dl}
\IF{\(B_k\le B_{\mathrm{crit}}\)} \STATE \textbf{return} \((0,\mathbf{1}[a\neq0])\) \ENDIF
\STATE compute \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{eff}}\) (Section~\ref{sec:method:predictive}); \(j\leftarrow j^{\mathrm{knee}}_k(a)\) via~\eqref{eq:knee} with \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{eff}}\)
\WHILE{\(j>0\) and \(d_k(j)>\max\{B_k-m,0.1\}\)} \STATE \(j\leftarrow j-1\) \ENDWHILE
\STATE \(a^{\mathrm{exec}}\leftarrow\min\{a,j\}\); \textbf{return} \((a^{\mathrm{exec}},\mathbf{1}[a^{\mathrm{exec}}\neq a])\)
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\paragraph{Evaluation arms.}
\textsc{Raw}: run \(a^{\mathrm{raw}}\) unshielded.
\textsc{Safety}: Algorithm~\ref{alg:cps} with banking switched off (\(j^{\mathrm{knee}}{=}a\)), leaving conformal feasibility alone.
\textsc{Certified}: the full CPS, banking plus feasibility.
Paired seeds keep the Wilcoxon and TOST tests valid across arms.

\subsection{Predictive and risk-aware perceptual budget}
\label{sec:method:predictive}

The idea is plain: bank lightly when there is slack, harder when danger looms. While the buffer stays comfortable, \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{eff}}{=}\varepsilon\) holds quality steady.
Two events raise the alarm. Either \(B_k\) drops below a risk threshold \(B_{\mathrm{risk}}\), or a short \(H\)-step look-ahead under a planning throughput floor (a low quantile of recent observations) foresees a dip below \(B_{\mathrm{risk}}\). Either way, we widen the budget,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:eps-eff}
    \varepsilon_{\mathrm{eff}}=\varepsilon+(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{risk}}-\varepsilon)\cdot f(B_k,\mathrm{lookahead}),
\end{equation}
with \(f\in[0,1]\), letting the shield \emph{pre-bank} more deeply before stalls force emergency cuts.
The ``improved'' experiments run with \(\varepsilon{=}\CPSepsilon\), \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{risk}}{=}4\), \(B_{\mathrm{risk}}{=}8\,\mathrm{s}\), and \(H{\ge}6\) at forecast quantile \(0.2\).

\subsection{Optional learned hosts and co-design}
\label{sec:method:codesign}

CPS asks for no learned policy.
When the host is an RL policy, we train PPO (optionally Lagrangian-shaped) with Stable-Baselines~3~\cite{raffin2021sb3,schulman2017proximal} on content-aware observations: throughput history, buffer, SI/TI, VMAF ladder.
\emph{Co-design:} wrap the training environment in CPS, and the action distribution the actor--critic sees changes. The policy can then learn to reach for bolder rungs that banking will trim in safety.
To gauge the effect, we pit two policies against each other under identical \mbox{5G} train/test provenance: one trained without the shield (shield-at-eval-only), one co-trained with CPS. Both face the improved shield at test time.

\subsection{Implementation notes}
\label{sec:method:impl}

At the primary \mbox{5G} operating point the defaults are \(\alpha{=}\CPSalpha\) (coverage target \(\CPScovTarget\)), window \(W{=}200\), \(\kappa{=}5\), margin \(m{=}0.5\,\mathrm{s}\), \(B_{\mathrm{crit}}{=}0.3\,\mathrm{s}\), and buffer cap \(12\,\mathrm{s}\).
Table~\ref{tab:cps:hypers} collects the shield and predictive-banking defaults from the hero experiments; empirical coverage is the fraction of steps with \(C^{\mathrm{act}}\ge\underline{C}_k\).
The projection costs almost nothing. One decision runs \(O(L{+}H)\) over the \(L\) ladder rungs and an \(H\)-step look-ahead, plus \(O(1)\) amortized conformal-quantile upkeep on the length-\(W\) residual window.
The numbers bear this out. Across \(20{,}000\) rollout decisions on a commodity CPU (\(L{=}6\), full predictive shield), per-decision wall time is \(131\,\mu\mathrm{s}\) at the median and \(310\,\mu\mathrm{s}\) at the \(99\)th percentile---roughly \(10^{-5}\) of the \(4\,\mathrm{s}\) chunk budget. CPS runs inline, and the control loop never feels it.

\section{Experimental evaluation}
\label{sec:eval}

We organize the evaluation around six research questions:

\begin{itemize}
  \item[\textbf{RQ1}] \emph{Banking vs.\ safety.} Does VMAF-knee banking reduce bitrate and rebuffering relative to conformal safety-only projection, with VMAF inside \(\pm\varepsilon\)?
  \item[\textbf{RQ2}] \emph{Coverage.} Does empirical conformal coverage meet the finite-sample target \(1{-}\alpha\)?
  \item[\textbf{RQ3}] \emph{Predictive banking.} Does a risk-aware, look-ahead budget deepen savings without leaving the perceptual envelope?
  \item[\textbf{RQ4}] \emph{Co-design.} How does training with CPS in the loop change the certified operating point relative to shield-at-eval-only operation?
  \item[\textbf{RQ5}] \emph{Per-chunk robustness.} Does banking remain sound and near-lossless when the shield operates on the true (non-monotone) per-chunk ladder rather than the pooled session-mean ladder?
  \item[\textbf{RQ6}] \emph{Hyperparameter sensitivity.} Are the results stable across the perceptual budget \(\varepsilon\) and miscoverage \(\alpha\), and does coverage track the target \(1{-}\alpha\)?
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Experimental setup and datasets}
\label{sec:eval:setup}

The testbed is a multi-video, chunk-timescale emulator (\(K_e{=}48\) chunks/episode, \(T_{\mathrm{c}}{=}4\,\mathrm{s}\), buffer cap \(12\,\mathrm{s}\) unless noted), driving twelve publicly available reference titles with session-mean VMAF ladders~\cite{li2016vmaf}: six Blender open animations (Big Buck Bunny, Tears of Steel, Sintel, Elephants Dream, Ducks Take Off, Cosmos Laundromat) and six Xiph DERF clips (Park Joy, Old Town Cross, Rush Hour, Into Tree, Sunflower, Kristen \& Sara).
We picked them for \emph{ladder structure}, not catalogue breadth: saturation-rich animation (Big Buck Bunny, Tears of Steel), steep natural motion (Park Joy, Ducks), near-flat easy codecs (Into Tree, Old Town Cross), and mixed held-out titles (Sintel, Cosmos, Kristen \& Sara) for generalization checks (Table~\ref{tab:ladder:spacing}).
Nine titles appear in PPO co-design training; three are held out entirely (Sintel, Cosmos Laundromat, Kristen \& Sara).
The negative broadband result (Section~\ref{sec:eval:improved}) shows we do not hand-pick regimes that flatter banking. CPS is a deterministic per-chunk projection fixed by the observed VMAF ladder and buffer state, so ladder shape---not semantic content---drives its effect, and the twelve-title suite spans both saturated and steep/inverted session-mean ladders (\(\LadPctNonMonotone\%\) non-monotone).

\textbf{Primary \mbox{5G} suite.} Synthetic \mbox{5G} traces, laced with intermittent dips and outages, held out from the co-design training seeds (\(\CPSepisodes\) paired episodes). Why synthetic? Public per-chunk 5G/mmWave throughput logs fit for chunk-level ABR replay stay scarce. To keep them honest, the generator is tuned to reported 5G/mmWave dip-and-outage dynamics~\cite{narayanan2021variegated,fezeu2024midband}, and the broadband suite below adds a real-network counterpart.
Train and test trace pools stay source-disjoint. Co-design policies train on the training pool alone and face held-out test seeds.

\textbf{Broadband suite.} \emph{Real measured} broadband traces: the FCC Measuring-Broadband-America fixed-line logs~\cite{fcc2015mba} and the Norway/Nornet mobile-broadband mobility logs~\cite{kvalbein2014} (27 held-out test traces, source-disjoint from training). Both are workhorse public datasets in ABR research. They stand as the real-network foil to the synthetic \mbox{5G} suite, and they show where banking runs out of headroom.

\textbf{Hosts.} Five host families cover the ABR design space: bitrate-greedy (forever at the top rung), buffer-based ABR (BBA-like), BOLA (the Lyapunov buffer-based rule of the dash.js reference player~\cite{spiteri2018bola,spiteri2018dashjs}), robust model-predictive control (RobustMPC~\cite{yin2015control}, a five-segment planner over a throughput prediction discounted by recent worst-case error), and PPO policies (content-aware ``proposed'' and content-blind Pensieve-style). CPS carries no host bias: one wrapper, applied at inference across all five families, tuned to none of them.

\textbf{Arms and pairing.} Every host rolls out \textsc{Raw}, \textsc{Safety}, and \textsc{Certified} on identical Python seeds---the same video--trace pairs. We report mean delivered bitrate, total rebuffering, stall-free rate, mean VMAF, intervention rate, and empirical conformal coverage. Significance rests on paired Wilcoxon signed-rank tests (zero-method \texttt{zsplit}) and TOST equivalence of VMAF within \(\pm\varepsilon{=}\CPSepsilon\)~\cite{lakens2017equivalence}, at defaults \(\alpha{=}\CPSalpha\) and coverage target \(\CPScovTarget\).
The \(p\)-values we quote are raw and two-sided. Since several paired contrasts run at once (bitrate and rebuffering across the host families and two shield settings), we checked that every reported bitrate and rebuffering effect survives Holm--Bonferroni correction at \(0.05\) over the reported family; the paired-episode bootstrap backs the co-design VMAF and rebuffering contrasts on top of that.
A caveat about diversity earns mention. Each episode is one \((\text{video},\text{trace})\) pair---video drawn uniformly from the twelve titles, trace from the held-out pool. Observations therefore \emph{cluster by title}, and effective independent-content diversity is twelve, not \(\CPSepisodes\). We treat the paired tests as evidence of \emph{within-pair} shield effects---shield against itself-off on the very same video--trace pair---and we report per-title ladder structure (Table~\ref{tab:ladder:spacing}) with held-out breakdown where applicable. Vanishingly small \(p\)-values (say \(<10^{-4}\)) track sign-consistency of a paired difference over \(\CPSepisodes\) pairs; read them as strong evidence for a within-pair effect, not as precise magnitudes.

\subsection{Model-agnostic banking on \mbox{5G} (RQ1--RQ2)}
\label{sec:eval:main}

Table~\ref{tab:cps:full} lays out absolute paired-arm metrics on synthetic \mbox{5G} test traces for five host families: greedy, BBA, BOLA, RobustMPC, and a Pensieve-style negative control.
Figure~\ref{fig:cps:overview} then distills the \textsc{Certified} vs.\ \textsc{Safety} contrast across hosts and regimes.
Banking behaves consistently wherever top rungs are reachable and saturated. On the full twelve-title suite, greedy and BBA savings are modest in absolute terms (\(\CPSGreedyBWcs\%\)/\(\CPSGreedyRebCs\%\) and \(\CPSBbaBWcs\%\)/\(\CPSBbaRebCs\%\); \(p{=}\CPSGreedyPRebCs\)) because steep ladders (Table~\ref{tab:ladder:spacing}) leave fewer near-lossless downshifts---exactly the regime dependence the mechanism predicts.
BOLA and RobustMPC show the same certified-vs-safety pattern (\(\CPSBolaBWcs\%\)/\(\CPSBolaRebCs\%\) and \(\CPSMpcBWcs\%\)/\(\CPSMpcRebCs\%\), \(p{=}\CPSMpcPRebCs\) or below), with larger fractional headroom where their proposals still reach saturated knees.
Perceptually, the savings are nearly free. Mean VMAF differences come to \(\CPSGreedyVMcs\), \(\CPSBbaVMcs\), \(\CPSBolaVMcs\), and \(\CPSMpcVMcs\) points across greedy, BBA, BOLA, and RobustMPC, every one inside \(\pm\varepsilon\) by TOST.
The two shield layers split the work. Against \textsc{Raw}, certified greedy cuts rebuffering by \(\CPSGreedyRebCr\%\) (BBA: \(\CPSBbaRebCr\%\)): conformal safety mops up the catastrophic stalls, banking adds the rest of the headroom.
Coverage tells its own story. Empirical coverage is \(\CPSGreedyCov\) (greedy) and \(\CPSBbaCov\) (BBA) against a \(\CPScovTarget\) target, which vindicates the \((n{+}1)\) conformal calibration at these episode lengths.
Coverage belongs to the throughput trace and the conformal calibrator, not the base policy, so it reads identically (\(\CPSGreedyCov\)) across every host and shield variant on this test set (Figure~\ref{fig:cps:coverage}).
The scope of the guarantee bears repeating. Proposition~\ref{prop:feas} is a \emph{per-chunk} probabilistic certificate, not a per-episode stall-free one: at coverage \(\CPSGreedyCov\), about \(8\%\) of chunks can watch realized throughput slip below \(\underline{C}_k\), and the forced downshift at \(B_{\mathrm{crit}}\) plus the first-chunk cold start explain the residual rebuffering in the certified arm.
The negative control does what it should. Pensieve caves to near-floor bitrates with near-zero stalls, so CPS rarely fires---banking cannot rescue a policy that never asks for saturated rungs.

That high intervention rate under greedy (Table~\ref{tab:cps:full}) is baked in \emph{by construction}; do not mistake it for the shield lording over an informed base policy. Greedy proposes the top rung every time, so nearly every banking downshift or feasibility repair shows up as an intervention. The rate gauges how far a host sits from the certified operating point, not how much ``credit'' the shield deserves. That is precisely why we run the model-agnostic contrast across hosts spanning the spectrum. Certified intervention slides down without exception: \(\CPSGreedyCertInt\%\) for always-top greedy, \(\CPSBolaCertInt\%\) for BOLA, a mere \(\CPSMpcCertInt\%\) for RobustMPC---which already plans warily against a discounted throughput estimate---and near zero for the floor-hugging Pensieve-style control. The sharper the base policy, the lighter the shield's load. Yet coverage holds at \(\CPSGreedyCov\) throughout, and the certified metrics lean more and more on the base policy's own calls, nudged only where banking headroom survives.

\subsection{Predictive banking and broadband boundary (RQ3)}
\label{sec:eval:improved}

Switch on risk-aware \(\varepsilon(B)\) and dip forecasting, and the certified-vs-safety bitrate reduction climbs to \(\CPSGreedyImpBWcs\%\) (greedy) and \(\CPSBbaImpBWcs\%\) (BBA), with rebuffering down \(\CPSGreedyImpRebCs\%\) and \(\CPSBbaImpRebCs\%\).
VMAF barely pays (\(\CPSGreedyImpVMcs\), \(\CPSBbaImpVMcs\)).

The broadband suite marks the edge of these gains. On source-disjoint \emph{real-measured} broadband traces (FCC MBA and Norway/Nornet~\cite{fcc2015mba,kvalbein2014}), the greedy certified-vs-safety bitrate change dwindles to \(\CPSbbBWcs\%\): top rungs are seldom feasible and saturated at once, so banking has almost nowhere to go.
Safety vs.\ raw still shears rebuffering by \(\CPSbbRebSr\%\), though. Under broadband stress the conformal feasibility layer---not banking---does the heavy lifting.
The ``BB greedy'' group in Figure~\ref{fig:cps:overview} draws the regime split in the open: banking savings pile up on synthetic \mbox{5G}, while broadband leaves the knee out of reach.
The split is by design, not a flaw. CPS banking earns its keep exactly when the ladder knee sits within reach.

\subsection{Learned hosts and co-design (RQ4)}
\label{sec:eval:codesign}

Two PPO settings warrant separate reporting. The \emph{primary} host is a content-aware policy trained on source-disjoint broadband traces and evaluated, untouched, on \mbox{5G}---the model-agnostic deployment case. The \emph{co-design} pair are two policies trained on matched \mbox{5G} traces---one shield-at-eval-only, one co-trained with CPS in the loop---set side by side on their certified arms (Table~\ref{tab:cps:codesign} and Figure~\ref{fig:cps:codesign}).
On the primary broadband-trained policy, certified banking vs.\ safety returns \(\CPSPropBWcs\%\) bitrate and \(\CPSPropRebCs\%\) rebuffering (\(p{=}\CPSPropPRebCs\)), at VMAF \(\Delta{=}\CPSPropVMcs\); the improved shield stretches to \(\CPSPropImpBWcs\%\) / \(\CPSPropImpRebCs\%\).
Smaller than greedy or BBA, and no surprise there: the learned policy already shuns the top rung much of the time, and CPS steps in only when a proposal leaves banking headroom.

\emph{Co-design.} Both policies train on matched \mbox{5G} traces (source-disjoint test). Under the improved shield at eval, the co-trained policy's certified arm hits VMAF \(\CPScoCpsVM\) and bitrate \(\CPScoCpsBr\)\,kb/s, against \(\CPScoRegVM\) / \(\CPScoRegBr\) for shield-at-eval-only operation (\(\Delta\)VMAF \(+\CPScoDVM\), \(p{=}\CPScoPVM\); \(\Delta\)reb \(+\CPScoDReb\)\,s, \(p{=}\CPScoPReb\)).
Weigh the fidelity gain against its stall cost through \(\mathrm{QoE}(w)=\overline{\mathrm{VMAF}}-w\cdot\mathrm{rebuffer(s)}\). The mean-QoE crossover sits at \(w^{\star}{=}\CPScoWstar\) VMAF points per second: co-design takes the fidelity-favoring and balanced stall weights \(w<w^{\star}\), and shield-at-eval-only operation prevails only where the appetite for stalls is very low.
A paired-episode bootstrap (\(\CPScoBootN\) episodes, \(20{,}000\) resamples) puts the VMAF gain at \([\CPScoDVMlo,\CPScoDVMhi]\), positive in \(\CPScoDVMposPct\%\) of resamples, the stall cost at \([\CPScoDRebLo,\CPScoDRebHi]\)\,s, and the crossover at \(w^{\star}\in[\CPScoWstarLo,\CPScoWstarHi]\) (median \(\CPScoWstarMed\)). The fidelity gain holds firm; \(w^{\star}\) stays positive but broad, for the plain reason that its denominator, the stall cost, is tiny.

\subsection{Per-chunk (non-monotone) ladder ablation (RQ5)}
\label{sec:eval:perchunk}

The headline runs apply the knee rule to the pooled session-mean ladder, a monotone one. A fair question: does banking survive on the \emph{true per-chunk} ladders, which often turn non-monotone (\(\PCinvPct\%\) of the \(\PCnChunks\) chunks hold at least one inverted adjacent pair; Table~\ref{tab:ladder:spacing})?
To settle it, we replay the deterministic banking stage on the per-chunk logs, pitting a shield that sees only the pooled ladder against one that sees the per-chunk ladder (Table~\ref{tab:perchunk}, Figure~\ref{fig:cps:perchunk}). Banking alone rides on VMAF; the feasibility projection ignores it and stays identical in both cases, which pins the pooling simplification down on its own.
Three findings hold at the default \(\varepsilon{=}\PCeps\).
One: the knee rule stays well defined on inverted ladders and banks \emph{more}, not less---mean saved bitrate is \(\PCbwPerchunk\%\) per-chunk against \(\PCbwPooled\%\) pooled, since a lower rung that inverts above the proposal clears the budget outright.
Two: a per-chunk shield holds realized per-chunk perceptual cost inside budget \emph{by construction} (mean \(\PCcostPerchunk\) VMAF points, never above \(\varepsilon\)).
Three: pooling is not free. Judged against the true per-chunk VMAF, the pooled-ladder shield overruns the budget on \(\PCviolPooled\%\) of chunks (mean cost \(\PCcostPooled\) points).
Put the three together and the pooled-ladder headline numbers look, if anything, \emph{conservative} about how much banking wins; per-chunk operation is sound, wiping out the residual budget violations for the price of a per-chunk VMAF feed. That bridges the content-diversity motivation and the pooled evaluation.

\subsection{Sensitivity to \(\varepsilon\) and \(\alpha\) (RQ6)}
\label{sec:eval:sensitivity}

The headline runs fix the pair at \(\varepsilon{=}\CPSepsilon\) and \(\alpha{=}\CPSalpha\). Tables~\ref{tab:abl:eps}--\ref{tab:abl:alpha} and Figure~\ref{fig:cps:ablation} show the greedy host is no slave to that choice.
Take the perceptual budget first. Sweeping \(\varepsilon\in\{\AblEpsLo,\dots,\AblEpsHi\}\) (at \(\alpha{=}\CPSalpha\)) trades banking for fidelity, and it does so cleanly: bitrate saved versus safety climbs from \(\AblBwLo\%\) to \(\AblBwHi\%\), while the mean VMAF change stays trifling (at most \(\AblVmMaxAbs\) points in magnitude) and never leaves the matching \(\pm\varepsilon\) envelope.
Empirical conformal coverage, tellingly, does not budge with \(\varepsilon\)---it answers to the throughput trace and calibrator alone, not the banking budget, which shows banking and the feasibility certificate run on separate tracks by design.
Now the miscoverage. Sweeping \(\alpha\in\{0.05,0.10,0.20\}\) (at \(\varepsilon{=}\CPSepsilon\)) drags empirical coverage in lock-step with the finite-sample target \(1{-}\alpha\) (coverage \(\AblCovMax\), \(0.919\), \(\AblCovMin\) for targets \(0.95, 0.90, 0.80\)): tighten \(\alpha\) and both coverage and intervention rate rise; loosen it and both ease off, just as the \((n{+}1)\) construction foretells. So the default operating point rides a smooth, well-mannered frontier, not a tuned sweet spot.

\section{Discussion and limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

\textbf{Scope.}
CPS is an auditable, model-agnostic mechanism that (i)~banks bandwidth on VMAF-saturated ladder knees inside a stated perceptual budget and (ii)~enforces download feasibility under a conformal throughput lower bound with target coverage \(1{-}\alpha\).
Its silences are deliberate. It never claims dominance over every ABR controller, nor a pathwise worst-case guarantee beyond the conformal assumption of residual exchangeability.
The payoff also depends on the regime. Banking gains gather where high rungs are often feasible (moderate-buffer synthetic \mbox{5G}); under harsh broadband stress, safety projection takes over and banking headroom thins out.

\textbf{Local ladder inversions.}
Table~\ref{tab:ladder:spacing} shows that, chunk by chunk, a sizable share of adjacent rungs in titles like Sintel and Big Buck Bunny run near-flat or even \emph{inverted}---a higher bitrate scoring no better, sometimes worse, in VMAF.
CPS needs no special case for any of this. Two reasons explain why.
The first is structural. The evaluated shield runs on the monotone session-mean ladder, so Proposition~\ref{prop:collapse} applies outright; and even on a fully per-chunk, non-monotone ladder, both CPS stages stay well defined.
The second is that each stage shrugs off inversions on its own terms. The feasibility projection~\eqref{eq:asafe} watches download time, never VMAF, so inversions leave safety untouched.
The knee rule~\eqref{eq:knee} picks the smallest rung within \(\varepsilon\) of the proposal's VMAF; when an inversion lets a lower rung score at least as high, that rung clears the budget on its own, and banking pockets the extra headroom rather than tripping over it.
Local inversions, then, can only widen the banking opportunity, and the algorithm sails through them by construction. The per-chunk ablation in Section~\ref{sec:eval:perchunk} bears this out: banked bytes match, and realized per-chunk perceptual cost stays inside budget. Per-chunk ladders buy accuracy, not correctness.

\textbf{Threats to validity.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item The broadband suite replays \emph{real measured} FCC and Norway/Nornet traces~\cite{fcc2015mba,kvalbein2014}, yet the \mbox{5G} suite is synthetic, and either way the chunk-level emulator strips out transport-stack dynamics and CDN caching~\cite{narayanan2021variegated}. Live-player validation waits for future work.
  \item Twelve publicly available titles and finite trace pools still cap catalogue diversity~\cite{kvalbein2014}; three are held out from PPO training but recur in CPS evaluation.
  \item QoE here is a VMAF-based surrogate. Subjective rankings of stall against fidelity may part from it~\cite{seufert2015survey}.
  \item Conformal coverage presumes exchangeable residuals. Sharp nonstationarity can erode the guarantee, though the online window adapts locally and the coverage loss stays bounded by the residual distribution shift~\cite{barber2023beyond}.
  \item Every arm---greedy, BBA, the PPO hosts---runs locally under the same finite-sample \((n{+}1)\) conformal correction. Co-design magnitudes hinge on the checkpoint, so we report the best-validation model of each policy. One point deserves weight: the co-design contrast uses a \emph{single} training seed per policy, so the \(95\%\) intervals come from a paired-episode bootstrap over the \(\CPScoBootN\) test episodes and speak to evaluation variance, not training-seed variance. Nailing down training-seed variance for \(w^{\star}\) would mean retraining many seeds; that we leave for later. Hence co-design stands as a supporting result, not a headline claim.
  \item Banking savings \emph{scale with ladder saturation} (Table~\ref{tab:ladder:spacing}); the twelve-title mix deliberately includes steep and non-saturated ladders, so greedy/BBA effect sizes are conservative relative to a saturation-rich subset alone.
  \item The Pensieve-style no-op reveals that host aggressiveness sets the banking opportunity. Do not carry the results over to arbitrary policies without the paired-arm protocol.
  \item The TOST margin equals the configured banking budget \(\varepsilon\)---a design parameter, not a preregistered clinical equivalence margin~\cite{lakens2017equivalence}.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Future work.}
Packet-level or field \mbox{5G} evaluation; applying CPS to RobustMPC and Comyco hosts; CVaR / tail-aware objectives~\cite{rockafellar2000}; telemetry-driven adaptation of \(\varepsilon\) and \(\alpha\); and preregistered non-inferiority studies with a fixed perceptual margin.
Real-player deployment is the direction we care about most. Every result here comes from a chunk-level emulator that strips out transport-stack dynamics, so dropping CPS into a production player like \texttt{dash.js}~\cite{spiteri2018dashjs} raises concrete challenges the simulation never sees. Three loom largest. First, throughput has to be read off noisy, bursty HTTP/TCP (or QUIC) samples whose exchangeability---the very assumption behind the conformal bound---can break under slow-start, congestion-control transients, and CDN re-routing; the calibration residuals would then call for periodic re-estimation, or a sliding, weighted-conformal variant that provably bounds coverage loss under drift~\cite{barber2023beyond}. Second, the feasibility check has to square the player's own buffer-occupancy signal, segment-level and delayed, with the certificate's buffer-margin accounting. Third, the per-decision budget hardens from our \(\sim\!100\,\mu\mathrm{s}\) micro\-benchmark into a soft real-time constraint running beside the player's ABR loop. Showing that the finite-sample coverage guarantee weathers these real-world jolts---and measuring whatever coverage slack they open up---is the obvious next step.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We built the Certified Perceptual Shield (CPS) for client-side ABR from three parts: VMAF-knee bandwidth banking, an online conformal throughput lower bound with finite-sample coverage, and certified buffer feasibility.
The evidence lines up. On \(\CPSepisodes\) paired synthetic \mbox{5G} episodes over twelve ladder-diverse titles, banking against conformal safety-only significantly reduces bitrate and rebuffering on greedy and BBA (\(\CPSGreedyBWcs\%\)/\(\CPSGreedyRebCs\%\) and \(\CPSBbaBWcs\%\)/\(\CPSBbaRebCs\%\)), with VMAF inside \(\pm\CPSepsilon\) and empirical coverage at \(\CPSGreedyCov\) against a \(\CPScovTarget\) target.
Predictive banking deepens the margin (\(\CPSGreedyImpBWcs\%\) greedy bitrate); broadband stress exposes the flip side, where safety carries the load once the ladder knee slips out of reach.
Where learning is on the table, co-training PPO with CPS tilts the certified point toward fidelity---a dependable \(+\CPScoDVM\) VMAF gain (bootstrap 95\% CI \([\CPScoDVMlo,\CPScoDVMhi]\))---at a QoE crossover of \(w^{\star}{=}\CPScoWstar\) VMAF points per second of stall (\(p{=}\CPScoPVM\) for VMAF).
CPS drops onto existing ABR controllers as a light wrapper---no retraining required---and it folds in cleanly with learned policies when co-design is worth the trouble.
